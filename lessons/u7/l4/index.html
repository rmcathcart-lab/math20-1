The Hotel Elevator

Priya’s hotel has floors 1 to 12. She gets on the elevator, rides 6 floors and gets off to fill her ice bucket. Then she gets back on and rides 4 floors to her room on floor 7.

On which floor did she get on the elevator?

?
Make a guess first. Then test it.
Floor 5 or floor 9! From floor 5: up 6 to the ice machine on 11, then down 4 to 7. From floor 9: down 6 to 3, then up 4 to 7.
Working backwards from 7, the ice machine is on 11 or 3, and the start is 6 floors from there: 17, 5, 9 or −3. But floors 17 and −3 aren’t in the building, so they don’t count.

1

What an Absolute Value Equation Means

An absolute value equation has the variable inside absolute value bars, for example |x-3|=5 or |2x+3|=x+6. Since |x-3| is the distance between x and 3, the equation |x-3|=5 asks for the numbers 5 units from 3: x=-2 or x=8.

An absolute value is never negative, so the value on the other side decides how many solutions there are.

For |A|=c, where c is a constant
c > 0two solutions, from A=c or A=-c
c = 0one solution, from A=0
c < 0no solution
Example 1

Two, one or none

Solve.

(a) |x|=7
x=7 or x=-7
(b) |x-3|=5
x-3=5 or x-3=-5
x=8 or x=-2
(c) |x+4|=0
x+4=0
x=-4 (one solution)
(d) |x-1|=-2
No solution: an absolute value cannot equal -2.
2

Solving Graphically

A solution of an equation is an x-value that makes the two sides equal. On a graph these are the x-coordinates where the graphs of the two sides meet. A graphing calculator gives two ways to find them.

Intersection methodGraph y= left side and y= right side; the solutions are the x-coordinates of the points of intersection (use intersect).
Zero methodMove every term to one side, so the equation reads (\dots)=0; graph y=(\dots); the solutions are its zeros, the x-intercepts (use zero).
On the TI-84 Plus CE, type |2x-4| as abs(2X-4) (MATH NUM); intersect and zero are in the CALC menu (2ndTRACE).
Example 2

Read the solutions from the graphs

Use the graphs to solve each equation. Then check each solution in the equation.

(a) |2x-4|=6
The graphs meet at (-1,\ 6) and (5,\ 6): x=-1 or x=5.
Check: |2(-1)-4|=\lvert -6\rvert =6 ✓   |2(5)-4|=|6|=6 ✓
(b) |x+1|=2x-1
The graphs meet only at (2,\ 3): x=2.
Check: |2+1|=3 and 2(2)-1=3 ✓
The left arm of the V never meets the line, so there is only one solution.
Example 3

The zero method

(a) Describe how to solve |3x-6|=x+2 with the zero method.
Rearrange: |3x-6|-x-2=0.
Graph y=|3x-6|-x-2 (abs(3X-6)-X-2).
Use zero on each x-intercept.
(b) The graph from (a) is shown. State the solution and check it.
The zeros are x=1 and x=4.
Check: |3-6|=3 and 1+2=3 ✓
|12-6|=6 and 4+2=6 ✓
On your own calculator. Intersection method for Example 2(a): Y_1= abs(2X-4), Y_2=6, then 5:intersect, pressing ENTER twice and typing a guess near each crossing (-1, then 5). Zero method for Example 3: Y_1= abs(3X-6)-X-2, then 2:zero with a left bound, a right bound and a guess on either side of each x-intercept.
3

Solving |A|=c Algebraically

When the other side is a constant, first isolate the absolute value (undo the other operations on the outside of the bars). Then use the key idea from Section 1: A=c or A=-c.

Example 4

Isolate the absolute value first

Solve.

(a) |2x-5|=9
2x-5=9 or 2x-5=-9
2x=14 or 2x=-4
x=7 or x=-2
(b) 3|x+2|-4=11
3|x+2|=15\Rightarrow|x+2|=5
x+2=5 or x+2=-5
x=3 or x=-7
(c) |4-3x|+8=2
|4-3x|=-6
No solution: an absolute value is never negative.
(d) -2|x-6|+1=1
-2|x-6|=0\Rightarrow|x-6|=0
x-6=0, x=6 (one solution)
(e) \tfrac{1}{2}|x+3|=4
|x+3|=8
x+3=8 or x+3=-8
x=5 or x=-11
(f) A student solved |x+2|+4=1 this way:  x+2+4=1 or x+2+4=-1,  so x=-5 or x=-7. Explain the error and give the correct solution.
The absolute value was not isolated first. |x+2|=-3 has no solution.
Check: \lvert -5+2\rvert +4=7\ne1 and \lvert -7+2\rvert +4=9\ne1. Both “solutions” fail.

Solving |A|=B by Cases

When the other side contains the variable, the number of solutions is not known in advance. Use the piecewise form of the absolute value (Lesson 2): on each side of the value that makes the inside zero, the bars can be replaced by an ordinary expression.

Solving by cases
1Find the value of x that makes the expression inside the bars equal to 0.
2Split the number line there into two subdomains.
3In each subdomain, replace |A| by A (where A\ge0) or by -A (where A<0).
4Solve the equation in each subdomain.
5Keep a solution only if it is in its own subdomain; combine the solutions that are kept.
Example 5

Two cases, one solution

Solve |x-4|=3x by cases.

Case 1: x\ge4, so |x-4|=x-4.
x-4=3x
-4=2x,  x=-2
Is -2\ge4? No: reject.
Case 2: x<4, so |x-4|=-(x-4).
-x+4=3x
4=4x,  x=1
Is 1<4? Yes: keep.
Solution: x=1 Check: |1-4|=3 and 3(1)=3 ✓
Example 6

Cases, then the graph

(a) Solve |2x+3|=x+6 by cases.
2x+3=0 at x=-\tfrac{3}{2}.
Case 1: x\ge-\tfrac{3}{2}:  2x+3=x+6,  x=3. In the subdomain: keep.
Case 2: x<-\tfrac{3}{2}:  -(2x+3)=x+6
-2x-3=x+6,  -3x=9,  x=-3. In the subdomain: keep.
Solution: x=-3 or x=3.
(b) Sketch y=|2x+3| and y=x+6 on the grid to confirm the solution.
They meet at (-3,\ 3) and (3,\ 9).
5

Solving Both Equations, Then Verifying

A shorter method ignores the subdomains: write A=B and A=-B, solve both, then check every answer in the original equation. An answer that fails the check is an extraneous solution: it solves one of the two linear equations but not the absolute value equation, usually because it makes the right side negative. Reject it.

To solve |A|=B
1Isolate the absolute value.
2Solve A=B and A=-B.
3Substitute each answer into the original equation; reject any that fail (the right side B must be \ge0).
There can be two solutions, one solution or no solution.
Example 7

Solve and verify

Solve and verify.

(a) |3x+1|=x-5
3x+1=x-5 \Rightarrow x=-3
3x+1=-(x-5) \Rightarrow 4x=4 \Rightarrow x=1
Check x=-3: \lvert -8\rvert =8, but -3-5=-8 ✗
Check x=1: |4|=4, but 1-5=-4 ✗
Both are extraneous: no solution. (The line y=x-5 stays below the V.)
(b) 2|x-3|=x+3
2(x-3)=x+3 \Rightarrow x=9
-2(x-3)=x+3 \Rightarrow -2x+6=x+3 \Rightarrow x=1
Check x=9: 2|6|=12, 9+3=12 ✓
Check x=1: 2\lvert -2\rvert =4, 1+3=4 ✓
x=1 or x=9
(c) |x+5|-2x=1
Isolate: |x+5|=2x+1
x+5=2x+1 \Rightarrow x=4
-(x+5)=2x+1 \Rightarrow -3x=6 \Rightarrow x=-2
Check x=4: |9|=9, 2(4)+1=9 ✓
Check x=-2: |3|=3, but 2(-2)+1=-3 ✗
x=4 (x=-2 is extraneous)
6

Problems

Absolute value equations describe numbers at a given distance from a target value: |x-a|=d means “x is d units from a”, so x=a+d or x=a-d. Working backwards, two numbers m and n are the solutions of |x-a|=d where a=\dfrac{m+n}{2} is the midpoint and d=\dfrac{|m-n|}{2} is half the distance between them.

Example 8

Tolerance and distance

(a) A cereal company fills boxes with a target mass of 750 g. A box is rejected if its mass differs from the target by more than 12 g. Write and solve an absolute value equation for the least and greatest masses that are accepted.
Let m be the mass in grams. |m-750|=12
m-750=12 or m-750=-12: m=762 or m=738. Accepted boxes are from 738 g to 762 g.
(b) Write an absolute value equation, in the form |x-a|=d, whose solutions are -3 and 11.
Midpoint a=\tfrac{-3+11}{2}=4; half the distance d=\tfrac{11-(-3)}{2}=7.
|x-4|=7
Check: \lvert -3-4\rvert =7 ✓  |11-4|=7 ✓
(c) The distance between a number and 2 is twice the number. Write an equation and determine the number.
|x-2|=2x
x-2=2x \Rightarrow x=-2:  \lvert -4\rvert =4, but 2(-2)=-4 ✗
-(x-2)=2x \Rightarrow 2=3x \Rightarrow x=\tfrac{2}{3}:  \left|-\tfrac{4}{3}\right|=\tfrac{4}{3}=2\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right) ✓
The number is \tfrac{2}{3}.
✓

Key ideas

How many solutions? For |A|=c: c>0 two (A=\pm c), c=0 one, c<0 none. An absolute value is never negative.
Graphically. Intersection method: graph both sides, use intersect. Zero method: move everything to one side, graph it, use zero. Type |\ | as abs(.
Isolate first. Undo what is outside the bars, then split: 3|x+2|-4=11\Rightarrow|x+2|=5\Rightarrow x+2=\pm5.
Variable on the right. By cases (keep a root only in its own subdomain), or solve A=B and A=-B and check every root: one that makes the right side negative is extraneous.
