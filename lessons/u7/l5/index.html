The Mountain Monk

At sunrise, a monk starts up the only path to a mountain-top temple. He walks at any speed, rests when he likes, and arrives at sunset. The next day he walks back down the same path, from sunrise to sunset.

Is there a spot on the path that he passes at exactly the same time of day on both days?

The Absolute Value of a Quadratic Equal to a Constant

To solve |A|=k where k>0, use the two cases from Lesson 4: the expression inside is either k or -k. When A is a quadratic expression, each case is a quadratic equation, so there can be up to four solutions.

To solve |f(x)|=k
  1. isolate the absolute value;
  2. if k<0 there is no solution, and if k=0 solve f(x)=0;
  3. if k>0 solve both f(x)=k and f(x)=-k (each in standard form: factor, or use the quadratic formula);
  4. a case with no real roots gives nothing; collect every real root.

Because the right side k is a positive constant, every root of the two cases is a solution (no root is extraneous). Checking one or two of them in the original equation is still a good habit.

Using the Quadratic Formula; How Many Solutions?

When a case does not factor, use the quadratic formula x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}. Give exact answers in simplest radical form, then round if asked.

The graph tells how many solutions to expect. If the parabola y=f(x) opens up and its vertex (p,\ q) is below the x-axis, the graph of y=|f(x)| is a W shape whose middle hump has its top at (p,\ |q|).

Example 3

Four solutions, then three

Use the graph of y=|x^2-2x-3| from Example 1.

(a) Solve |x^2-2x-3|=2. Give exact answers and answers to the nearest hundredth.
Case 1: x^2-2x-3=2\Rightarrow x^2-2x-5=0
x=\frac{2\pm\sqrt{4+20}}{2}=\frac{2\pm2\sqrt6}{2}=1\pm\sqrt6
Case 2: x^2-2x-3=-2\Rightarrow x^2-2x-1=0
x=\frac{2\pm\sqrt{4+4}}{2}=\frac{2\pm2\sqrt2}{2}=1\pm\sqrt2
x=1\pm\sqrt6 or x=1\pm\sqrt2
x\approx-1.45,\ -0.41,\ 2.41,\ 3.45 (four, since 0<2<4)
(b) Solve |x^2-2x-3|=4 exactly. Why are there only three solutions?
Case 1: x^2-2x-7=0\Rightarrow x=\frac{2\pm\sqrt{32}}{2}=1\pm2\sqrt2
Case 2: x^2-2x+1=0\Rightarrow(x-1)^2=0\Rightarrow x=1 (double root)
Solutions: x=1-2\sqrt2,\ 1,\ 1+2\sqrt2.

How many solutions?

Consider |x^2-6x+5|=k.

(a) In vertex form, x^2-6x+5=(x-3)^2-4. Without solving, state the number of solutions when k=-2, k=0, k=2, k=4 and k=7.
The parabola has zeros 1 and 5 and vertex (3,\ -4), so the reflected hump has its top at (3,\ 4).
k=-2: 0   k=0: 2 (x=1,\ 5)   k=2: 4   k=4: 3   k=7: 2

A Variable on the Other Side: Extraneous Solutions

In |A|=B where B contains the variable (for example |x^2-4|=3x), the right side could be negative for some x, and an absolute value is never negative. Solving the two cases can then produce extraneous solutions: values that solve a case but not the original equation.

To solve |A|=B when B contains x
Solve A=B and A=-B, then check every root in the original equation (equivalently, keep only the roots that make B\ge0). Reject the others as extraneous.

The method from Lesson 4 also works: split the number line where the expression inside the absolute value is 0, write |A| as A or -A on each part, solve, and keep only the roots that lie in their part. Either way, the check is required.

Example 5

Solve and check

Solve and check.

(a) |x^2-4|=3x
Case 1: x^2-4=3x
x^2-3x-4=0\Rightarrow(x-4)(x+1)=0\Rightarrow x=4,\ -1
Case 2: x^2-4=-3x
x^2+3x-4=0\Rightarrow(x+4)(x-1)=0\Rightarrow x=-4,\ 1
Check (the right side 3x must be \ge0):
x=4: |12|=12\ \checkmark   x=1: \lvert -3\rvert =3\ \checkmark
x=-1: \lvert -3\rvert =3\ne-3 (extraneous)
x=-4: |12|=12\ne-12 (extraneous)
Solutions: x=1 or x=4.
(b) |2x+1|=x^2-2
Case 1: 2x+1=x^2-2
x^2-2x-3=0\Rightarrow(x-3)(x+1)=0\Rightarrow x=3,\ -1
Case 2: -(2x+1)=x^2-2
x^2+2x-1=0\Rightarrow x=\frac{-2\pm\sqrt8}{2}=-1\pm\sqrt2
Check (the right side x^2-2 must be \ge0):
x=3: |7|=7=9-2\ \checkmark
x=-1: x^2-2=-1<0 (extraneous)
x=-1+\sqrt2\approx0.41: x^2-2\approx-1.83<0 (extraneous)
x=-1-\sqrt2\approx-2.41: x^2-2=1+2\sqrt2>0, and |2x+1|=\lvert -1-2\sqrt2\rvert =1+2\sqrt2\ \checkmark
Solutions: x=3 or x=-1-\sqrt2.

Equations with Two Absolute Values

Two numbers have the same absolute value when they are equal or opposites.

|A|=|B|\iff A=B\ \text{ or }\ A=-B.  Both sides are never negative, so no root is extraneous.

For a sum or difference such as |A|+|B|=k, use subdomains: mark where each expression inside is 0 on a number line, write each absolute value without its bars on each interval (A if A\ge0, -A if A<0), solve on each interval, and keep only roots that lie in that interval.

Example 6

Equal or opposite

Solve.

(a) |x+5|=|2x-1|
x+5=2x-1\Rightarrow x=6
x+5=-(2x-1)\Rightarrow x+5=-2x+1
3x=-4\Rightarrow x=-\frac43
Solutions: x=6 or x=-\frac43.
Check: |11|=|11|\ \checkmark; \left|\frac{11}{3}\right|=\left|-\frac{11}{3}\right|\ \checkmark
(b) |x^2-3|=|2x|
x^2-3=2x\Rightarrow x^2-2x-3=0\Rightarrow(x-3)(x+1)=0
x^2-3=-2x\Rightarrow x^2+2x-3=0\Rightarrow(x+3)(x-1)=0
Solutions: x=-3,\ -1,\ 1,\ 3.
Check x=1: \lvert -2\rvert =|2|\ \checkmark

Solve |x-3|+|x+1|=6 using subdomains.

Solving Graphically and Solving Problems

A graphing calculator gives a quick check, or decimal answers when the algebra is messy.

Intersection method: graph Y_1= the left side (abs( is in the MATH menu under NUM) and Y_2= the right side; use intersect at each point where the graphs meet.
Zero method: graph Y_1= (left side) - (right side) and use zero at each x-intercept.

Use a window that shows every intersection (the W shape and the whole line). In a word problem, an equation such as |h-10|=3 means “h is exactly 3 away from 10”, so h=13 or h=7.

Example 8 on the calculator. Intersection: Y1 = abs(−X² + 8X − 10), Y2 = 3, window 0\le x\le8, 0\le y\le12, then 5:intersect: ENTER twice and a guess near each crossing. Zero: Y1 = abs(−X² + 8X − 10) − 3, then 2:zero with a left bound, a right bound and a guess.
Example 8

The drone and the camera

A drone rises and then lands. Its height is h=-t^2+8t metres after t seconds, 0\le t\le8. A camera on the ground records it only while it is within 3 m of a height of 10 m.

(a) Explain why the times when the drone is exactly 3 m from a height of 10 m are the solutions of \lvert -t^2+8t-10\rvert =3. Solve the equation exactly, then to the nearest hundredth of a second.
The distance between h and 10 is |h-10|=\lvert -t^2+8t-10\rvert .
Case 1: -t^2+8t-10=3\Rightarrow t^2-8t+13=0
t=\frac{8\pm\sqrt{64-52}}{2}=\frac{8\pm2\sqrt3}{2}=4\pm\sqrt3
Case 2: -t^2+8t-10=-3\Rightarrow t^2-8t+7=0
(t-1)(t-7)=0\Rightarrow t=1,\ 7
t=1,\ 4-\sqrt3,\ 4+\sqrt3,\ 7
t\approx1.00,\ 2.27,\ 5.73,\ 7.00 s (all in 0\le t\le8)
(b) Check with a graphing calculator, then state the time intervals during which the camera records the drone.
Y_1= abs(−X² + 8X − 10), Y_2=3, window 0\le x\le8, 0\le y\le12: intersect gives 1, 2.27, 5.73, 7.
The drone reaches 7 m at 1 s, 13 m at 2.27 s (its maximum is 16 m at 4 s), falls back to 13 m at 5.73 s and to 7 m at 7 s.
The camera records from 1 s to about 2.27 s, and from about 5.73 s to 7 s (where 7\le h\le13).

Key ideas

Two cases. Isolate |f(x)|=k. If k<0, no solution; if k>0, solve f(x)=k and f(x)=-k by factoring or the quadratic formula. Up to four solutions.
Count with the W. Compare k with the top of the reflected hump, |q|: below it 4, touching it 3, above it 2; k=0 gives the 2 zeros, k<0 gives 0.
Variable on the right. For |A|=B, solve A=B and A=-B, then check every root: one that makes B<0 is extraneous.
Two absolute values. |A|=|B|: A=B or A=-B. |A|+|B|=k: subdomains, keeping roots in their own interval. On the calculator: intersect or zero.
