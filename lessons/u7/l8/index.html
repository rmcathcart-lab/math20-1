The Night Bridge

Four friends must cross a rickety rope bridge at night. They have one flashlight, and nobody can be on the bridge without it. The bridge holds two people at a time, and two people walk together at the slower one’s pace.

Alone, they cross in 1, 2, 5 and 10 minutes.

What is the shortest time to get all four across?

?
Write down your best time. Then try to beat it.
17 minutes. Most people let the fastest friend walk everyone across: 10+1+5+1+2=19 minutes.
Better: 1 and 2 cross (2), 1 brings the light back (1), 5 and 10 cross together (10), 2 brings it back (2), 1 and 2 cross (2). 2+1+10+2+2=17.
Twist: the two slowest walk together, so the 5-minute walk hides inside the 10-minute one.

TopicKey factsExample
Absolute value of a number|a| is the distance from a to 0, so |a|\ge0. |a|=a if a\ge0 and |a|=-a if a<0. The bars are grouping symbols: simplify inside first. The distance between a and b is |a-b|. |3-8|-|-2|= 5-2=3
Distance from -4 to 7: |-4-7|=11
Graph of y=|f(x)|Keep the part of y=f(x) on or above the x-axis; reflect the part below it in the x-axis. The x-intercepts are invariant points. Same domain; the range has no negative values. A line gives a V with its vertex at the zero; for a parabola, the part between or outside the zeros is reflected. y=|x^2-4|: the part for -2<x<2 is reflected; the vertex (0,\ -4) becomes a local maximum (0,\ 4); range \{y\mid y\ge0,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}
Piecewise form|A|=A where A\ge0 and |A|=-A where A<0. Solve A\ge0 to find where each piece applies. |2x-6|= \begin{cases}2x-6, & \text{if } x\ge3\\ -2x+6, & \text{if } x<3\end{cases}
Absolute value equationsIsolate the absolute value. |A|= a negative number has no solution. Otherwise solve A=B and A=-B (or use the cases), then verify every answer in the original equation and reject extraneous ones. Graphically: the x-coordinates of the intersection points. |x-1|=2x+4:
x-1=2x+4 gives x=-5 (|-6|=6\ne-6: reject)
x-1=-(2x+4) gives x=-1 (2=2 ✓)
Reciprocal functions y=\dfrac{1}{f(x)}
Every reciprocal. A point (x,\ y) becomes \left(x,\ \frac{1}{y}\right). Zeros of f \to vertical asymptotes; the horizontal asymptote is y=0. Invariant points where f(x)=\pm1. Same sign as f. Where f increases, the reciprocal decreases (and the other way). y-intercept c\to\frac{1}{c}. Domain: all real numbers except the zeros of f; y=0 is never in the range.
Linear f. One vertical asymptote, two branches in opposite regions; range \{y\mid y\ne0,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}.
Quadratic f with vertex (p,\ q). Two, one or no vertical asymptotes (two, one or no zeros). If q\ne0, the turning point is \left(p,\ \frac{1}{q}\right): a maximum point if a>0, a minimum point if a<0. Range: reciprocals of the range of f, e.g. f(x)\ge-4 gives y\le-\frac{1}{4} or y>0.

Common errors tap a card to see the fix

|a+b|=|a|+|b|?
Not in general: |3+(-5)|=2 but |3|+|-5|=8.
-|5|=5?
-|5|=-5: the minus is outside the bars.
Splitting into cases before isolating
Isolate the absolute value first, then split.
Not checking the answers
When the other side contains x, verify each one: some are extraneous.
Reflecting the whole parabola
Reflect only the part below the x-axis.
The reciprocal of a minimum is a minimum?
It turns into a maximum (and a maximum into a minimum).
A reciprocal graph crossing the x-axis or an asymptote
It never does: \frac{1}{f(x)}\ne0, and it is undefined at the zeros of f.

Absolute Value and Its Graphs

Distance from zero. Simplify inside the bars first; an absolute value is never negative. For a graph, everything below the x-axis is reflected up, and the zeros stay put.
Example 1

Numbers and piecewise form

(a) Evaluate |-8|-|3-10|+|\sqrt[3]{-27}|.
=8-|-7|+|-3|=8-7+3=\boldsymbol{4}
(b) Order from least to greatest: |-2.5|, -|-4|, |\sqrt{2}-3|, -|1-2|
Values: 2.5, -4, 3-\sqrt{2}\approx1.59, -1
\boldsymbol{-|-4|<-|1-2|<|\sqrt{2}-3|<|-2.5|}
(c) Find the distance between -6.5 and 2.3 on a number line.
|-6.5-2.3|=|-8.8|=\boldsymbol{8.8}
(d) Write y=|5-2x| in piecewise form.
5-2x\ge0\Rightarrow x\le\frac{5}{2}
y=\begin{cases}5-2x, & \text{if } x\le\frac{5}{2}\\ 2x-5, & \text{if } x>\frac{5}{2}\end{cases}
Example 2

Sketch y=|f(x)|

The graph of y=f(x) is shown. Sketch y=|f(x)| on the same grid. State the invariant points and the range of y=|f(x)|, and write y=|f(x)| in piecewise form.

(a) f(x)=4-2x
Invariant point (2,\ 0); the part for x>2 is reflected.
Range \{y\mid y\ge0,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}
y=\begin{cases}4-2x, & \text{if } x\le2\\ 2x-4, & \text{if } x>2\end{cases}
(b) f(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^2-2
Invariant points (-2,\ 0), (2,\ 0); the vertex (0,\ -2) becomes a local maximum (0,\ 2).
Range \{y\mid y\ge0,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}
y=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}x^2-2, & \text{if } x\le-2 \text{ or } x\ge2\\ -\frac{1}{2}x^2+2, & \text{if } -2<x<2\end{cases}

Absolute Value Equations

Isolate, split, verify. 1 Isolate |A|. If it equals a negative number: no solution. 2 Solve A=B and A=-B. 3 Verify each answer in the original equation; reject the extraneous ones.
Example 3

Linear absolute value equations

Solve algebraically and verify.

(a) 3|x+2|-4=11
3|x+2|=15\Rightarrow|x+2|=5
x+2=5 or x+2=-5
\boldsymbol{x=3} or \boldsymbol{x=-7}. Check: 3(5)-4=11 ✓, 3|-5|-4=11 ✓
(b) |3x-1|+6=2
|3x-1|=-4
An absolute value is never negative: no solution.
(c) |x+4|=3x-2
x+4=3x-2\Rightarrow x=3; check: |7|=7, 3(3)-2=7 ✓
x+4=-(3x-2)\Rightarrow4x=-2\Rightarrow x=-\frac{1}{2}; check: \left|\frac{7}{2}\right|=\frac{7}{2} but 3\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)-2=-\frac{7}{2} (extraneous)
Solution: \boldsymbol{x=3}
(d) A greenhouse is kept within 3.5^\circ\text{C} of 24^\circ\text{C}. Write and solve an absolute value equation for the lowest and highest allowed temperatures.
|T-24|=3.5
T-24=3.5 or T-24=-3.5
T=\boldsymbol{27.5^\circ\text{C}} (highest) or T=\boldsymbol{20.5^\circ\text{C}} (lowest)
Example 4

Quadratic absolute value equations

Solve algebraically. Verify where needed.

(a) |x^2-3x-4|=6
x^2-3x-4=6\Rightarrow x^2-3x-10=0\Rightarrow(x-5)(x+2)=0
x^2-3x-4=-6\Rightarrow x^2-3x+2=0\Rightarrow(x-1)(x-2)=0
\boldsymbol{x=-2,\ 1,\ 2,\ 5} (the right side is a positive constant, so none is extraneous)
(b) |x^2-5|=4x
x^2-5=4x\Rightarrow x^2-4x-5=0\Rightarrow(x-5)(x+1)=0
x^2-5=-4x\Rightarrow x^2+4x-5=0\Rightarrow(x+5)(x-1)=0
Check: x=5: 20=20 ✓; x=1: |-4|=4 ✓;
x=-1 and x=-5 make 4x<0 (extraneous).
Solutions: \boldsymbol{x=1} or \boldsymbol{x=5}
Check on the calculator. Graph Y_1= abs(X²-3X-4) and Y_2=6, then 2ndCALC 5:intersect: press ENTER twice and type a guess near each crossing. The four crossings are at x=-2,\ 1,\ 2,\ 5.

Keep it or extraneous? each side of the equation is a graph

Each case solves the equation with a ghost of the left side: y=A or y=-A. A crossing with a ghost is only a solution if it sits on the real graph y=|A|, which is never below the x-axis.

Reciprocal Functions

Start from f. Its zeros give the vertical asymptotes, the lines y=\pm1 give the invariant points, and its vertex gives the turning point. Then each branch has the same sign as f and heads toward the asymptotes.
Example 5

The reciprocal of a linear function

Let f(x)=2x-4.

(a) Write the equation of the reciprocal function and state its asymptotes, its invariant points and its y-intercept.
y=\dfrac{1}{2x-4}. Asymptotes x=2 and y=0.
2x-4=1: x=\frac{5}{2}; 2x-4=-1: x=\frac{3}{2}. Invariant points \left(\frac{5}{2},\ 1\right), \left(\frac{3}{2},\ -1\right).
y-intercept -\frac{1}{4}.
(b) Sketch y=f(x) and its reciprocal on the grid, and state the domain and range of the reciprocal.
Domain \{x\mid x\ne2,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}; range \{y\mid y\ne0,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}
Example 6

One quadratic, both transformations

Let f(x)=-x^2+6x-5. Its graph is shown on both grids.

(a) Sketch y=|f(x)|. State its invariant points and its range.
f(x)=-(x-1)(x-5)=-(x-3)^2+4. The parts for x<1 and x>5 are reflected.
Invariant points (1,\ 0), (5,\ 0); range \{y\mid y\ge0,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}.
(b) Sketch y=\dfrac{1}{f(x)}. State the asymptotes, the turning point, the exact invariant points, the domain and the range.
Asymptotes x=1, x=5, y=0. Vertex (3,\ 4), a<0: minimum point \left(3,\ \frac{1}{4}\right).
f(x)=1: x^2-6x+6=0, x=3\pm\sqrt{3}; f(x)=-1: x^2-6x+4=0, x=3\pm\sqrt{5}.
Invariant points (3\pm\sqrt{3},\ 1), (3\pm\sqrt{5},\ -1). Domain \{x\mid x\ne1,\ x\ne5,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
Range \left\{y\mid y<0 \text{ or } y\ge\frac{1}{4},\ y\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

Building the reciprocal, one feature at a time

The same five steps for a line and for a parabola. Each feature of y=\dfrac{1}{f(x)} comes from a feature of y=f(x).

Example 7

Points, intervals and working backwards

(a) The point (-4,\ -3) is on y=f(x). State the matching points on y=|f(x)| and on y=\dfrac{1}{f(x)}.
\boldsymbol{(-4,\ 3)} and \boldsymbol{\left(-4,\ -\frac{1}{3}\right)}
(b) Which of the points (2,\ 1), (5,\ -1), (0,\ 0), (3,\ 4) on y=f(x) must also be on both y=|f(x)| and y=\dfrac{1}{f(x)}? Explain.
Only \boldsymbol{(2,\ 1)}: |1|=1 and \frac{1}{1}=1. ((5,\ -1) is invariant for the reciprocal but becomes (5,\ 1) on y=|f(x)|; \frac{1}{0} is undefined; \frac{1}{4}\ne4.)
(c) A function f is positive and decreasing on the interval 0<x<5. Describe y=|f(x)| and y=\dfrac{1}{f(x)} on that interval.
|f(x)|=f(x) there (no reflection), so it is also positive and decreasing. The reciprocal is positive and increasing.
(d) The graph of y=\dfrac{1}{f(x)}, where f is quadratic, has a minimum point (2,\ -0.2) and no vertical asymptotes. It passes through \left(0,\ -\frac{1}{9}\right). Determine f(x).
Vertex of f: (2,\ -5), a maximum (a<0). f(0)=-9.
f(x)=a(x-2)^2-5: -9=4a-5, a=-1. \boldsymbol{f(x)=-(x-2)^2-5=-x^2+4x-9}

Key ideas

Absolute value. A distance, so never negative. Simplify inside the bars first; the distance between a and b is |a-b|.
y=|f(x)|. Keep what is on or above the x-axis, reflect what is below. The zeros are invariant; the range has no negatives. Piecewise: A where A\ge0, -A where A<0.
Equations. Isolate |A|, solve A=B and A=-B, then verify every answer. When B contains x, some answers can be extraneous.
y=\dfrac{1}{f(x)}. Zeros become vertical asymptotes, y=0 is the horizontal asymptote, f=\pm1 gives invariant points, a vertex (p,\ q) gives \left(p,\ \frac{1}{q}\right) (max and min swap), same sign as f.
