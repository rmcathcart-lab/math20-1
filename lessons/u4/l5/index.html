Solving Quadratic Equations by Factoring

If a product is zero, one of its factors must be zero. Get a 0 on one side, factor the other side, and each factor hands you a root. No calculator today: every answer is exact.

Mystery Cards

Two cards lie face down. All you know is what their numbers multiply to.

7\times\boxed{\phantom{0}}=0. What number is on the card?
0. Seven times any other number is not 0.
2
\left(\boxed{\phantom{0}}-2\right)\times5=0. What number is on the card?
2. The 5 can’t be 0, so the bracket must be: 2-2=0.

The big one. Pair A multiplies to 0. Pair B multiplies to 12. For which pair can you say for sure what one of the cards is? Guess first.
A Pair A only
B Pair B only
C Both pairs
D Neither pair
A. The only way to multiply to 0 is for a card to be 0. Twelve has endless pairs: 3\times4, 2\times6, -1\times(-12), \tfrac12\times24, … Deal pair B a few times.
That’s today’s big idea: get 0 on one side, and then each factor takes its turn being 0.

1

The Zero Product Property

If a product of numbers is zero, at least one of the numbers must be zero. This is the zero product property.

Zero product property:   if ab=0, then a=0 or b=0.

So when one side of an equation is a product of factors and the other side is 0, set each factor equal to 0 and solve. The values that make the equation true are its roots (or solutions). A constant factor such as 5 or -2 can never be 0, so it gives no root.

Example 1

Each factor takes a turn at 0

Solve each equation.

(a) (x+6)(x-4)=0
x+6=0 or x-4=0
\boldsymbol{x=-6} or \boldsymbol{x=4}
(b) 4x(x-9)=0
4x=0 or x-9=0
\boldsymbol{x=0} or \boldsymbol{x=9}
(c) (2x-7)(3x+4)=0
2x-7=0 or 3x+4=0
\boldsymbol{x=\tfrac{7}{2}} or \boldsymbol{x=-\tfrac{4}{3}}
(d) -5(x+1)(x-8)=0
-5\ne0, so x+1=0 or x-8=0
\boldsymbol{x=-1} or \boldsymbol{x=8}
2

Solving a Quadratic Equation in Standard Form

A quadratic equation can be written in standard form ax^2+bx+c=0,\ a\ne0. The zero product property only works when one side is 0, so:

1Move every term to one side so the other side is 0.
2Divide out (or factor out) any common constant factor; clear fractions by multiplying by the LCD.
3Factor fully.
4Set each factor with a variable equal to 0 and solve.
5Check in the original equation.
!
Never divide both sides by x (or by any factor with a variable): that factor could be 0, and you lose a root. Factor it out instead.
Example 2

Standard form, then factor

Solve.

(a) x^2+3x-28=0
(x+7)(x-4)=0
\boldsymbol{x=-7} or \boldsymbol{x=4}
(b) 16x^2-25=0
Difference of squares: (4x-5)(4x+5)=0
\boldsymbol{x=\tfrac{5}{4}} or \boldsymbol{x=-\tfrac{5}{4}}   \left(x=\pm\tfrac{5}{4}\right)
(c) 6x^2=21x
6x^2-21x=0
3x(2x-7)=0
\boldsymbol{x=0} or \boldsymbol{x=\tfrac{7}{2}}   (dividing by x would lose x=0)
(d) 3x^2-48=0
3(x^2-16)=0
3(x-4)(x+4)=0
\boldsymbol{x=\pm4}

Why you never divide by x

The solutions of an equation are the x-values where the left side and the right side are equal: where their graphs cross. Dividing by x quietly throws one of those crossings away.

Example 3

Common factors, fractions and a double root

Solve. A root that comes from a squared factor is a double root (the equation has only one distinct root).

(a) 4x^2-4x-15=0
Product ac=-60, sum -4: -10 and 6.
4x^2-10x+6x-15=0
2x(2x-5)+3(2x-5)=0
(2x-5)(2x+3)=0
\boldsymbol{x=\tfrac{5}{2}} or \boldsymbol{x=-\tfrac{3}{2}}
(b) -2x^2+10x=12
-2x^2+10x-12=0
Divide by -2: x^2-5x+6=0
(x-2)(x-3)=0
\boldsymbol{x=2} or \boldsymbol{x=3}
(c) 9x^2+4=12x
9x^2-12x+4=0
(3x-2)^2=0   (perfect square)
\boldsymbol{x=\tfrac{2}{3}} (a double root)
(d) \tfrac{1}{4}x^2-x-3=0
Multiply by 4: x^2-4x-12=0
(x-6)(x+2)=0
\boldsymbol{x=6} or \boldsymbol{x=-2}

When the right side isn’t 0 yet

Brackets on one side and a number on the other is not ready for the zero product property. Expand, simplify and move everything to one side first.

Example 4

Expand and simplify first

Expand and simplify first, then solve.

(a) (x+2)(x-5)=8
x^2-3x-10=8
x^2-3x-18=0
(x-6)(x+3)=0
\boldsymbol{x=6} or \boldsymbol{x=-3}
(Setting x+2=8 and x-5=8 is wrong: the right side must be 0.)
(b) 2x(x+3)=(x+1)^2+11
2x^2+6x=x^2+2x+1+11
x^2+4x-12=0
(x+6)(x-2)=0
\boldsymbol{x=-6} or \boldsymbol{x=2}
(c) (x-4)(x+1)=2(x-4)
x^2-3x-4-2x+8=0
x^2-5x+4=0
(x-1)(x-4)=0
\boldsymbol{x=1} or \boldsymbol{x=4}
(Dividing by x-4 would lose x=4.)

The roots of the equation, the zeros of the function and the x-intercepts of its graph are the same numbers. A double root means the parabola just touches the x-axis. (Unit 5 develops these graphs.)

Example 5

Read it, then solve it

The graph of y=x^2-2x-8 is shown.

(a) Read the x-intercepts from the graph.
\boldsymbol{x=-2} and \boldsymbol{x=4}, the points (-2,0) and (4,0).
(b) Confirm by solving x^2-2x-8=0 algebraically, and state the zeros of the function.
(x-4)(x+2)=0, so x=4 or x=-2.
The zeros of y=x^2-2x-8 are -2 and 4.
4

Writing an Equation from Its Roots

Work backwards: a root r comes from the factor (x-r). A fractional root x=\tfrac{p}{q} comes from the factor (qx-p), which keeps the coefficients integers.

If the roots are r_1 and r_2, an equation is (x-r_1)(x-r_2)=0.
Root x=\tfrac{2}{3}\ \Rightarrow\ factor (3x-2);   root x=-\tfrac{1}{4}\ \Rightarrow\ factor (4x+1).

Multiplying the equation by any non-zero constant gives an equation with the same roots, so there are many correct answers; give the one with the smallest integer coefficients.

Example 6

Roots → equation

Write a quadratic equation in standard form, with integer coefficients, that has the given roots.

(a) 5 and -3
(x-5)(x+3)=0
\boldsymbol{x^2-2x-15=0}
(b) -\tfrac{1}{2} and 4
x=-\tfrac{1}{2}\ \Rightarrow\ 2x+1=0
(2x+1)(x-4)=0
\boldsymbol{2x^2-7x-4=0}
(c) 0 and -\tfrac{7}{3}
x(3x+7)=0
\boldsymbol{3x^2+7x=0}
(d) a double root of \tfrac{3}{5}
(5x-3)(5x-3)=0
\boldsymbol{25x^2-30x+9=0}
5

Problems That Lead to a Quadratic Equation

1Let a variable stand for an unknown (draw a diagram when there is a shape) and write the other unknowns in terms of it.
2Write an equation from the information, put it in standard form and solve it by factoring.
3Reject inadmissible roots: a length, area or time cannot be negative, a count must be a whole number, and a dimension cannot make another dimension negative.
4Answer the question asked, with units.
Example 7

Number problems

(a) The product of two consecutive odd integers is 143. Determine the integers.
Let the integers be n and n+2.
n(n+2)=143
n^2+2n-143=0
(n+13)(n-11)=0
n=-13 or n=11
The integers are 11 and 13, or -13 and -11 (both pairs work).
(b) Twice the square of a number is 15 more than the number. Determine the number.
Let the number be n.
2n^2=n+15
2n^2-n-15=0
(2n+5)(n-3)=0
The number is 3 or -\tfrac{5}{2}.
Check: 2(3)^2=18=3+15 ✓
Example 8

A garden and a picture frame

(a) A rectangular garden is 3 m longer than twice its width. Its area is 65\text{ m}^2. Determine its dimensions and its perimeter.
Let the width be w; the length is 2w+3.
w(2w+3)=65
2w^2+3w-65=0
(2w+13)(w-5)=0
w=-\tfrac{13}{2} (reject: a width is positive) or w=5
The garden is 5 m by 13 m; perimeter =2(5+13)=\boldsymbol{36} m.
(b) A 20 cm by 14 cm photo has a frame of uniform width x cm around it. The photo and frame together cover 432\text{ cm}^2. Determine x.
The outer dimensions are (20+2x) by (14+2x).
(20+2x)(14+2x)=432
280+68x+4x^2=432
4x^2+68x-152=0\ \Rightarrow\ x^2+17x-38=0
(x+19)(x-2)=0, so x=-19 (reject) or x=2.
The frame is 2 cm wide. (Check: 24\times18=432 ✓)

Height and time: which answers make sense?

A height formula h in terms of the time t gives a parabola. The ground is h=0; a given height is a horizontal line. A time before the throw (t<0) is rejected, but two positive times can both be right: one on the way up, one on the way down.

Example 9

The ball from the balcony

A ball is thrown upward from a balcony. Its height above the ground after t seconds is h=-5t^2+20t+25 metres.

(a) When does the ball hit the ground?
The ground is h=0.
-5t^2+20t+25=0
Divide by -5: t^2-4t-5=0
(t-5)(t+1)=0
t=5 or t=-1 (reject: time after the throw is not negative).
It lands after 5 s.
(b) When is the ball 40 m above the ground?
-5t^2+20t+25=40
-5t^2+20t-15=0
t^2-4t+3=0
(t-1)(t-3)=0
t=1 s (on the way up) and t=3 s (on the way down). Both are admissible.
✓

Key ideas

Zero product property. If ab=0, then a=0 or b=0. Set each factor with a variable equal to 0. A constant factor (-5, 3) is never 0 and gives no root.
Standard form first. Move everything to one side (=0), divide out a common constant or clear fractions, factor fully, solve, check. Never divide by x: factor it out. A squared factor gives a double root.
Same numbers, three names. Roots of ax^2+bx+c=0 = zeros of y=ax^2+bx+c = x-intercepts of its graph. Backwards: root r → factor (x-r); root \tfrac{p}{q} → factor (qx-p).
Problems. Let a variable stand for an unknown, write and solve the equation, then reject inadmissible roots (negative lengths, areas or times; non-whole counts). Answer with units.
