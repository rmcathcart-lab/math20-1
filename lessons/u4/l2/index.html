Who Am I?

I am a two-digit number. Multiply my two digits and you get 24. Add them and you get 10.

How do you know?
46, or 64! The digits must be 4 and 6: 4\times6=24 and 4+6=10. (3 and 8 multiply to 24 but add to 11; 2 and 12 is not two digits.) Two numbers fit the riddle, depending on which digit comes first.

Factoring ax^2+bx+c

When the leading coefficient is not 1, look for two numbers with product \textcolor{#a33357}{ac} and sum \textcolor{#12805a}{b}. They split the middle term in two, and the four terms then factor by grouping. This is called decomposition.

I can…
  • factor ax^2+bx+c by decomposition: find m and n with mn=ac and m+n=b, split the middle term, group
  • use the sign rules for ac and b, and recognize a trinomial that does not factor over the integers
  • factor by guess and check and check any factoring by expanding
  • take out the GCF (or -1) first so the answer is fully factored
  • factor trinomials in two variables and with fraction coefficients
  • recognize and factor perfect-square trinomials A^2\pm2AB+B^2=(A\pm B)^2
RF1 Factor polynomial expressions of the form ax^2+bx+c, a^2x^2-b^2y^2, a(f(x))^2+b(f(x))+c and a^2(f(x))^2-b^2(g(y))^2 (a, b and c rational).

The Method of Decomposition

When two binomials are expanded, the two middle products combine into the x-term. In (2x+3)(x+4) the four parts of the area model are 2x^2, \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{8x}, \textcolor{#c27a0e}{3x} and 12, so the trinomial is 2x^2+11x+12. The two middle parts multiply to 8x\cdot3x=24x^2, and (2x^2)(12)=24x^2 too. So the middle numbers 8 and 3 multiply to ac=2\times12=24 and add to b=11.

This is always true, so to factor ax^2+bx+c we decompose (split) the middle term using two numbers with product ac and sum b, then factor by grouping.

Decomposition for ax^2+bx+c
1 · Find
m and n with m\times n=\textcolor{#a33357}{ac} and m+n=\textcolor{#12805a}{b}.
2 · Split
Rewrite bx as \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{mx}+\textcolor{#c27a0e}{nx}.
3 · Group
Factor the four terms by grouping.
4 · Check
Expand your answer.
Example 1

The middle products

(a) Expand (3x+2)(2x+5), showing all four products before you collect like terms.
6x^2+15x+4x+10=\boldsymbol{6x^2+19x+10}
(b) For 6x^2+19x+10:  ac= 60. The two middle coefficients from (a) are 15 and 4: their product is 60 and their sum is 19.
ac=6\times10=60; the middle coefficients 15 and 4 have product 60 and sum 19.

The product–sum hunt

Draw the X: ac on top, b underneath. List the factor pairs of ac and check their sums until one is b, and circle it. Then split, group and factor out the common bracket.

Negative Terms, and Guess and Check

The sign rules from Lesson 1 still apply, now to ac: if ac>0 the two numbers have the same sign as b; if ac<0 they have opposite signs. When you group, take a negative factor out of the second pair if needed so the brackets match. If no pair of integers works, the trinomial does not factor over the integers.

Which signs? Tap to check.

ac>0,\ b>0
both positive
2x^2+7x+3: 6,\ 1
ac>0,\ b<0
both negative
2x^2-7x+3: -6,\ -1
ac<0,\ b>0
opposite signs, the bigger one positive
2x^2+5x-3: 6,\ -1
ac<0,\ b<0
opposite signs, the bigger one negative
2x^2-5x-3: -6,\ 1
Example 3

Decomposition with negative terms

Factor by decomposition.

(a) 4x^2-4x-15
ac=-60, sum -4: -10,\ 6
4x^2-10x+6x-15
=2x(2x-5)+3(2x-5)
=\boldsymbol{(2x-5)(2x+3)}
(b) 6a^2-13a+6
ac=36, sum -13: -4,\ -9
6a^2-4a-9a+6
=2a(3a-2)-3(3a-2)
=\boldsymbol{(3a-2)(2a-3)}
(c) 5y^2+13y-6
ac=-30, sum 13: 15,\ -2
5y^2+15y-2y-6
=5y(y+3)-2(y+3)
=\boldsymbol{(y+3)(5y-2)}

Guess and check

Guess and check is another method. Choose factors of a for the first terms of the binomials and factors of c for the last terms, expand mentally, and adjust until the middle term is right. It is fast when a and c have few factors; decomposition is more reliable when they have many.

Example 4

Guess and check

Factor 3x^2-7x-6 by guess and check.

First terms must be 3x and x: (3x\quad)(x\quad). The last terms multiply to -6.
Try (3x-2)(x+3): middle term 9x-2x=7x. Right size, wrong sign: switch the signs.
Try (3x+2)(x-3): middle term -9x+2x=-7x ✓   3x^2-7x-6=\boldsymbol{(3x+2)(x-3)}

GCF First, and Negative Leading Coefficients

Always take out the GCF first: the trinomial left is smaller and easier to decompose, and the answer is then fully factored. If a is negative, take out -1 (or the negative GCF) first. A trinomial in ascending order can be factored either way, as in Lesson 1.
!
Fully factored? Decomposing 12x^2+2x-4 without the GCF gives (2x-1)(6x+4), but 6x+4 still has a common factor of 2. Look inside every bracket before you stop.
Example 5

Factor fully

(a) 12x^2+2x-4
=2(6x^2+x-2);  ac=-12, sum 1: 4,\ -3
=2(6x^2+4x-3x-2)
=2[2x(3x+2)-1(3x+2)]
=\boldsymbol{2(3x+2)(2x-1)}
(b) -6x^2+7x+3
=-(6x^2-7x-3);  ac=-18, sum -7: -9,\ 2
=-(6x^2-9x+2x-3)
=-[3x(2x-3)+1(2x-3)]
=\boldsymbol{-(2x-3)(3x+1)}
(c) 8+2x-3x^2
=-(3x^2-2x-8);  ac=-24, sum -2: -6,\ 4
=-[3x(x-2)+4(x-2)]=\boldsymbol{-(x-2)(3x+4)}
or, in ascending order, \boldsymbol{(2-x)(4+3x)}.
(d) 18x^3-33x^2-30x
=3x(6x^2-11x-10);  ac=-60, sum -11: -15,\ 4
=3x[3x(2x-5)+2(2x-5)]
=\boldsymbol{3x(2x-5)(3x+2)}

Two Variables and Rational Coefficients

For ax^2+bxy+cy^2, decompose exactly as before; each binomial has an x-term and a y-term.
Example 6

Two variables

Factor.

(a) 3x^2+7xy-6y^2
ac=-18, sum 7: 9,\ -2
3x^2+9xy-2xy-6y^2
=3x(x+3y)-2y(x+3y)
=\boldsymbol{(x+3y)(3x-2y)}
(b) 10a^2-23ab+12b^2
ac=120, sum -23: -8,\ -15
10a^2-8ab-15ab+12b^2
=2a(5a-4b)-3b(5a-4b)
=\boldsymbol{(5a-4b)(2a-3b)}

Fraction coefficients

The coefficients a, b and c may be fractions. Take out a fractional common factor so that integers are left in the brackets: dividing each term by \tfrac{1}{2} is the same as multiplying it by 2. When the denominators differ, take out \tfrac{1}{\text{LCD}}.

\dfrac{1}{2}x^2-x-4=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(x^2-2x-8\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}(x-4)(x+2)
Check: \tfrac{1}{2}\times x^2, \tfrac{1}{2}\times(-2x), \tfrac{1}{2}\times(-8) give back the original terms.
Example 7

Fraction coefficients

Factor fully.

(a) \tfrac{1}{2}x^2+x-12
=\tfrac{1}{2}(x^2+2x-24)
=\boldsymbol{\tfrac{1}{2}(x+6)(x-4)}
(b) \tfrac{3}{4}x^2-3x-24
=\tfrac{3}{4}(x^2-4x-32)
(3\div\tfrac{3}{4}=4, 24\div\tfrac{3}{4}=32)
=\boldsymbol{\tfrac{3}{4}(x-8)(x+4)}
(c) \tfrac{1}{2}x^2+\tfrac{5}{4}x+\tfrac{1}{2}
LCD 4: =\tfrac{1}{4}(2x^2+5x+2)
ac=4, sum 5: 4,\ 1
=\boldsymbol{\tfrac{1}{4}(2x+1)(x+2)}

Perfect-Square Trinomials

Squaring a binomial gives a perfect-square trinomial:

A^2+2AB+B^2=(A+B)^2A^2-2AB+B^2=(A-B)^2
Test: the first and last terms are perfect squares (A^2 and B^2, both positive), and the middle term is \pm2AB.

Decomposition also works on these, but recognizing the pattern is faster. A trinomial like x^2+6x-9 is not a perfect square: its last term is negative.

Example 8

Spot the perfect squares

(a) Which are perfect-square trinomials? Write those in the form (A\pm B)^2.

(i) 9x^2+12x+4
A=3x, B=2, 2AB=12x ✓
\boldsymbol{(3x+2)^2}
(ii) x^2-10x+16
B=4 but 2(x)(4)=8x\ne10x. No.
(iii) 25x^2-20xy+4y^2
2(5x)(2y)=20xy ✓
\boldsymbol{(5x-2y)^2}
(iv) 4x^2+6x+9
2(2x)(3)=12x\ne6x. No.
(b) Fill in the blank so that each trinomial is a perfect square.
x^2-{}18x{}+81    16x^2+40x+{}25    9x^2{}+42x+49
2(x)(9)=18x;   40x=2(4x)B\Rightarrow B=5, B^2=25;   42x=2A(7)\Rightarrow A=3x, A^2=9x^2.
Example 9

Perfect squares at work

(a) Factor 64x^2-48x+9.
A=8x, B=3, 2AB=48x:  \boldsymbol{(8x-3)^2}
(b) Factor fully: 2x^3+20x^2+50x.
2x(x^2+10x+25)=\boldsymbol{2x(x+5)^2}
(c) A square floor tile has an area of (4x^2+28x+49) cm². Find an expression for its side length, then its side and perimeter when x=3.
4x^2+28x+49=(2x+7)^2, so the side is (2x+7) cm.
When x=3: side 13 cm, perimeter 4(13)= 52 cm (area 169 cm²).
✓

Key ideas

Decomposition. Find m,n with mn=ac, m+n=b. Split bx=mx+nx, group in pairs, take out the common bracket. Check by expanding.
Signs. ac>0: same sign as b. ac<0: opposite signs, the bigger one like b. Take out a negative so the brackets match. No pair: does not factor over the integers.
GCF first. Take out the GCF, or -1 when a<0, so the answer is fully factored. Fractions: take out \tfrac{1}{\text{LCD}} (or the fraction in front) so integers are left.
Patterns. Two variables: each bracket has an x- and a y-term. Perfect squares: A^2\pm2AB+B^2=(A\pm B)^2; the last term must be positive.
