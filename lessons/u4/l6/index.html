Solving Radical Equations and Checking for Extraneous Roots

A radical equation has the variable under a square root. To solve it we square both sides, which undoes the root. But squaring can't tell 3 from -3, so it can hand us a fake answer. That is why every answer gets checked.

I can…
  • state the restriction on the variable by solving “radicand \ge 0” (and reverse the sign when I divide by a negative)
  • solve a radical equation: restriction, isolate the radical, square both sides, solve, verify
  • explain why squaring can create an extraneous root, and reject it
  • solve equations with a radical on each side, squaring any coefficient too
  • check a radical equation with a graph: the roots are where the two sides meet
  • solve a formula with a square root for the variable under the root
AN3 Solve problems that involve radical equations (limited to square roots).
Today: restrictions, isolate–square–solve–verify, extraneous roots, a radical on each side, and formulas with a square root.
1

Restrictions on the Variable

A radical equation has the variable under a radical sign. In this course the radicals are square roots. A square root of a negative number is not a real number, so the radicand must be non-negative.
Key idea
\sqrt{\textcolor{#7b4fc4}{A}} is defined only when \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{A}\ge0. To find the restriction, solve the inequality “radicand \ge0”. Dividing or multiplying an inequality by a negative number reverses the sign.
The restriction is the domain of the graph: y=\sqrt{x-2} starts at x=2 and goes right; y=\sqrt{3-x} starts at x=3 and goes left. When an equation has two radicals, x must satisfy both restrictions at once.
y=\sqrt{x-2},\ x\ge2
y=\sqrt{3-x},\ x\le3
Example 1

State the restriction

State the restriction on the variable.

(a) \sqrt{x+4}=6
x+4\ge0
\boldsymbol{x\ge-4}
(b) \sqrt{2x-5}+1=4
2x-5\ge0
\boldsymbol{x\ge\tfrac{5}{2}}
(c) \sqrt{6-3x}=9
6-3x\ge0\Rightarrow-3x\ge-6
\boldsymbol{x\le2} (sign reverses)
(d) \sqrt{x-1}+\sqrt{5-x}=2
x\ge1 and x\le5
\boldsymbol{1\le x\le5}
(e) \sqrt{x}=\sqrt{x+3}-1
x\ge0 and x\ge-3
Both hold when \boldsymbol{x\ge0}.
2

Solving by Squaring and Verifying

Squaring undoes a square root: \left(\sqrt{A}\right)^2=A when A\ge0. Square both sides, not each term: get the radical alone first. If there is a number in front of the radical, divide by it first (or square it too: \left(2\sqrt{A}\right)^2=4A).
Solving a radical equation
1 State the restriction
2 Isolate the radical
3 Square both sides
4 Solve
5 Verify each answer in the original equation (left side = right side)
Example 2

Solve and verify

Solve and verify.

(a) \sqrt{3x+1}=5
Restriction: x\ge-\tfrac{1}{3}
3x+1=25
3x=24\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{x=8}
Verify: \sqrt{3(8)+1}=\sqrt{25}=5 ✓
(b) 2\sqrt{x-1}+3=9
Restriction: x\ge1
2\sqrt{x-1}=6\Rightarrow\sqrt{x-1}=3
x-1=9\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{x=10}
Verify: 2\sqrt{9}+3=6+3=9 ✓
(c) \sqrt{4x-3}-7=-2
Restriction: x\ge\tfrac{3}{4}
\sqrt{4x-3}=5
4x-3=25\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{x=7}
Verify: \sqrt{25}-7=-2 ✓
(d) \sqrt{\dfrac{x+4}{3}}=2
Restriction: x\ge-4
\tfrac{x+4}{3}=4
x+4=12\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{x=8}
Verify: \sqrt{\tfrac{12}{3}}=\sqrt{4}=2 ✓
3

Extraneous Roots

The symbol \sqrt{\phantom{x}} means the principal (non-negative) square root: \sqrt{9}=3, never -3. So an equation like \sqrt{x}=-3 can have no solution.

Squaring is not a reversible step. If a=b then a^2=b^2, but a^2=b^2 does not mean a=b: 3^2=(-3)^2 and 3\ne-3. The squared equation can therefore have a root that the original equation does not have. A value found by correct algebra that does not satisfy the original equation is an extraneous root. It must be rejected, which is why every answer is verified.

Key idea
After isolating: if \sqrt{A} equals a negative number, there is no solution (squaring would produce an extraneous root). A value that breaks a restriction is also extraneous.
!
A negative root is fine. What can't be negative is the value of the square root, not x. In \sqrt{1-x}=3, squaring gives 1-x=9, so x=-8, and it checks: \sqrt{1-(-8)}=\sqrt{9}=3 ✓.
Example 3

Catch the extraneous root

(a) Solve \sqrt{x}=-3 by squaring. Verify, and state the solution.
Squaring: x=9.
Verify: left side \sqrt{9}=3, right side -3. 3\ne-3.
x=9 is extraneous. There is no solution.
(b) Solve \sqrt{x+3}+5=2 and verify.
Restriction: x\ge-3
\sqrt{x+3}=-3 (already impossible: a square root is never negative)
Squaring anyway: x+3=9\Rightarrow x=6
Verify: \sqrt{9}+5=8\ne2. x=6 is extraneous; no solution.
(c) The graphs of y=\sqrt{x+3}+5 and y=2 are shown. How do they confirm your answer to (b)?
The radical graph starts at (-3,5) and only rises, so it never reaches the line y=2. No intersection means no solution.

A Radical on Each Side

When each side is a single radical, square both sides at once: \left(\sqrt{A}\right)^2=\left(\sqrt{B}\right)^2 gives A=B. A coefficient in front of a radical is squared too. Both radicands must be non-negative, so check the answer in both radicands: a value that makes either one negative is extraneous.
Example 4

A radical on each side

Solve and verify.

(a) \sqrt{5x-2}=\sqrt{2x+7}
Restrictions: x\ge\tfrac{2}{5} and x\ge-\tfrac{7}{2}, so x\ge\tfrac{2}{5}.
5x-2=2x+7
3x=9\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{x=3}
Verify: \sqrt{13}=\sqrt{13} ✓
(b) 3\sqrt{x-2}=\sqrt{5x+6}
Restriction: x\ge2
9(x-2)=5x+6
9x-18=5x+6
4x=24\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{x=6}
Verify: 3\sqrt{4}=6, \sqrt{36}=6 ✓
(c) \sqrt{2x-9}=\sqrt{x-7}
Restriction: x\ge\tfrac{9}{2} and x\ge7, so x\ge7.
2x-9=x-7\Rightarrow x=2
Verify: \sqrt{-5} is not real (x=2 breaks x\ge7).
x=2 is extraneous: no solution.
!
Square the coefficient too. \left(3\sqrt{x-2}\right)^2=3^2\left(\sqrt{x-2}\right)^2=9(x-2), not 3(x-2).
5

Checking with a Graph

A graphing calculator can solve or check a radical equation. Enter the left side as Y_1 and the right side as Y_2, press GRAPH, then use 2ndTRACE (CALC), 5: intersect. The x-coordinate of each point of intersection is a root. If the graphs do not meet, there is no solution; an extraneous root shows up as an x-value where the graphs do not meet. Graphing also gives decimal approximations when a root is not a whole number.
Example 5

Algebra and a graph

Consider the equation \sqrt{2x-4}=4.

(a) Solve algebraically and verify.
Restriction: x\ge2
2x-4=16\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{x=10}
Verify: \sqrt{16}=4 ✓
(b) Graph Y_1=\sqrt{2x-4} and Y_2=4 in the window x\!:[-2,14,2], y\!:[-2,6,1]. Sketch the display and mark the point of intersection.
The graphs meet at (10,4), so the root is \boldsymbol{x=10}. The radical graph starts at (2,0): no part of it exists for x<2.
6

Problems Modelled by a Radical Equation

Many formulas contain a square root. To find the variable under the radical, substitute the known values, isolate the radical, square both sides and solve. Round only at the end, as the question asks.
Example 6

Skid marks

A police officer estimates a car’s speed from the length of its skid marks with the formula s=4\sqrt{10d}, where s is the speed in km/h and d is the length of the skid marks in metres. How long are the skid marks of a car travelling at 80 km/h?

80=4\sqrt{10d}\Rightarrow\sqrt{10d}=20\Rightarrow10d=400\Rightarrow d=40
Verify: 4\sqrt{400}=4(20)=80 ✓   The skid marks are 40 m long.

The pendulum: a formula with a fraction under the root

The period of a pendulum is T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L}{9.8}}. To find L, peel the formula from the outside in: divide by 2\pi, square, then multiply by 9.8.

Example 7

The pendulum

The period of a pendulum (the time for one full swing, in seconds) is T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L}{9.8}}, where L is its length in metres.

(a) Determine the period of a 1.5 m pendulum, to the nearest hundredth of a second.
T=2\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{1.5}{9.8}}
=2\pi\sqrt{0.15306\ldots}
\approx\boldsymbol{2.46} s
(b) Determine the length that gives a period of 2.5 s, to the nearest centimetre.
2.5=2\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{L}{9.8}}
\sqrt{\tfrac{L}{9.8}}=\tfrac{2.5}{2\pi}=0.39789\ldots
\tfrac{L}{9.8}=0.15831\ldots
L\approx1.551 m, about 155 cm
(c) Solve the formula for L.
\tfrac{T}{2\pi}=\sqrt{\tfrac{L}{9.8}}
\tfrac{T^2}{4\pi^2}=\tfrac{L}{9.8}
\boldsymbol{L=\dfrac{9.8T^2}{4\pi^2}}
✓

Key ideas

Restriction first. Radicand \ge0. Dividing by a negative reverses the sign: 6-3x\ge0\Rightarrow x\le2. Two radicals: both at once.
Isolate, square, solve, verify. Get the radical alone, then square both sides (not each term). Square a coefficient too: \left(2\sqrt{A}\right)^2=4A.
Extraneous roots. 3^2=(-3)^2, so squaring can add a root the original doesn't have. If \sqrt{A}= a negative number: no solution. Reject any value that fails the check or breaks a restriction.
Graphs and formulas. Roots are where Y_1 and Y_2 meet; no meeting, no solution. In a formula, substitute, isolate the root, square, solve, and round at the end.
