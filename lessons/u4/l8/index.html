Warm-up

The Mystery Box

A shipping box has whole-number edges (in cm), all different. The label only gives a formula for its volume: V=x^3+6x^2+8x

1
For x=1, what volume does the label give? Which three different whole numbers multiply to it?
1+6+8=15 cm³, and 15=1\times3\times5.
2
For x=2: the volume, and the three edges?
8+24+16=48 cm³, and 48=2\times4\times6. A pattern: x, then 2 more, then 4 more.
!
The big one. Which three edges give x^3+6x^2+8x for every x? Guess first.
A x,\ x+6,\ x+8
B x,\ x+2,\ x+4
C x^2,\ 6x,\ 8x
D x+1,\ x+3,\ x+5
B. Every term has an x: take out the GCF, x(x^2+6x+8). Then two numbers with product 8 and sum 6: 2 and 4. So V=x(x+2)(x+4). Check x=10: 10\times12\times14=1\,680 and 1\,000+600+80=1\,680 ✓.
A just reads off the coefficients. C uses the terms as edges, but the volume is their sum, not their product. D would give a constant term of 15.

Challenge your neighbour: make your own mystery box. Pick edges like x, x+1, x+5, expand the volume, and see if they can unpack it.

Factoring methods tap a grey box to check yourself

MethodWhen to use itExample
Greatest common factor (GCF)Always first. Take out a negative GCF if the leading term is negative. A binomial can be the common factor. -6x^2+18x= -6x(x-3)
5a(a-2)+3(a-2)= (a-2)(5a+3)
GroupingFour terms: group in pairs, factor each pair, take out the common binomial. x^3-4x^2+3x-12= x^2(x-4)+3(x-4)
= (x-4)(x^2+3)
x^2+bx+cTwo numbers with product c and sum b. Works with two variables. x^2-3xy-28y^2= (x-7y)(x+4y)
ax^2+bx+c (decomposition)Two numbers with product ac and sum b; split the middle term and group. Factor out a fraction for rational coefficients. 6x^2+7x-3: ac= -18, 9+(-2) =7
= 3x(2x+3)-(2x+3)=(2x+3)(3x-1)
Perfect square trinomialFirst and last terms are squares and the middle term is 2AB. 9x^2-30x+25= (3x-5)^2
Difference of squaresA^2-B^2=(A-B)(A+B). A sum of squares does not factor. x^4-81= (x^2-9)(x^2+9)
= (x-3)(x+3)(x^2+9)
SubstitutionA repeated expression f(x) or x^2: let A=f(x), factor, substitute back, simplify each factor. (x+1)^2-5(x+1)+6= (A-2)(A-3)
= (x-1)(x-2)
Factoring checklist
1GCF first.
2Count the terms: two → difference of squares; three → x^2+bx+c, decomposition, perfect square, or substitution; four → grouping.
3Check every factor and factor again if you can (factor fully).
4Check by expanding.

A binomial is a factor of a polynomial if it appears in the fully factored form, or is a product of factors that do.

The factoring machine the checklist as a sorting machine: guess each gate before you press Next

Solving by factoring

Steps. Move every term to one side (ax^2+bx+c=0), divide out a common factor, factor, and use the zero product property: if AB=0 then A=0 or B=0. Never divide both sides by an expression containing the variable (a root is lost).
Same numbers, three names. The roots of ax^2+bx+c=0, the zeros of y=ax^2+bx+c and the x-intercepts of its graph. Roots r_1 and r_2 give the equation (x-r_1)(x-r_2)=0; a root \frac{p}{q} gives the factor (qx-p). In a word problem, reject roots that are inadmissible (a negative length or time).

Radical equations

1State the restrictions: each radicand \ge0, and the side opposite an isolated radical \ge0.
2Isolate a radical.
3Square both sides (square a binomial side as (a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2).
4If a radical remains, isolate it and square again.
5Solve (often a quadratic by factoring).
6Verify every root in the original equation.
!
Squaring can create an extraneous root because a^2=b^2 does not mean a=b (for example 3^2=(-3)^2).

Common errors tap a card to see the fix

(x-2)^2\ne x^2+4
(x-2)^2=x^2-4x+4
(2x-4)(x+2) is not fully factored
2(x-2)(x+2)
x^2+9 does not factor
a sum of squares: no two numbers have product 9 and sum 0
Dividing x^2=5x by x loses the root x=0
x(x-5)=0: x=0 or x=5
A root of the squared equation is not automatically a root of the radical equation
Verify every root in the original

Factoring

Factor fully: keep going until no factor can be factored again. A bracket can be the thing that repeats: box it, call it \textcolor{#c27a0e}{A} (and \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{B}), factor, then unwrap in brackets and simplify each factor. Simplifying can uncover a new GCF.
Example 1

Factor fully

(a) 4x^3-8x^2-60x
GCF: =4x(x^2-2x-15)
=\boldsymbol{4x(x-5)(x+3)}
(b) 10x^2-11xy-6y^2
ac=-60; -15+4=-11
=10x^2-15xy+4xy-6y^2
=5x(2x-3y)+2y(2x-3y)=\boldsymbol{(2x-3y)(5x+2y)}
(c) x^3+2x^2-9x-18
=x^2(x+2)-9(x+2)
=(x+2)(x^2-9)
=\boldsymbol{(x+2)(x-3)(x+3)}
(d) \frac{1}{3}x^2-2x-9
=\frac{1}{3}(x^2-6x-27)
=\boldsymbol{\frac{1}{3}(x-9)(x+3)}

Check each part with the factoring machine above (Example 1 chips).

Example 2

Substitution and differences of squares

Factor fully.

(a) 3(x-1)^2+4(x-1)-4
Let A=x-1: 3A^2+4A-4=(3A-2)(A+2)
=[3(x-1)-2][(x-1)+2]
=\boldsymbol{(3x-5)(x+1)}
(b) 9x^4-37x^2+4
Let A=x^2: 9A^2-37A+4=(9A-1)(A-4)
=(9x^2-1)(x^2-4)
=\boldsymbol{(3x-1)(3x+1)(x-2)(x+2)}
(c) 16x^4-81y^4
=(4x^2-9y^2)(4x^2+9y^2)
=\boldsymbol{(2x-3y)(2x+3y)(4x^2+9y^2)}
(The sum of squares does not factor.)
(d) 25(x+1)^2-4(x-2)^2
=[5(x+1)-2(x-2)][5(x+1)+2(x-2)]
=(3x+9)(7x+1)
=\boldsymbol{3(x+3)(7x+1)}
(e) \dfrac{x^2}{9}-\dfrac{y^2}{4}
=\boldsymbol{\left(\dfrac{x}{3}-\dfrac{y}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{x}{3}+\dfrac{y}{2}\right)}

Is it a factor? Finding k

To test a binomial, factor fully and look for it among the factors. To find k so that x^2+kx+c factors, list every factor pair of c: each pair’s sum is a possible k. For a perfect square, the middle term must be \pm2AB.

Example 3

Factors and missing coefficients

(a) Is x+4 a factor of 2x^2+5x-12?
ac=-24; 8+(-3)=5:
2x^2+8x-3x-12=2x(x+4)-3(x+4)=(x+4)(2x-3). Yes.
(b) Is 3x+2 a factor of 6x^3+x^2-2x?
=x(6x^2+x-2)=x(3x+2)(2x-1). Yes.
(c) Find every integer k so that x^2+kx-12 can be factored over the integers.
Pairs with product -12: 1,-12; -1,12; 2,-6; -2,6; 3,-4; -3,4.
\boldsymbol{k=\pm11,\ \pm4,\ \pm1}
(d) Find k so that 9x^2+kx+49 is a perfect square trinomial.
9x^2=(3x)^2, 49=7^2, so (3x\pm7)^2=9x^2\pm42x+49.
\boldsymbol{k=42} or \boldsymbol{k=-42}

Solving Quadratic Equations

Get 0 on one side first: (x-4)(x+1)=6 does not say either factor is 6. Then factor and set each factor equal to 0. Check the solving pipeline above (Example 4 chips). On a graph, the roots are where the parabola crosses the x-axis; a double root is where it just touches.
Example 4

Solve by factoring

(a) 6x^2=7x+3
6x^2-7x-3=0; ac=-18, -9+2=-7
3x(2x-3)+(2x-3)=0
(2x-3)(3x+1)=0, \boldsymbol{x=\frac{3}{2}} or \boldsymbol{x=-\frac{1}{3}}
(b) (x-4)(x+1)=6
Expand first: x^2-3x-4=6
x^2-3x-10=0
(x-5)(x+2)=0, \boldsymbol{x=5} or \boldsymbol{x=-2}
(c) 3x^2-48=0
3(x^2-16)=0\Rightarrow3(x-4)(x+4)=0
\boldsymbol{x=4} or \boldsymbol{x=-4}
(d) 4x^2+20x+25=0
(2x+5)^2=0
\boldsymbol{x=-\frac{5}{2}} (a double root)
Example 5

Zeros, intercepts and equations from roots

(a) Determine the zeros of y=2x^2-x-6 and the x-intercepts of its graph.
2x^2-x-6=0: ac=-12, -4+3=-1,
2x(x-2)+3(x-2)=(x-2)(2x+3)=0
Zeros: \boldsymbol{2} and \boldsymbol{-\frac{3}{2}}. x-intercepts: \boldsymbol{(2,\,0)} and \boldsymbol{\left(-\frac{3}{2},\,0\right)}.
(b) Write a quadratic equation with integer coefficients whose roots are \frac{2}{3} and -5.
Factors (3x-2) and (x+5):
(3x-2)(x+5)=0, so \boldsymbol{3x^2+13x-10=0}.

Word problems: draw it, write it, reject what can’t happen

Let the unknown be a length, write the area (or product) as an equation, get 0 on one side and factor. A negative length or time is a real root of the equation but inadmissible in the problem. Check the answer in the story, not just in the equation.

Example 6

The picture frame

A 20 cm by 30 cm photo is surrounded by a frame of uniform width x cm. The photo and frame together cover 1\,064 cm². Determine the width of the frame.

(20+2x)(30+2x)=1\,064
600+100x+4x^2=1\,064\Rightarrow4x^2+100x-464=0\Rightarrow4(x^2+25x-116)=0\Rightarrow4(x+29)(x-4)=0
x=4 or x=-29 (a width cannot be negative: inadmissible).
The frame is 4 cm wide. (Check: 28\times38=1\,064 ✓)

Radical Equations

Each side of a radical equation is a graph: the roots are the x-values where the two graphs meet. Squaring both sides also lets in the meeting points of the other side with the mirror image of the radical (the “-\sqrt{\ }” half), and those are the extraneous roots. Check the radical-equation steps above (Example 7 chips).
Example 7

Solve and verify

(a) 3\sqrt{2x-1}-2=7
Restriction: x\ge\frac{1}{2}
3\sqrt{2x-1}=9\Rightarrow\sqrt{2x-1}=3
2x-1=9\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{x=5}
Verify: 3\sqrt{9}-2=7 ✓
(b) \sqrt{x+8}+4=x
\sqrt{x+8}=x-4 (restrictions: x\ge4)
x+8=x^2-8x+16
0=x^2-9x+8=(x-1)(x-8)
x=8: \sqrt{16}+4=8 ✓
x=1: \sqrt{9}+4=7\ne1 (extraneous)
Root: \boldsymbol{x=8}

Two radicals: isolate one, square, isolate the other, square again

With two radicals, move one to the other side first. Squaring 1+\sqrt{x+4} is squaring a binomial: (a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2 leaves a middle term 2\sqrt{x+4} with a radical still in it, so isolate that and square again.

Example 8

Two radicals

Consider the equation \sqrt{2x+1}-\sqrt{x+4}=1.

(a) State the restrictions on x.
2x+1\ge0\Rightarrow x\ge-\frac{1}{2}, and x+4\ge0\Rightarrow x\ge-4. Together: \boldsymbol{x\ge-\frac{1}{2}}.
(b) Explain why an extraneous root may appear when this equation is solved algebraically.
Solving requires squaring both sides. If two quantities have equal squares, they are not necessarily equal (a^2=b^2 also when a=-b), so the squared equation can have a root that the original equation does not.
(c) Solve the equation and verify.
\sqrt{2x+1}=1+\sqrt{x+4}
2x+1=1+2\sqrt{x+4}+x+4\;\Rightarrow\;x-4=2\sqrt{x+4}
x^2-8x+16=4x+16\;\Rightarrow\;x^2-12x=0\;\Rightarrow\;x(x-12)=0
x=12: \sqrt{25}-\sqrt{16}=1 ✓    x=0: \sqrt{1}-\sqrt{4}=-1\ne1 (extraneous)
The root is \boldsymbol{x=12}.
✓

Key ideas

Factoring checklist. GCF first (negative if the leading term is). Count the terms: 2 → difference of squares (a sum doesn’t factor); 3 → product–sum, decomposition, perfect square or substitution; 4 → grouping. Factor again, then expand to check.
Solving by factoring. 0 on one side, divide out a number GCF (never x), factor, zero product property. Roots = zeros = x-intercepts. Root \frac{p}{q} → factor (qx-p).
Word problems. Sketch, let x be the unknown, write the equation, solve, and reject an inadmissible root (negative length or time). Check in the story.
Radical equations. Restrictions, isolate, square (a binomial side as (a\pm b)^2), square again if a radical remains, solve, verify every root: squaring can add an extraneous root.
