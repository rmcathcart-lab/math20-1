Warm-up

Two Truths and a Lie

Two of these are true and one is a lie. Which one is the lie?

AWalk once around the outside of any triangle and you turn a total of 360°.
BYou can build a triangle from sticks 3 cm, 4 cm and 8 cm long.
CEvery triangle has at least two acute angles.
?
How could you prove it?
B is the lie. The two short sticks together are only 3 + 4 = 7 cm, so they can’t reach across 8 cm. A is true: you turn at each corner, and after one lap you face the way you started, a full 360°. C is true: the angles add to 180°, so two of them can’t both be 90° or more.

Unit Summary

Standard position: vertex at the origin, initial arm on the positive x-axis, rotation counterclockwise to the terminal arm. The reference angle \theta_R is the acute angle between the terminal arm and the x-axis. A quadrantal angle (0^\circ,\ 90^\circ,\ 180^\circ,\ 270^\circ) lies on an axis.
Point P(x,\,y) on the terminal arm:
r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}, \sin\theta=\dfrac{y}{r},\quad \cos\theta=\dfrac{x}{r},\quad \tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{x}\ \ (x\ne0).
Reflecting P(x,\,y) in the y-axis, the origin and the x-axis gives the angles 180^\circ-\theta, 180^\circ+\theta and 360^\circ-\theta (same \theta_R).

\theta_R=180^\circ-\theta

Choosing

Right triangle: SOH CAH TOA. A side and its opposite angle known (AAS, ASA after finding the third angle, SSA): the sine law. Otherwise (SAS, SSS): the cosine law. A negative cosine means an obtuse angle.
!
Good habits. Calculator in Degree mode. Sketch every problem and label sides with the lowercase letter of the opposite angle. Keep full calculator values between steps and round only the final answer. A bearing is measured clockwise from north, written with three digits (e.g. 062°). Common errors: \theta_R is measured to the x-axis, never the y-axis; the inverse key gives only one angle; in the cosine law, do the multiplication 2bc\cos A before subtracting.

Angles, Points and Ratios

From a point: drop a line to the x-axis to make the reference triangle. Its legs are |x| and |y|, its hypotenuse is r (always positive). The signs of x and y give the signs of the ratios; \tan^{-1}\left|\frac{y}{x}\right| gives \theta_R; the quadrant turns \theta_R into \theta.

Exact Values and Solving Equations

The inverse key gives one angle; there are usually two. Think of the unit circle: \sin\theta=a is a height (a horizontal line meets the circle twice), \cos\theta=a is a sideways distance (a vertical line), and \tan\theta=a is a slope (a line through the origin: opposite quadrants).

4

The Sine Law, the Cosine Law and the Ambiguous Case

Before any calculating, sort the triangle. Mark what is given. A right angle? Primary ratios. A full pair (a side and the angle opposite it)? The sine law; but if the given angle is not between the two given sides (SSA), first count the triangles. No full pair (SAS, SSS)? The cosine law.
Example 6

Sine law, cosine law and area

Sides to the nearest tenth, angles to the nearest degree.

(a) In \triangle PQR, \angle P=48^\circ, \angle Q=71^\circ and p=15.2 cm. Determine r.
\angle R=180^\circ-48^\circ-71^\circ=61^\circ. A full pair (p, \angle P) is known: sine law.
\dfrac{r}{\sin61^\circ}=\dfrac{15.2}{\sin48^\circ}\Rightarrow r=\dfrac{15.2\sin61^\circ}{\sin48^\circ}\approx\boldsymbol{17.9} cm
(b) A triangle has sides 7 m, 9 m and 13 m. Determine its largest angle.
SSS: cosine law. The largest angle is opposite the longest side (13 m).
\cos C=\dfrac{7^2+9^2-13^2}{2(7)(9)}=\dfrac{-39}{126}\approx-0.3095\Rightarrow C\approx\boldsymbol{108^\circ} (negative cosine: obtuse)
(c) Determine the area of the triangle in (b) to the nearest square metre.
\text{Area}=\dfrac{1}{2}(7)(9)\sin C\approx\dfrac{1}{2}(63)(0.9509)\approx\boldsymbol{30} m2 (using the unrounded C)
One line each, no rounding on the way. The TI-84 opens a bracket after sin: close it. For (c), 2ndANS keeps the unrounded C.
Example 7

Two triangles

In \triangle ABC, \angle A=38^\circ, a=9 cm and b=12 cm.

(a) Show that two triangles are possible.
h=b\sin A=12\sin38^\circ\approx7.39 cm. h<a<b (7.39<9<12), so there are two triangles.
(b) Solve both triangles (sides to the nearest tenth, angles to the nearest degree).
\sin B=\dfrac{12\sin38^\circ}{9}\approx0.8209\Rightarrow B\approx55^\circ or B\approx180^\circ-55^\circ=125^\circ
Triangle 1: C\approx180^\circ-38^\circ-55^\circ=87^\circ, c=\dfrac{9\sin87^\circ}{\sin38^\circ}\approx\boldsymbol{14.6} cm
Triangle 2: C\approx180^\circ-38^\circ-125^\circ=17^\circ, c=\dfrac{9\sin17^\circ}{\sin38^\circ}\approx\boldsymbol{4.3} cm
(c) How many triangles are possible if instead (i) a=7 cm, (ii) a=13 cm, (iii) a=12\sin38^\circ cm?
(i) 7<h: none. (ii) a\ge b: one. (iii) a=h: one (a right triangle, B=90^\circ).
The calculator finds only the acute B. The second one is 180^\circ-B: always check that it still leaves room for C (the three angles add to 180^\circ).
5

Problem Solving

Sketch it, then find a triangle with enough information to start. A side found in one triangle becomes a given in the next. For bearings, draw a north line at every turning point; the angle between two paths from one point is the difference of their bearings.
Example 8

A building and two boats

(a) Two points A and B on level ground are 120 m apart and in line with the base F of a building. From A the angle of elevation of the top T is 31^\circ; from B, closer to the building, it is 52^\circ. Determine the height of the building to the nearest tenth of a metre.
\triangle ABT: \angle ABT=180^\circ-52^\circ=128^\circ, \angle ATB=180^\circ-31^\circ-128^\circ=21^\circ.
Sine law: BT=\dfrac{120\sin31^\circ}{\sin21^\circ}\approx172.46 m
Right \triangle BFT: TF=BT\sin52^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{135.9} m
(b) Two boats leave a dock at the same time. One travels at 14 km/h on a bearing of 030^\circ; the other travels at 18 km/h on a bearing of 145^\circ. Sketch a diagram and determine how far apart they are after 1.5 h, to the nearest tenth of a kilometre.
Distances: 14(1.5)=21 km and 18(1.5)=27 km. Angle between the paths: 145^\circ-30^\circ=115^\circ.
SAS: cosine law.
d^2=21^2+27^2-2(21)(27)\cos115^\circ\approx1170+479.25=1649.25
d\approx\boldsymbol{40.6} km
Store, don’t round. Keep BT on the screen and use 2ndANS in the next step. In (b), \cos115^\circ is negative, so “minus” a negative adds.

Key ideas

Standard position. \theta_R is the acute angle to the x-axis: \theta, 180^\circ-\theta, \theta-180^\circ, 360^\circ-\theta. CAST gives the signs: All, Sin, Tan, Cos.
From a point. r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}, \sin\theta=\frac{y}{r}, \cos\theta=\frac{x}{r}, \tan\theta=\frac{y}{x}. Exact values come from the 1,1,\sqrt2 and 1,\sqrt3,2 triangles.
Solving. \theta_R from the inverse key with |a|, two quadrants from the sign and CAST, then place \theta_R. |a|>1 for sin or cos: no solution.
Which tool? Right angle: SOH CAH TOA. A full pair: sine law (SSA: compare a with h=b\sin A and b). SAS or SSS: cosine law. Area =\frac12ab\sin C.
