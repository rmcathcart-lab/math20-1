The CAST Rule: Signs and Solving for Angles

Every trig ratio has a size and a sign. The size comes from the reference angle; the sign comes from the quadrant. Put the two together and an equation like \sin\theta=0.6 has two answers from 0^\circ to 360^\circ: one in each quadrant where sine is positive.

I can…
  • use the CAST rule to tell whether \sin\theta, \cos\theta or \tan\theta is positive or negative in each quadrant
  • write a ratio of \theta as \pm the same ratio of its reference angle \textcolor{#c27a0e}{\theta_R}, and find the other angle with the same ratio
  • find the exact values of the other two ratios from one ratio and its quadrant
  • solve \sin\theta=a, \cos\theta=a and \tan\theta=a for 0^\circ\le\theta<360^\circ with the inverse keys
  • spot quadrantal solutions and equations with no solution, isolate the ratio first, and keep only the solutions in a given interval
T2 Solve problems, using the three primary trigonometric ratios for angles from 0° to 360° in standard position.
Today: the CAST rule, a ratio as ± the ratio of its reference angle, the other ratios from one ratio, and solving sin θ = a, cos θ = a and tan θ = a from 0° to 360°.
1

Signs of the Ratios: the CAST Rule

For a point P(x,\ y) on the terminal arm, \sin\theta=\dfrac{y}{r}, \cos\theta=\dfrac{x}{r} and \tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{x}, where r>0 always. So the sign of a ratio depends only on the signs of x and y:
  • \sin\theta has the sign of y (positive above the x-axis);
  • \cos\theta has the sign of x (positive right of the y-axis);
  • \tan\theta is positive when x and y have the same sign.

The diagram shows which ratios are positive in each quadrant. Read the letters counterclockwise from Quadrant IV: C, A, S, T, the CAST rule. (Memory aid from Quadrant I: All Students Take Calculus.)

The ratios not named in a quadrant are negative there. On the axes (quadrantal angles) a ratio is 0, 1, -1 or undefined, as in Lesson 2.

2

Writing a Ratio Using Its Reference Angle

The terminal arms of \theta and of its reference angle \textcolor{#c27a0e}{\theta_R} make the same right triangle with the x-axis, only with different signs on x and y. So a ratio of \theta has the same size as that ratio of \textcolor{#c27a0e}{\theta_R}, and CAST gives the sign.

Ratio of \theta =\ \pm (same ratio of \textcolor{#c27a0e}{\theta_R}), with the sign from CAST for the quadrant of \theta.
Example: 150^\circ is in Quadrant II with \textcolor{#c27a0e}{\theta_R=30^\circ}, so \sin 150^\circ=\sin 30^\circ, \cos 150^\circ=-\cos 30^\circ, \tan 150^\circ=-\tan 30^\circ.

This also finds the other angle from 0^\circ to 360^\circ with the same ratio: it has the same reference angle and lies in the other quadrant where that ratio has the same sign.

Example 2

Same size, CAST sign

Write each ratio as the same ratio of its reference angle, with the correct sign.

(a) \cos 132^\circ
Quadrant II, \theta_R=180^\circ-132^\circ=48^\circ; cos is negative in II.
\boldsymbol{\cos 132^\circ=-\cos 48^\circ}
(b) \sin 214^\circ
Quadrant III, \theta_R=214^\circ-180^\circ=34^\circ; sin is negative.
\boldsymbol{\sin 214^\circ=-\sin 34^\circ}
(c) \tan 297^\circ
Quadrant IV, \theta_R=360^\circ-297^\circ=63^\circ; tan is negative.
\boldsymbol{\tan 297^\circ=-\tan 63^\circ}
(d) \sin 349^\circ
Quadrant IV, \theta_R=11^\circ; sin is negative.
\boldsymbol{\sin 349^\circ=-\sin 11^\circ}
(e) Determine the other angle \theta, 0^\circ\le\theta<360^\circ, with \cos\theta=\cos 132^\circ.
\cos 132^\circ is negative, so \theta is in Quadrant II or III. The reference angle is 48^\circ.
Quadrant III: \theta=180^\circ+48^\circ=\boldsymbol{228^\circ}.
(f) Determine the other angle \theta, 0^\circ\le\theta<360^\circ, with \tan\theta=\tan 128^\circ.
\tan 128^\circ is negative (Quadrant II), so the other angle is in Quadrant IV. \theta_R=52^\circ.
\theta=360^\circ-52^\circ=\boldsymbol{308^\circ}.

3

Finding the Other Ratios from One Ratio

If one ratio and enough sign information are given, the other two ratios can be found exactly:

  1. Use the signs (CAST) to decide the quadrant.
  2. Read two of x, y, r from the given ratio, with the signs for that quadrant (r is always positive).
  3. Find the third with x^2+y^2=r^2, and give it the sign for the quadrant.
  4. Sketch the angle and its reference triangle, then write the ratios. Rationalize any radical denominator.
Example 3

Two clues, one quadrant

Given \cos\theta=-\dfrac{8}{17} and \tan\theta>0, determine the exact values of \sin\theta and \tan\theta.

\cos\theta<0 and \tan\theta>0: Quadrant III (only T positive).
\cos\theta=\dfrac{x}{r}=\dfrac{-8}{17}: x=-8, r=17.
y^2=r^2-x^2=289-64=225, so y=-15 (negative in Quadrant III). Point P(-8,\ -15).
\boldsymbol{\sin\theta=\dfrac{y}{r}=-\dfrac{15}{17}}    \boldsymbol{\tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{x}=\dfrac{-15}{-8}=\dfrac{15}{8}}
Example 4

Exact values with radicals

Determine the exact values of the other two ratios. Rationalize the denominators.

(a) \tan\theta=\dfrac{2}{5} and \sin\theta<0
\tan\theta>0 and \sin\theta<0: Quadrant III.
\tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{x}=\dfrac{-2}{-5}: x=-5, y=-2.
r=\sqrt{(-5)^2+(-2)^2}=\sqrt{29}
\boldsymbol{\sin\theta=\dfrac{-2}{\sqrt{29}}=-\dfrac{2\sqrt{29}}{29}}
\boldsymbol{\cos\theta=\dfrac{-5}{\sqrt{29}}=-\dfrac{5\sqrt{29}}{29}}
(b) \sin\theta=\dfrac{2}{3} and 90^\circ<\theta<180^\circ
Quadrant II: y=2, r=3, x<0.
x^2=9-4=5, so x=-\sqrt{5}.
\boldsymbol{\cos\theta=\dfrac{x}{r}=-\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}}
\boldsymbol{\tan\theta=\dfrac{2}{-\sqrt{5}}=-\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}}=-\dfrac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}}
!
r never gets a minus sign. It is a distance. The minus signs belong to x and y, and the quadrant decides which ones.

Solving \sin\theta=a, \cos\theta=a and \tan\theta=a

An equation like \sin\theta=0.42 usually has two solutions from 0^\circ to 360^\circ, because the ratio has the same sign in two quadrants. Set the calculator to Degree mode first (TI-84: MODE, choose DEGREE).

To solve \sin\theta=a, \cos\theta=a or \tan\theta=a for 0^\circ\le\theta<360^\circ:
  1. Use the sign of a and CAST to find the two quadrants.
  2. Find the reference angle with the inverse key on the positive value: \textcolor{#c27a0e}{\theta_R}=\sin^{-1}|a| (2ndSIN), \cos^{-1}|a| or \tan^{-1}|a|.
  3. Place \textcolor{#c27a0e}{\theta_R} in each quadrant:   I: \theta_R  ·  II: 180^\circ-\theta_R  ·  III: 180^\circ+\theta_R  ·  IV: 360^\circ-\theta_R.

Use |a| in step 2 so the calculator always returns an acute angle; \sin^{-1}(-0.42) gives -24.8^\circ, which is not in the interval. Keep the full calculator value and round only the final answers.

Example 5

Solve for \theta, 0^\circ\le\theta<360^\circ, to the nearest tenth of a degree.

I: \boldsymbol{\theta\approx24.8^\circ}

(b) \cos\theta=-0.73

II: 180^\circ-43.11^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{136.9^\circ}
III: 180^\circ+43.11^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{223.1^\circ}
(c) \tan\theta=-3.5

\theta_R=\tan^{-1}(3.5)\approx74.05^\circ
II: 180^\circ-74.05^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{105.9^\circ}
IV: 360^\circ-74.05^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{285.9^\circ}

5

Quadrantal Solutions and Equations with No Solution

Since \sin\theta=\dfrac{y}{r} and \cos\theta=\dfrac{x}{r} with |x|\le r and |y|\le r, the values of sine and cosine are always between -1 and 1. So \sin\theta=a and \cos\theta=a have no solution when a>1 or a<-1. Tangent can be any real number, so \tan\theta=a always has two solutions.

When a is 0, 1 or -1, the solutions are quadrantal angles, and there may be only one. Use the values from Lesson 2:

\theta0^\circ90^\circ180^\circ270^\circ
\sin\theta010-1
\cos\theta10-10
\tan\theta0undefined0undefined
Example 6

One, two or none

Solve for \theta, 0^\circ\le\theta<360^\circ.

(a) \sin\theta=-1
y=-r: the point is on the negative y-axis.
\boldsymbol{\theta=270^\circ} (one solution)
(b) \cos\theta=0
x=0: on the y-axis.
\boldsymbol{\theta=90^\circ} or \boldsymbol{270^\circ}
(c) \tan\theta=0
y=0: on the x-axis.
\boldsymbol{\theta=0^\circ} or \boldsymbol{180^\circ}
(d) \cos\theta=-1.15
-1.15<-1, and cosine is never less than -1. No solution.

6

Rearranging First, Restricted Intervals and Problems

If the ratio is not alone, isolate it first, exactly as you would isolate x in a linear equation. If the question gives a smaller interval (such as 0^\circ\le\theta\le180^\circ), find all the solutions, then keep only those in the interval.

Example 7

Isolate, then solve

Solve, to the nearest tenth of a degree.

(a) 5\sin\theta+2=0, 0^\circ\le\theta<360^\circ
5\sin\theta=-2, so \sin\theta=-0.4.
Negative sin: III, IV.
\theta_R=\sin^{-1}(0.4)\approx23.58^\circ
\theta\approx180^\circ+23.58^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{203.6^\circ}
\theta\approx360^\circ-23.58^\circ\approx\boldsymbol{336.4^\circ}
(b) 3(2-\cos\theta)=4, 0^\circ\le\theta<360^\circ
6-3\cos\theta=4, so -3\cos\theta=-2 and \cos\theta=\dfrac{2}{3}.
Positive cos: I, IV.
\theta_R=\cos^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)\approx48.19^\circ
\boldsymbol{\theta\approx48.2^\circ} or \boldsymbol{311.8^\circ}
(c) 3\tan\theta+8=0, 0^\circ\le\theta\le180^\circ
\tan\theta=-\dfrac{8}{3}. Negative tan: II, IV.
\theta_R=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{8}{3}\right)\approx69.44^\circ
II: \theta\approx110.6^\circ   IV: \theta\approx290.6^\circ
Only \boldsymbol{110.6^\circ} is in 0^\circ\le\theta\le180^\circ.
Fractions go straight in. 2ndcos⁻¹2÷3)ENTER gives 48.1896… and 2ndtan⁻¹8÷3)ENTER gives 69.4439…: no rounding before the last step.

A problem in context: the bicycle pedal

When a model like h=17\sin\theta gives a height, “at what angles?” is an equation to solve: put in the height, isolate \sin\theta, then use CAST.

Example 8

The bicycle pedal

A bicycle pedal turns about its axle. When the crank arm has rotated \theta from the horizontal (in standard position), the height of the pedal above the axle is h=17\sin\theta, in centimetres. A negative height means the pedal is below the axle.

(a) For 0^\circ\le\theta<360^\circ, at what angles is the pedal 6 cm below the axle? Answer to the nearest tenth of a degree.
17\sin\theta=-6, so \sin\theta=-\dfrac{6}{17}.
Negative sin: III, IV. \theta_R=\sin^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{6}{17}\right)\approx20.67^\circ
\boldsymbol{\theta\approx200.7^\circ} or \boldsymbol{339.3^\circ}
(b) Can the pedal ever be 20 cm above the axle? Explain using the equation.
17\sin\theta=20 gives \sin\theta=\dfrac{20}{17}>1.
Sine is never greater than 1, so there is no solution: the pedal is never more than 17 cm (the crank length) from the axle.
Reference angle in one line: 2ndsin⁻¹6÷17)ENTER gives 20.6673…, then 180+2ndAnsENTER gives 200.667…

Key ideas

CAST. Positive ratios: I All, II Sin, III Tan, IV Cos. The others are negative. r>0 always, so the signs of x and y decide.
Same size, CAST sign. Ratio of \theta =\pm same ratio of \theta_R. The other angle with the same ratio has the same \theta_R, in the other quadrant with that sign.
Other ratios from one. Quadrant from the clues → two of x, y, r with signs → the third from x^2+y^2=r^2 → ratios (rationalize).
Solving. Isolate the ratio. Sign → two quadrants. \theta_R=\sin^{-1}|a| (or cos, tan). Place it: \theta_R, 180^\circ-\theta_R, 180^\circ+\theta_R, 360^\circ-\theta_R. |a|>1 for sin or cos: no solution. 0,\pm1: quadrantal.
