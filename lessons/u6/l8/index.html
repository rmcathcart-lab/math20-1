The Proof That 2 = 1

Here is a “proof” that 2 = 1. Every step looks legal.

  1. 1a=btwo equal numbers
  2. 2a^2=abmultiply both sides by a
  3. 3a^2-b^2=ab-b^2subtract b²
  4. 4(a+b)(a-b)=b(a-b)factor both sides
  5. 5a+b=bdivide both sides by a − b
  6. 6b+b=breplace a with b
  7. 72b=bcollect like terms
  8. 82=1divide both sides by b

But 2 is not 1. Which step is the mistake?

?
Point to the step, then say why.
Step 5. Since a=b, the number a-b is 0. Step 4 is true (0=0), but “dividing both sides by a-b” divides by 0, which is never allowed. Cancel a factor that is 0 and you can “prove” anything.

When the LCD Gives a Quadratic Equation

Often, after multiplying by the LCD, the x^2 terms do not cancel and the result is a quadratic equation. Write it in standard form ax^2+bx+c=0, then solve it by factoring (or by the quadratic formula). A quadratic gives up to two roots, and each one must be compared with the NPVs and checked.

Key idea
1Factor the denominators; state the NPVs.
2Multiply every term by the LCD.
3Write the quadratic in standard form.
4Solve by factoring or the quadratic formula.
5Reject any root that is an NPV.
6Verify the remaining roots.
Example 1

State the NPVs, solve and verify

State the NPVs, solve and verify.

(a) x+\dfrac{6}{x}=5
x\ne0. Multiply by x:
x^2+6=5x
x^2-5x+6=0
(x-2)(x-3)=0
\boldsymbol{x=2} or \boldsymbol{x=3}
Verify x=2: 2+3=5 ✓
Verify x=3: 3+2=5 ✓
(b) \dfrac{x}{x-2}=\dfrac{8}{x}
x\ne2,\ 0
x^2=8(x-2)
x^2-8x+16=0
(x-4)^2=0
\boldsymbol{x=4} (a double root)
Verify: LS =\tfrac{4}{2}=2, RS =\tfrac{8}{4}=2 ✓
(c) \dfrac{3}{x}+\dfrac{x}{x+2}=2
x\ne0,\ -2. LCD x(x+2)
3(x+2)+x^2=2x(x+2)
x^2+3x+6=2x^2+4x
0=x^2+x-6
0=(x+3)(x-2)
\boldsymbol{x=-3} or \boldsymbol{x=2}
x=-3: -1+\tfrac{-3}{-1}=2 ✓
x=2: \tfrac{3}{2}+\tfrac{2}{4}=2 ✓
2

Rejecting a Non-Permissible Root

When one root of the quadratic is an NPV, it is extraneous: it solves the polynomial equation but makes a denominator of the original equation 0. Reject it and keep the other root. If both roots are rejected, the equation has no solution.

Example 2

An extra root from cross-multiplying

Consider the equation \dfrac{x^2-2x-15}{x-5}=2x.

(a) State the NPV and solve by cross-multiplying.
x\ne5
x^2-2x-15=2x(x-5)
x^2-2x-15=2x^2-10x
0=x^2-8x+15=(x-3)(x-5)
x=3 or x=5. Reject x=5 (NPV). The solution is \boldsymbol{x=3}.
(b) Explain why x=5 does not satisfy the original equation, even though it solves the quadratic.
At x=5 the left side is \tfrac{0}{0}, which is undefined. Cross-multiplying multiplied both sides by x-5, which is 0 when x=5, so it created this extra root.
(Another view: for x\ne5, the left side simplifies to \tfrac{(x-5)(x+3)}{x-5}=x+3, and x+3=2x gives only x=3.)

Why the extra root appears: the graphs

Multiplying both sides by the LCD also multiplies the gap between the two sides by the LCD. At an NPV the LCD is 0, so the gap becomes 0 there: the new equation has a meeting point that the original equation never had.

Example 3

State the NPVs and solve

State the NPVs and solve.

(a) \dfrac{x}{x-3}-\dfrac{2}{x+1}=\dfrac{8}{x^2-2x-3}
x^2-2x-3=(x-3)(x+1): x\ne3,\ -1
x(x+1)-2(x-3)=8
x^2+x-2x+6=8
x^2-x-2=0
(x-2)(x+1)=0
x=2 or x=-1. Reject x=-1 (NPV).
The solution is \boldsymbol{x=2}.
(b) \dfrac{x+1}{x-4}=\dfrac{5}{4-x}+x
x\ne4. \tfrac{5}{4-x}=-\tfrac{5}{x-4}; multiply by x-4:
x+1=-5+x(x-4)
x+1=-5+x^2-4x
0=x^2-5x-6=(x-6)(x+1)
\boldsymbol{x=6} or \boldsymbol{x=-1} (both permissible)
x=6: LS =\tfrac{7}{2}, RS =-\tfrac{5}{2}+6=\tfrac{7}{2} ✓
x=-1: LS =0, RS =1-1=0 ✓
Example 4

Both roots rejected

Show that \dfrac{x}{x-3}-\dfrac{3}{x}=\dfrac{9}{x^2-3x} has no solution.

x^2-3x=x(x-3): NPVs x\ne0,\ 3. LCD x(x-3)
x^2-3(x-3)=9\Rightarrow x^2-3x+9=9\Rightarrow x^2-3x=0\Rightarrow x(x-3)=0
x=0 or x=3: both are NPVs and are rejected. The equation has no solution.

Using the Quadratic Formula

If the quadratic does not factor, use the quadratic formula. Give exact roots in simplest radical form unless the question asks for a rounded answer. A graphing calculator (graph both sides and use intersect) confirms the decimal values.

Key idea
For ax^2+bx+c=0: x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}

If b^2-4ac<0, there are no real roots, so the rational equation has no real solution.

Confirm the decimals: graph both sides and intersect. Example 5(b): Y_1= 1/(X−1)+1/(X+1), Y_2=1. Brackets around each denominator. Then 2ndTRACE 5: intersect, ENTERENTER, and type a guess near the crossing before the last ENTER.
Example 5

Exact, then rounded

Solve. Give exact answers, then round to the nearest hundredth.

(a) \dfrac{2}{x}+1=\dfrac{x}{3} (solved graphically in Lesson 7)
x\ne0. LCD 3x:
6+3x=x^2
x^2-3x-6=0
x=\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt{9-4(1)(-6)}}{2}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt{33}}{2}}
x\approx\boldsymbol{4.37} or x\approx\boldsymbol{-1.37} (matches the graph)
(b) \dfrac{1}{x-1}+\dfrac{1}{x+1}=1
x\ne1,\ -1. LCD (x-1)(x+1):
(x+1)+(x-1)=x^2-1
x^2-2x-1=0
x=\dfrac{2\pm\sqrt{4+4}}{2}=\dfrac{2\pm2\sqrt{2}}{2}=\boldsymbol{1\pm\sqrt{2}}
x\approx\boldsymbol{2.41} or x\approx\boldsymbol{-0.41}

4

Rational Equations in Formulas

Many science formulas contain reciprocals. When two resistors R_1 and R_2 (in ohms, \Omega) are connected in parallel, the combined resistance R satisfies

\dfrac{1}{R}=\dfrac{1}{R_1}+\dfrac{1}{R_2}

Substituting the given information often gives a rational equation that leads to a quadratic. Reject a root that is not reasonable (a resistance is positive).

Example 7

Resistors in parallel

Two resistors are connected in parallel. One has a resistance 5\ \Omega greater than the other, and their combined resistance is 6\ \Omega. Determine the resistance of each.

Let the smaller resistance be R; the other is R+5.
\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{1}{R}+\dfrac{1}{R+5} (R\ne0,\ -5). LCD 6R(R+5):
R(R+5)=6(R+5)+6R
R^2+5R=12R+30\Rightarrow R^2-7R-30=0\Rightarrow(R-10)(R+3)=0
R=10 or R=-3 (reject: a resistance is positive). The resistances are 10\ \Omega and 15\ \Omega.
Check: \tfrac{1}{10}+\tfrac{1}{15}=\tfrac{3+2}{30}=\tfrac{1}{6} ✓
✓

Key ideas

Clear the denominators. Factor them and state the NPVs first. Multiply every term by the LCD, then write the quadratic in standard form ax^2+bx+c=0.
Factor, or use the formula. Find the two numbers with the X. If it won't factor: x=\tfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}. If b^2-4ac<0, there is no real solution.
Every root gets a verdict. A root that is an NPV is extraneous: reject it. It appears because the LCD is 0 there. One root, both, or neither can survive (both rejected: no solution).
Exact, then rounded; does it make sense? Simplest radical form first (1\pm\sqrt{2}), then decimals. In a formula problem, reject a root that is not reasonable: a resistance is positive.
