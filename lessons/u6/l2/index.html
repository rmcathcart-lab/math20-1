Sam’s Shortcut

Sam “simplifies” \dfrac{16}{64} by crossing out the two 6s, and gets \dfrac{1}{4}.

Is Sam’s answer right?

?
Check it. Then hunt for another fraction where crossing out a digit works.
Yes, \frac{16}{64} really is \frac{1}{4} (16\times4=64), but only by luck! Crossing out digits is not a real rule: crossing out the 2s in \frac{12}{24} also gives \frac{1}{4}, but \frac{12}{24}=\frac{1}{2}.
With two-digit numbers (a fraction less than 1, no zeros) the trick works only four times: \frac{16}{64}=\frac{1}{4}, \frac{19}{95}=\frac{1}{5}, \frac{26}{65}=\frac{2}{5} and \frac{49}{98}=\frac{4}{8}.

Simplifying Rational Expressions by Factoring

A rational expression is simplified the way a fraction is: factor the top and the bottom, then divide out the common factors. The non-permissible values are stated first, from the original denominator, and they stay with the answer.

Equivalent Forms and Simplest Form

A fraction is simplified by dividing out a common factor: \dfrac{18}{24}=\dfrac{3\cdot6}{4\cdot6}=\dfrac{3}{4}. Rational expressions work the same way. Two expressions are equivalent if they have the same value for every permissible value of the variable. An expression is in simplest form when its numerator and denominator have no common factor other than 1 (or -1).

To simplify a rational expression
  1. Factor the numerator and the denominator fully.
  2. State the NPVs from the original denominator.
  3. Divide out the common factors.
  4. Write the result with its NPVs.
The simplified expression keeps the NPVs of the original, even when the factor that caused them is gone.

With monomials, use the exponent laws: \dfrac{x^5}{x^2}=x^3.

Example 1

Common factors

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{18x^5}{24x^2}
NPV: x\ne0
\dfrac{6x^2\cdot3x^3}{6x^2\cdot4}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3x^3}{4}},\ x\ne0
(b) \dfrac{-10a^2b}{35ab^3}
NPVs: a\ne0,\ b\ne0
\dfrac{5ab\cdot(-2a)}{5ab\cdot7b^2}=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{2a}{7b^2}},\ a\ne0,\ b\ne0
(c) \dfrac{6(x-2)}{9(x-2)(x+1)}
NPVs: x\ne2,\ -1
\dfrac{3(x-2)\cdot2}{3(x-2)\cdot3(x+1)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2}{3(x+1)}},\ x\ne2,\ -1
(d) \dfrac{(m+4)^2}{(m+4)(m-3)}
NPVs: m\ne-4,\ 3
\boldsymbol{\dfrac{m+4}{m-3}},\ m\ne-4,\ 3

Divide Out Factors, Not Terms

Only a factor of the whole numerator and of the whole denominator can be divided out. A term (a part joined by + or -) cannot. For example, \dfrac{x+6}{x+2} is not \dfrac{6}{2}=3: try x=2, which gives \dfrac{8}{4}=2, not 3. To check a simplification, substitute a permissible value into the original and the result: they must agree.
Check on the calculator. Store a test value in X: 3STO→X,T,θ,nENTER. Then type the original and your answer with X,T,θ,n for x: (x^2-25)\div(x-5) gives 8 and x+5 gives 8 ✓. Same number, so the simplification agrees at x=3.
Example 2

Find the error

Each simplification is wrong. Explain the error and give the correct simplest form with its NPVs.

(a) \dfrac{x+8}{4}=x+2
4 is not a factor of the whole numerator; only the term 8 was divided. x+8 and 4 have no common factor, so \boldsymbol{\dfrac{x+8}{4}} is already in simplest form (no NPVs).
(b) \dfrac{3x+12}{3}=x+12
3 was divided out of only one term. Factor first: \dfrac{3(x+4)}{3}=\boldsymbol{x+4} (no NPVs).
(c) \dfrac{x^2-25}{x-5}=x-5  (the squares were “cancelled”)
x^2 and 25 are terms, not factors. \dfrac{(x-5)(x+5)}{x-5}=\boldsymbol{x+5},\ x\ne5
(d) \dfrac{x^2+4x}{x}=x+4
The working is right but the NPV is missing. \dfrac{x(x+4)}{x}=\boldsymbol{x+4},\ x\ne0

Example 3

Factor, then divide out

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{4x-20}{x^2-25}
\dfrac{4(x-5)}{(x-5)(x+5)}; NPVs x\ne\pm5
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{4}{x+5}},\ x\ne\pm5
(b) \dfrac{x^2+2x-24}{x^2-7x+12}
\dfrac{(x+6)(x-4)}{(x-3)(x-4)}; NPVs x\ne3,\ 4
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x+6}{x-3}},\ x\ne3,\ 4
(c) \dfrac{3y^2-12y}{6y^2-18y-24}
\dfrac{3y(y-4)}{6(y^2-3y-4)}=\dfrac{3y(y-4)}{6(y-4)(y+1)}
NPVs y\ne4,\ -1
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{y}{2(y+1)}},\ y\ne4,\ -1
(d) \dfrac{2x^2+7x-15}{4x^2-9}
ac=-30: 10 and -3. 2x^2+10x-3x-15=(2x-3)(x+5)
\dfrac{(2x-3)(x+5)}{(2x-3)(2x+3)}; NPVs x\ne\pm\frac{3}{2}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x+5}{2x+3}},\ x\ne\pm\frac{3}{2}
Example 4

Two trinomials, and a common factor first

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{6a^2+13a+6}{3a^2-4a-4}
\dfrac{(2a+3)(3a+2)}{(3a+2)(a-2)}; NPVs a\ne-\frac{2}{3},\ 2
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2a+3}{a-2}},\ a\ne-\frac{2}{3},\ 2
(b) \dfrac{x^3-4x}{x^3+x^2-6x}
\dfrac{x(x-2)(x+2)}{x(x+3)(x-2)}; NPVs x\ne0,\ 2,\ -3
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x+2}{x+3}},\ x\ne0,\ 2,\ -3

Opposite Binomials

a-b and b-a are opposites: b-a=-(a-b). For example, 7-x=-(x-7). So a quotient of opposites is -1. To use this, factor -1 out of one of them (also out of a trinomial with a negative x^2 term: 12+x-x^2=-(x^2-x-12)).

\dfrac{a-b}{b-a}=\dfrac{a-b}{-(a-b)}=-1,\ \ a\ne b
But \dfrac{a+b}{b+a}=1: 3+x and x+3 are the same, not opposites.
Example 5

Opposites

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{x-7}{7-x}
NPV x\ne7
\dfrac{x-7}{-(x-7)}=\boldsymbol{-1},\ x\ne7
(b) \dfrac{10-5a}{a^2-4}
\dfrac{5(2-a)}{(a-2)(a+2)}=\dfrac{-5(a-2)}{(a-2)(a+2)}; NPVs a\ne\pm2
=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{5}{a+2}},\ a\ne\pm2
(c) \dfrac{9-x^2}{x^2+x-12}
\dfrac{(3-x)(3+x)}{(x+4)(x-3)}=\dfrac{-(x-3)(x+3)}{(x+4)(x-3)}
NPVs x\ne-4,\ 3
=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{x+3}{x+4}},\ x\ne-4,\ 3
(d) \dfrac{12+x-x^2}{2x^2-7x-4}
\dfrac{-(x^2-x-12)}{(2x+1)(x-4)}=\dfrac{-(x-4)(x+3)}{(2x+1)(x-4)}
NPVs x\ne-\frac{1}{2},\ 4
=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{x+3}{2x+1}},\ x\ne-\frac{1}{2},\ 4

Two Variables and Equivalent Forms

With two variables, factor in the same way and state the NPVs for the variable asked for (for example, x in terms of y). To decide whether two expressions are equivalent, simplify both, or rewrite one to match the other. To write an equivalent expression with a new denominator, multiply the numerator and the denominator by the same factor.
Example 6

Two variables

Simplify, and state the non-permissible values for x in terms of y.

(a) \dfrac{x^2-9y^2}{2x+6y}
\dfrac{(x-3y)(x+3y)}{2(x+3y)}
NPV x\ne-3y
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x-3y}{2}},\ x\ne-3y
(b) \dfrac{x^2-xy-6y^2}{3y-x}
\dfrac{(x-3y)(x+2y)}{-(x-3y)}
NPV x\ne3y
=\boldsymbol{-(x+2y)},\ x\ne3y
(c) \dfrac{2x^2+5xy-3y^2}{4x^2-y^2}
\dfrac{(2x-y)(x+3y)}{(2x-y)(2x+y)}
NPVs x\ne\pm\frac{y}{2}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x+3y}{2x+y}},\ x\ne\pm\frac{y}{2}
Example 7

Equivalent or not?

Is each expression equivalent to \dfrac{x-3}{x+2} (for its permissible values)? Explain.

(a) \dfrac{x^2-9}{x^2+5x+6}
\dfrac{(x-3)(x+3)}{(x+2)(x+3)}=\dfrac{x-3}{x+2}
Yes, for x\ne-2,\ -3.
(b) \dfrac{3-x}{-x-2}
\dfrac{-(x-3)}{-(x+2)}=\dfrac{x-3}{x+2}
Yes, for x\ne-2.
(c) \dfrac{3-x}{x+2}
No: only the numerator is the opposite, so it is -\dfrac{x-3}{x+2}. (x=0: \frac{3}{2} vs -\frac{3}{2}.)
(d) Determine the missing numerator: \dfrac{x-3}{x+2}=\dfrac{?}{x^2-4}
x^2-4=(x+2)(x-2), so multiply the numerator and denominator by (x-2):
?=(x-3)(x-2)=\boldsymbol{x^2-5x+6},\ x\ne\pm2
(e) \dfrac{x^2+kx-15}{x^2-9} simplifies to \dfrac{x+5}{x+3}. Determine k.
x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3). The factor (x-3) was divided out, so the numerator is (x+5)(x-3)=x^2+2x-15.  \boldsymbol{k=2}

Rational Expressions in Geometry

A missing length is often a quotient: \text{length}=\dfrac{\text{area}}{\text{width}}. Simplify the quotient by factoring, then use it. In a context, the variable must also keep every length positive.
Example 8

The garden

A rectangular garden has an area of (2x^2+9x-5)\text{ m}^2 and a length of (x+5) m.

(a) Write and simplify an expression for its width.
Width =\dfrac{2x^2+9x-5}{x+5}
=\dfrac{(2x-1)(x+5)}{x+5}
=\boldsymbol{2x-1} m
(x\ne-5; in context x>\frac{1}{2})
(b) For which value of x is the garden a square? State its side length.
x+5=2x-1\Rightarrow \boldsymbol{x=6}. The side is 11 m (check: 2(36)+54-5=121=11^2).
(c) Determine the exact perimeter and area when x=3+\sqrt{2}.
Length =8+\sqrt2
Width =2(3+\sqrt2)-1=5+2\sqrt2
Perimeter =2(13+3\sqrt2)
=\boldsymbol{26+6\sqrt2} m
Area =(8+\sqrt2)(5+2\sqrt2)
=40+16\sqrt2+5\sqrt2+4
=\boldsymbol{44+21\sqrt2}\text{ m}^2
Example 9

The box

A box (rectangular prism) has a square base of side x cm and a height of (x+3) cm.

(a) Write the surface area and the volume in factored form.
SA=2x^2+4x(x+3)
=6x^2+12x
=\boldsymbol{6x(x+2)}\text{ cm}^2
V=\boldsymbol{x^2(x+3)}\text{ cm}^3
(b) Write the ratio \dfrac{SA}{V} in simplest form and state the NPVs.
\dfrac{6x(x+2)}{x^2(x+3)}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{6(x+2)}{x(x+3)}},\ x\ne0,\ -3
(in context x>0)
(c) Evaluate the ratio for x=3 and x=6. What do you notice?
x=3: \dfrac{6(5)}{3(6)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{5}{3}}
x=6: \dfrac{6(8)}{6(9)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{8}{9}}
The larger box has less surface area per unit of volume.
✓

Key ideas

Four steps. Factor the numerator and the denominator fully; state the NPVs from the original denominator; divide out the common factors; write the result with its NPVs.
Factors, not terms. Only a factor of the whole numerator and the whole denominator can be divided out: \dfrac{x+6}{x+2}\ne3. Check by substituting a permissible value into the original and the result.
Opposites. \dfrac{a-b}{b-a}=-1: factor -1 out of one of them. But \dfrac{a+b}{b+a}=1. Two sign flips cancel; one flip gives the opposite.
Equivalent forms. Simplify both, or multiply the numerator and denominator by the same factor. In geometry, length =\dfrac{\text{area}}{\text{width}}, and every length must be positive.
