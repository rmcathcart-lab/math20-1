The 17 Camels

A farmer leaves 17 camels: \tfrac{1}{2} to one child, \tfrac{1}{3} to another and \tfrac{1}{9} to the third.

A traveller lends them her camel. From the 18, they take 9, 6 and 2, and she rides off on her own camel.

Everyone got their share. Where did the extra camel come from?

?
Did each child get exactly the share in the will?
The farmer never gave away the whole herd! \tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{3}+\tfrac{1}{9}=\tfrac{9}{18}+\tfrac{6}{18}+\tfrac{2}{18}=\tfrac{17}{18}, so \tfrac{1}{18} of the herd was left to nobody. With 18 camels, \tfrac{1}{18} of the herd is exactly one camel: the borrowed one goes home. And every child got a little more than the will said: 9>8.5, 6>5.\overline{6}, 2>1.\overline{8}.

Adding and Subtracting with Binomial and Trinomial Denominators

Same method as Lesson 5, bigger denominators: factor every denominator, build the LCD from the factors, give each numerator its missing factor, combine, and factor the numerator to see whether anything cancels. When a whole product is subtracted, the minus sign reaches every term.

Binomial Denominators with No Common Factor

The method is the same as for monomial denominators. When the denominators have no common factor, the LCD is their product. Leave the LCD in factored form: it shows the NPVs and makes the final check for common factors easy.
Key idea
\dfrac{a}{x+p}+\dfrac{b}{x+q}=\dfrac{a\textcolor{#c27a0e}{(x+q)}+b\textcolor{#c27a0e}{(x+p)}}{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{(x+p)(x+q)}}Expand the numerator, collect like terms, then factor it to see whether anything cancels.
Example 1

No common factor

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{3}{x+2}+\dfrac{5}{x-4}
x\ne-2,\ 4
\dfrac{3(x-4)+5(x+2)}{(x+2)(x-4)}
=\dfrac{3x-12+5x+10}{(x+2)(x-4)}
=\dfrac{8x-2}{(x+2)(x-4)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2(4x-1)}{(x+2)(x-4)}}
(b) \dfrac{4}{a-3}-\dfrac{1}{2a+1}
a\ne3,\ -\tfrac{1}{2}
\dfrac{4(2a+1)-(a-3)}{(a-3)(2a+1)}
=\dfrac{8a+4-a+3}{(a-3)(2a+1)}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{7(a+1)}{(a-3)(2a+1)}}
(c) \dfrac{2}{x}-\dfrac{3}{x+5}
x\ne0,\ -5
\dfrac{2(x+5)-3x}{x(x+5)}
=\dfrac{2x+10-3x}{x(x+5)}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{10-x}{x(x+5)}}

Binomial numerators: the minus sign meets the bracket

With binomial numerators, each product is a binomial times a binomial. Expand the product being subtracted inside brackets first, then remove the brackets, changing every sign.

Example 2

Binomial numerators

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{x+1}{x-2}-\dfrac{x-3}{x+4}
x\ne2,\ -4
\dfrac{(x+1)(x+4)-(x-3)(x-2)}{(x-2)(x+4)}
=\dfrac{x^2+5x+4-(x^2-5x+6)}{(x-2)(x+4)}
=\dfrac{10x-2}{(x-2)(x+4)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2(5x-1)}{(x-2)(x+4)}}
(b) \dfrac{2y}{y+3}+\dfrac{y-1}{y-2}
y\ne-3,\ 2
\dfrac{2y(y-2)+(y-1)(y+3)}{(y+3)(y-2)}
=\dfrac{2y^2-4y+y^2+2y-3}{(y+3)(y-2)}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3y^2-2y-3}{(y+3)(y-2)}} (does not factor)
(c) 2-\dfrac{x+1}{x+3}
x\ne-3
\dfrac{2(x+3)-(x+1)}{x+3}
=\dfrac{2x+6-x-1}{x+3}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x+5}{x+3}}

Factor First: Denominators with a Common Factor

If the denominators share a factor, multiplying them gives a common denominator that is too big, and the answer then needs extra simplifying. Factor every denominator first.
LCD of polynomials
Factor each denominator fully. The LCD is the product of every different factor, each used the greatest number of times it appears in any one denominator.   e.g. x^2-4x=x(x-4) and x:  LCD =x(x-4).

Always factor the numerator of the result. It may share a factor with the denominator, and the answer is not in simplest form until that factor is cancelled.

Example 3

Factor the denominators first

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{5}{2x+6}-\dfrac{1}{x+3}
\dfrac{5}{2(x+3)}-\dfrac{1}{x+3};  x\ne-3
LCD 2(x+3)
\dfrac{5-2}{2(x+3)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3}{2(x+3)}}
(b) \dfrac{3}{x^2-4x}+\dfrac{2}{x}
\dfrac{3}{x(x-4)}+\dfrac{2}{x};  x\ne0,\ 4
LCD x(x-4)
\dfrac{3+2(x-4)}{x(x-4)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2x-5}{x(x-4)}}
(c) \dfrac{4}{3a-6}-\dfrac{5}{4a-8}
\dfrac{4}{3(a-2)}-\dfrac{5}{4(a-2)};  a\ne2
LCD 12(a-2)
\dfrac{16-15}{12(a-2)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{1}{12(a-2)}}
Example 4

Answers that reduce further

Simplify fully. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{2}{x-1}-\dfrac{4}{x^2-1}
\dfrac{2}{x-1}-\dfrac{4}{(x-1)(x+1)};  x\ne\pm1
\dfrac{2(x+1)-4}{(x-1)(x+1)}=\dfrac{2x-2}{(x-1)(x+1)}
=\dfrac{2(x-1)}{(x-1)(x+1)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2}{x+1}},\ x\ne\pm1
(b) \dfrac{x}{x-5}-\dfrac{25}{x^2-5x}
\dfrac{x}{x-5}-\dfrac{25}{x(x-5)};  x\ne0,\ 5
\dfrac{x\cdot x-25}{x(x-5)}=\dfrac{x^2-25}{x(x-5)}
=\dfrac{(x-5)(x+5)}{x(x-5)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x+5}{x}},\ x\ne0,\ 5

Trinomial Denominators

Factor each trinomial (Unit 4), then build the LCD from the factors. Most trinomial questions share one factor, so the LCD has three factors, not four.
Example 5

Trinomial denominators

Simplify fully. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{3}{x^2+5x+6}+\dfrac{2}{x^2-x-6}
\dfrac{3}{(x+2)(x+3)}+\dfrac{2}{(x-3)(x+2)}
x\ne-2,\ \pm3;  LCD (x+2)(x+3)(x-3)
\dfrac{3(x-3)+2(x+3)}{(x+2)(x+3)(x-3)}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{5x-3}{(x+2)(x+3)(x-3)}}
(b) \dfrac{3}{x^2-2x-8}-\dfrac{1}{x^2-6x+8}
\dfrac{3}{(x-4)(x+2)}-\dfrac{1}{(x-4)(x-2)}
x\ne4,\ \pm2;  LCD (x-4)(x+2)(x-2)
\dfrac{3(x-2)-(x+2)}{(x-4)(x+2)(x-2)}=\dfrac{2x-8}{(x-4)(x+2)(x-2)}
=\dfrac{2(x-4)}{(x-4)(x+2)(x-2)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2}{(x+2)(x-2)}}
(c) \dfrac{2}{2x^2+5x-3}+\dfrac{1}{x^2+3x}
ac=2(-3)=-6 on top, b=5 underneath
1\times(-6): sum -5   -1\times6: sum 5
2\times(-3): sum -1   -2\times3: sum 1
2x^2+5x-3=(2x-1)(x+3) (decomposition: 6 and -1);  x^2+3x=x(x+3)
x\ne0,\ \tfrac{1}{2},\ -3;  LCD x(2x-1)(x+3)
\dfrac{2x+(2x-1)}{x(2x-1)(x+3)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{4x-1}{x(2x-1)(x+3)}}

Opposite Factors and Simplifying Each Term First

Factors such as x-5 and 5-x are opposites: 5-x=-(x-5). Rewrite the opposite factor and move its negative sign to the front of the fraction (this changes + to - or - to +).
Key idea
\dfrac{b}{a-x}=\dfrac{b}{-(x-a)}=-\dfrac{b}{x-a}    e.g. 9-a^2=-(a^2-9)=-(a-3)(a+3)

If one of the expressions can be simplified on its own, simplify it first, but take the NPVs from the original denominators.

Example 6

Opposites and simplifying first

Simplify fully. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{6}{x-5}+\dfrac{2}{5-x}
x\ne5
\dfrac{6}{x-5}-\dfrac{2}{x-5}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{4}{x-5}}
(b) \dfrac{3x}{x^2-16}-\dfrac{2}{4-x}
x\ne\pm4
\dfrac{3x}{(x-4)(x+4)}+\dfrac{2}{x-4}
=\dfrac{3x+2(x+4)}{(x-4)(x+4)}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{5x+8}{(x-4)(x+4)}}
(c) \dfrac{a+2}{a^2-a-6}+\dfrac{2}{9-a^2}
a\ne3,\ -2,\ -3
\dfrac{a+2}{(a-3)(a+2)}=\dfrac{1}{a-3}
\dfrac{1}{a-3}-\dfrac{2}{(a-3)(a+3)}
=\dfrac{a+3-2}{(a-3)(a+3)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{a+1}{(a-3)(a+3)}}

Example 7

Up the river and back

A boat travels 36 km up a river and 36 km back. Its speed in still water is 12 km/h and the current flows at c km/h, so the boat moves at 12-c km/h upstream and 12+c km/h downstream.

(a) Write the total travel time as a single rational expression in simplest form. What values of c make sense?
\dfrac{36}{12-c}+\dfrac{36}{12+c}
=\dfrac{36(12+c)+36(12-c)}{(12-c)(12+c)}
=\dfrac{432+36c+432-36c}{(12-c)(12+c)}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{864}{(12-c)(12+c)}} hours
In context 0\le c<12 (the boat must be able to go upstream).
(b) Determine the total time when the current is 3 km/h, and check with the original expression.
\dfrac{864}{(9)(15)}=\dfrac{864}{135}=\boldsymbol{6.4} h
Check: \dfrac{36}{9}+\dfrac{36}{15}=4+2.4=6.4 ✓
(c) How much longer does the upstream trip take than the downstream trip? Write a simplified expression and evaluate it for c=3.
\dfrac{36(12+c)-36(12-c)}{(12-c)(12+c)}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{72c}{(12-c)(12+c)}} hours
c=3: \dfrac{216}{135}=1.6 h
(4-2.4=1.6 ✓)

What the current does to the total time

The current helps on the way down and slows the boat on the way up. The two effects do not cancel: the slow leg always costs more time than the fast leg saves.

Extension: complex fractions

A complex fraction has a fraction in its numerator or denominator. Simplify the top and the bottom into single fractions, then divide (multiply by the reciprocal, Lesson 4). The NPVs come from every denominator, and the whole divisor cannot be 0.

Example 8

A complex fraction

Simplify \dfrac{1+\dfrac{1}{x}}{1-\dfrac{1}{x^2}}. State the non-permissible values.

Top: \dfrac{x+1}{x}.  Bottom: \dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2}=\dfrac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x^2}.
\dfrac{x+1}{x}\div\dfrac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x^2}=\dfrac{x+1}{x}\times\dfrac{x^2}{(x-1)(x+1)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x}{x-1}}
NPVs: x\ne0 (inner denominators) and x\ne\pm1 (the bottom cannot be 0).
✓

Key ideas

No common factor. The LCD is the product, left factored: (x+p)(x+q). Give each numerator the factor it is missing, expand, collect, then factor the numerator.
The minus sign meets the bracket. Expand the subtracted product inside brackets, then change every sign: -(x^2-5x+6)=-x^2+5x-6.
Factor first. Factor every denominator; the LCD uses each different factor the greatest number of times it appears. Factor the result’s numerator and cancel. Opposites: 5-x=-(x-5), so the sign in front flips.
NPVs and checks. NPVs come from the original denominators. Check with a value such as x=1, or with Y_1 (question) and Y_2 (answer) in the TABLE: ERROR rows are NPVs.
