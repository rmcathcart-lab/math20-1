Would You Rather?

Plate A: \tfrac{1}{2} of a cake, then \tfrac{1}{3} of the same cake, then \tfrac{1}{6} more.

Plate B: \tfrac{1}{2} of a cake, then \tfrac{1}{4}, then \tfrac{1}{8}, then \tfrac{1}{16}, … Every piece is half the one before, and the pieces never stop coming.

Would you rather have Plate A or Plate B?

?
Pick one, then convince the person beside you.
It’s a tie: exactly one cake each. Plate A: \tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{3}+\tfrac{1}{6}=\tfrac{3}{6}+\tfrac{2}{6}+\tfrac{1}{6}=1. Plate B creeps up on 1: \tfrac{1}{2},\ \tfrac{3}{4},\ \tfrac{7}{8},\ \tfrac{15}{16},\ \dots Each piece fills half of the gap that is left, so it never passes 1, and forever adds up to exactly 1. That is Unit 1’s infinite geometric series: S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}=\dfrac{1/2}{1-1/2}=1. (Plate A is still faster to eat.)

Adding and Subtracting with Monomial Denominators

Rational expressions add and subtract just like fractions: find the lowest common denominator, rewrite each term over it, then combine the numerators. Put each numerator in brackets when you subtract, and keep the non-permissible values with the answer.

I can…
  • add and subtract fractions using the LCD, and do the same when the numerators contain a variable
  • combine expressions with the same denominator, using brackets when subtracting and keeping the NPVs
  • find the LCD of monomials, such as 6x^2y and 4xy^3, and rewrite each expression over it
  • add and subtract expressions with different monomial denominators, including two variables and whole-number terms
  • catch common errors by substituting a number into the question and into my answer
AN5 Perform operations on rational expressions (limited to numerators and denominators that are monomials, binomials or trinomials).
Today: adding and subtracting rational expressions with monomial denominators.
1

Review: Adding and Subtracting Fractions

Fractions can be added or subtracted only when their denominators are the same. The smallest denominator that works is the lowest common denominator (LCD): the lowest common multiple of the denominators.

To add or subtract fractions:
  1. Find the LCD.
  2. Write each fraction as an equivalent fraction with the LCD as its denominator (multiply its numerator and denominator by the same number).
  3. Add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator.
  4. Simplify.
The calculator can check these answers, but it is the steps that carry over to rational expressions. For (a): 5÷6+3÷8ENTER, then MATH1ENTER (►Frac) gives 29/24.
Example 1

Fractions first

Simplify.

(a) \dfrac{5}{6}+\dfrac{3}{8}
LCD 24
\dfrac{20}{24}+\dfrac{9}{24}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{29}{24}}
(b) \dfrac{7}{10}-\dfrac{4}{15}
LCD 30
\dfrac{21}{30}-\dfrac{8}{30}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{13}{30}}
(c) \dfrac{2}{3}-\dfrac{5}{12}+\dfrac{3}{4}
LCD 12
\dfrac{8}{12}-\dfrac{5}{12}+\dfrac{9}{12}
=\dfrac{12}{12}=\boldsymbol{1}
(d) \dfrac{4}{9}-\dfrac{7}{6}
LCD 18
\dfrac{8}{18}-\dfrac{21}{18}=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{13}{18}}
2

Numerical Denominators

The same steps work when the numerators contain a variable. Multiply the whole numerator by the factor (use brackets), then expand and collect like terms. When subtracting, the minus sign applies to every term of the numerator that follows it.

\dfrac{a}{c}-\dfrac{b+d}{c}=\dfrac{a\textcolor{#c93a33}{-(b+d)}}{c}=\dfrac{a\textcolor{#c93a33}{-b-d}}{c}
Put each numerator in brackets before you subtract.

These denominators are numbers, never 0, so there are no non-permissible values.

Example 2

Brackets around every numerator

Simplify.

(a) \dfrac{x+3}{4}+\dfrac{2x-1}{6}
LCD 12
\dfrac{3(x+3)+2(2x-1)}{12}
=\dfrac{3x+9+4x-2}{12}
=\dfrac{7x+7}{12}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{7(x+1)}{12}}
(b) \dfrac{3a+2}{5}-\dfrac{a-4}{10}
LCD 10
\dfrac{2(3a+2)-(a-4)}{10}
=\dfrac{6a+4-a+4}{10}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{5a+8}{10}}
(c) \dfrac{y-2}{3}-\dfrac{2y+5}{4}+\dfrac{y}{6}
LCD 12
\dfrac{4(y-2)-3(2y+5)+2y}{12}
=\dfrac{4y-8-6y-15+2y}{12}
=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{23}{12}} (the y-terms cancel)
3

Expressions with the Same Denominator

When the denominators are already the same, add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator. State the non-permissible values (NPVs) from the denominator first. Then simplify: factor the numerator and cancel common factors. The NPVs stay with the answer, even if the factor that caused them cancels.
Example 3

Same denominator

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{7}{2x}+\dfrac{3}{2x}
x\ne0
\dfrac{10}{2x}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{5}{x}}
(b) \dfrac{4a+1}{3a}-\dfrac{a-5}{3a}
a\ne0
\dfrac{4a+1-(a-5)}{3a}
=\dfrac{3a+6}{3a}=\dfrac{3(a+2)}{3a}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{a+2}{a}}
(c) \dfrac{5m-2}{m+4}-\dfrac{2m-14}{m+4}
m\ne-4
\dfrac{5m-2-2m+14}{m+4}
=\dfrac{3m+12}{m+4}=\dfrac{3(m+4)}{m+4}
=\boldsymbol{3},\ m\ne-4
(d) \dfrac{x^2}{x-3}-\dfrac{9}{x-3}
x\ne3
\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}=\dfrac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}
=\boldsymbol{x+3},\ x\ne3
4

The LCD of Monomials

For monomial denominators, build the LCD one piece at a time.

LCD of monomials: the lowest common multiple of the coefficients, times each variable raised to the highest power that appears.   e.g. 6x^2y and 4xy^3:   LCD =12x^2y^3.

To rewrite an expression over the LCD, ask “what do I multiply my denominator by to get the LCD?” and multiply the numerator and the denominator by that monomial.

Example 4

Build the LCD

Determine the LCD.

(a) 5x and 3x^2
\boldsymbol{15x^2}
(b) 8a and 12b
\boldsymbol{24ab}
(c) 6m^2n and 9mn^3
\boldsymbol{18m^2n^3}
(d) 2x, 4 and x^3
\boldsymbol{4x^3}

Fill in the missing numerator.

(e) \dfrac{5}{6x^2y}=\dfrac{\boxed{\phantom{000}}}{12x^2y^3}
\boldsymbol{10y^2}:  6x^2y\times2y^2=12x^2y^3, so multiply the numerator by 2y^2.
(f) \dfrac{3}{4xy^3}=\dfrac{\boxed{\phantom{000}}}{12x^2y^3}
\boldsymbol{9x}:  4xy^3\times3x=12x^2y^3, so multiply by 3x.

5

Different Monomial Denominators

To add or subtract rational expressions:
  1. State the NPVs (each variable in a monomial denominator \ne0).
  2. Find the LCD.
  3. Rewrite each expression over the LCD.
  4. Combine the numerators (brackets when subtracting), expand and collect like terms.
  5. Simplify: factor the numerator if possible and cancel common factors.
Example 5

Different monomial denominators

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{2}{3x}+\dfrac{5}{6x}
x\ne0; LCD 6x
\dfrac{4}{6x}+\dfrac{5}{6x}=\dfrac{9}{6x}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3}{2x}}
(b) \dfrac{4}{a}-\dfrac{3}{a^2}
a\ne0; LCD a^2
\dfrac{4a}{a^2}-\dfrac{3}{a^2}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{4a-3}{a^2}}
(c) \dfrac{5}{6m^2n}-\dfrac{7}{9mn^3}
m\ne0,\ n\ne0; LCD 18m^2n^3
\dfrac{5(3n^2)}{18m^2n^3}-\dfrac{7(2m)}{18m^2n^3}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{15n^2-14m}{18m^2n^3}}
(d) \dfrac{1}{x}+\dfrac{2}{3x^2}-\dfrac{1}{2x^3}
x\ne0; LCD 6x^3
\dfrac{6x^2}{6x^3}+\dfrac{4x}{6x^3}-\dfrac{3}{6x^3}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{6x^2+4x-3}{6x^3}}
Example 6

Binomial numerators

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{x+2}{3x}+\dfrac{x-4}{2x}
x\ne0; LCD 6x
\dfrac{2(x+2)+3(x-4)}{6x}=\dfrac{2x+4+3x-12}{6x}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{5x-8}{6x}}
(b) \dfrac{2y-1}{4y}-\dfrac{y-3}{6y^2}
y\ne0; LCD 12y^2
\dfrac{3y(2y-1)-2(y-3)}{12y^2}=\dfrac{6y^2-3y-2y+6}{12y^2}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{6y^2-5y+6}{12y^2}} (the numerator does not factor)
(c) \dfrac{3a-b}{a^2b}-\dfrac{a-2b}{ab^2}
a\ne0,\ b\ne0; LCD a^2b^2
\dfrac{b(3a-b)-a(a-2b)}{a^2b^2}=\dfrac{3ab-b^2-a^2+2ab}{a^2b^2}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{-a^2+5ab-b^2}{a^2b^2}}
(d) \dfrac{x+6}{2x}-\dfrac{x+9}{3x}
x\ne0; LCD 6x
\dfrac{3(x+6)-2(x+9)}{6x}=\dfrac{3x+18-2x-18}{6x}
=\dfrac{x}{6x}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{1}{6}},\ x\ne0
The answer has no variable, but the original is undefined at x=0, so the restriction stays.
6

Whole-Number Terms, Common Errors and Problems

A term with no denominator has denominator 1:  3=\dfrac{3}{1}, so it is rewritten over the LCD like any other term.

!
Three common errors.
  • Adding the denominators:  \dfrac{2}{x}+\dfrac{3}{y}\ne\dfrac{5}{x+y}
  • Forgetting the brackets when subtracting.
  • Cancelling terms instead of factors.
A quick test for an answer: substitute a number (not an NPV) into the question and into the answer. If the values differ, there is an error. (Equal values do not prove the answer is right, but they catch most mistakes.)
Let the calculator substitute. Store the number in X, then type the question with X: 1STO→X,T,θ,nENTER, then 5÷(2X,T,θ,n)−(X,T,θ,n−3)÷(2X,T,θ,n)ENTER gives 3.5. Type your answer the same way and compare.
Example 7

Whole numbers and errors

(a) Simplify 3-\dfrac{2}{x}.
x\ne0; LCD x
\dfrac{3x}{x}-\dfrac{2}{x}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3x-2}{x}}
(b) Simplify \dfrac{x}{4}-\dfrac{3}{x}+1.
x\ne0; LCD 4x
\dfrac{x^2-12+4x}{4x}=\dfrac{x^2+4x-12}{4x}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{(x+6)(x-2)}{4x}}
(c) Kayla’s work is shown. Find her error and correct it.
\dfrac{5}{2x}-\dfrac{x-3}{2x}=\dfrac{5-x-3}{2x}=\dfrac{2-x}{2x}
The minus sign was not applied to -3:  5-(x-3)=5-x+3.
Correct: \boldsymbol{\dfrac{8-x}{2x}},\ x\ne0.
Test x=1:  \dfrac{5}{2}-\dfrac{-2}{2}=\dfrac{7}{2};  \dfrac{8-1}{2}=\dfrac{7}{2} ✓; Kayla’s gives \dfrac{1}{2}.
(d) Diego wrote \dfrac{2}{x}+\dfrac{3}{y}=\dfrac{5}{x+y}. Show that this is wrong by substituting x=1 and y=1, then simplify correctly.
Left side: 2+3=5. Right side: \dfrac{5}{2}. Not equal.
Correct: LCD xy,  \dfrac{2y}{xy}+\dfrac{3x}{xy}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3x+2y}{xy}},  x\ne0,\ y\ne0.
Example 8

Up and down the mountain

A hiker climbs a 9 km trail at an average speed of v km/h and comes down a longer 15 km trail at 2v km/h. (Time =\dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}.)

(a) Write the total time as a single rational expression in simplest form.
\dfrac{9}{v}+\dfrac{15}{2v}=\dfrac{18}{2v}+\dfrac{15}{2v}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{33}{2v}} hours,  v>0 (a speed is positive).
(b) Determine the total time when v=3 km/h.
\dfrac{33}{2(3)}=\boldsymbol{5.5} h.
Check: \dfrac{9}{3}+\dfrac{15}{6}=3+2.5=5.5 ✓
(c) Write a simplified expression for how much longer the climb takes than the descent, and evaluate it for v=3 km/h.
\dfrac{9}{v}-\dfrac{15}{2v}=\dfrac{18-15}{2v}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3}{2v}} hours.
When v=3: \dfrac{3}{6}=0.5 h, or 30 min.
✓

Key ideas

Same denominator. Combine the numerators and keep the denominator. NPVs first, and they stay: \dfrac{3(m+4)}{m+4}=3,\ m\ne-4.
LCD of monomials. LCM of the coefficients times the highest power of each variable: 6x^2y, 4xy^3 → 12x^2y^3. Multiply each term’s top and bottom by what its denominator is missing.
Brackets when subtracting. The minus hits every term: 5-(x-3)=5-x+3. A whole number has denominator 1: 3=\dfrac{3x}{x}.
Test your answer. Substitute a number that is not an NPV into the question and the answer. Different values mean an error. Never add denominators or cancel terms.
