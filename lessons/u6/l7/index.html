The Dice Deal

Ava and Ben roll two dice and add the numbers.

Ava wins if the sum is 6, 7 or 8, the three most common sums. Ben wins on any other sum.

Is this game fair? Would you rather be Ava or Ben?

?
Convince the person beside you.
Not fair, and it’s Ben’s game! There are 36 equally likely rolls. Only 5 + 6 + 5 = 16 of them make 6, 7 or 8, so Ava wins 16 out of 36 (about 44%) and Ben wins 20 out of 36 (about 56%). Ava has the three most likely sums, but Ben has the other eight. To make it fair, give Ava 2 and 12 as well: 16 + 1 + 1 = 18 of the 36 rolls.

1

Rational Equations and Their Non-Permissible Values

A rational equation is an equation with a variable in a denominator, such as \dfrac{3}{x}+1=\dfrac{5}{2x}. Before solving, find the non-permissible values (NPVs): factor every denominator in the original equation and set each factor with a variable equal to 0. No NPV can be a solution, because it would make a denominator 0.

A value is a solution (root) if it is permissible and makes the left side (LS) equal to the right side (RS). To verify a solution, substitute it into the original equation and evaluate each side separately.

Example 1

NPVs first

State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{7}{x}+2=\dfrac{3}{4x}
\boldsymbol{x\ne0}
(b) \dfrac{x}{x+5}=\dfrac{2}{3x-1}
x+5\ne0 and 3x-1\ne0:
\boldsymbol{x\ne-5,\ \tfrac{1}{3}}
(c) \dfrac{1}{x^2-6x}=\dfrac{4}{x^2-36}
x(x-6) and (x-6)(x+6)
\boldsymbol{x\ne0,\ 6,\ -6}
(d) \dfrac{3}{x^2+x-12}-\dfrac{1}{x}=2
(x+4)(x-3) and x
\boldsymbol{x\ne-4,\ 3,\ 0}
Example 2

Is it a solution?

Verify whether the given value is a solution of the equation.

(a) x=3;   \dfrac{6}{x}+1=\dfrac{9}{x}
x=3 is permissible (x\ne0).
LS =\tfrac{6}{3}+1=3   RS =\tfrac{9}{3}=3
LS = RS, so x=3 is a solution.
(b) x=1;   \dfrac{x}{x+3}=\dfrac{4}{x}
x=1 is permissible (x\ne-3,\ 0).
LS =\tfrac{1}{4}   RS =\tfrac{4}{1}=4
LS \ne RS, so x=1 is not a solution.
2

Solving by Multiplying by the LCD

Multiplying every term on both sides by the lowest common denominator (LCD) clears all the fractions and leaves a polynomial equation. Terms with no denominator (like the 1 in \frac{3}{x}+1) must be multiplied too.

Solving a rational equation
  1. Factor each denominator and state the NPVs.
  2. Multiply every term by the LCD and simplify.
  3. Solve the resulting equation.
  4. Reject any value that is an NPV.
  5. Verify in the original equation.
Example 3

Clear the fractions

State the NPVs, then solve. Verify the solution to (a).

(a) \dfrac{2}{x}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{7}{3x}
NPV: x\ne0.  LCD =3x
3x\left(\tfrac{2}{x}\right)+3x\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right)=3x\left(\tfrac{7}{3x}\right)
6+x=7
\boldsymbol{x=1}
Verify: LS =2+\tfrac{1}{3}=\tfrac{7}{3}, RS =\tfrac{7}{3} ✓
(b) \dfrac{x+1}{2x}-\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{1}{x}
NPV: x\ne0.  LCD =4x
2(x+1)-3x=4
2x+2-3x=4
-x=2
\boldsymbol{x=-2}
(c) \dfrac{4}{3x}+\dfrac{x-2}{x}=\dfrac{5}{6}
NPV: x\ne0.  LCD =6x
8+6(x-2)=5x
8+6x-12=5x
\boldsymbol{x=4}
3

One Fraction on Each Side: Cross-Multiplying

An equation with exactly one fraction on each side is a proportion. Cross-multiplying is a shortcut for multiplying both sides by the LCD.

If \dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}  (b,\ d\ne0), then ad=bc.
!
Put brackets around binomials when you cross-multiply. If x^2 terms appear on both sides, they may cancel and leave a linear equation.
Example 4

Cross-multiply

State the NPVs and solve.

(a) \dfrac{4}{x-1}=\dfrac{3}{x+2}
NPVs: x\ne1,\ -2
4(x+2)=3(x-1)
4x+8=3x-3
\boldsymbol{x=-11}
(b) \dfrac{2x+1}{x-3}=\dfrac{4x-5}{2x+1}
NPVs: x\ne3,\ -\tfrac{1}{2}
(2x+1)^2=(4x-5)(x-3)
4x^2+4x+1=4x^2-17x+15
21x=14
\boldsymbol{x=\tfrac{2}{3}}
(c) \dfrac{5}{3-y}=2
NPV: y\ne3
\tfrac{5}{3-y}=\tfrac{2}{1}
5=2(3-y)
5=6-2y
\boldsymbol{y=\tfrac{1}{2}}
Example 5

Boys and girls

A club has 7 more girls than boys. The ratio of boys to girls is 4:5. Write and solve a rational equation to determine the number of boys and girls.

Let b be the number of boys; there are b+7 girls.
\dfrac{b}{b+7}=\dfrac{4}{5}   (b\ne-7)
5b=4(b+7)\Rightarrow5b=4b+28\Rightarrow b=28
There are 28 boys and 35 girls. (Check: \tfrac{28}{35}=\tfrac{4}{5} ✓)
4

Binomial and Trinomial Denominators

Factor every denominator first. The LCD is the product of all the different factors, each used the greatest number of times it appears in any one denominator. Denominators that are opposites, like x-4 and 4-x, are related by 4-x=-(x-4), so \dfrac{3}{4-x}=-\dfrac{3}{x-4}.
Example 6

Factor, then clear

State the NPVs, solve and verify.

(a) \dfrac{3}{x+2}+\dfrac{1}{x-2}=\dfrac{8}{x^2-4}
x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2). NPVs: x\ne-2,\ 2
LCD =(x-2)(x+2)
3(x-2)+1(x+2)=8
4x-4=8,  \boldsymbol{x=3}
Verify: LS =\tfrac{3}{5}+1=\tfrac{8}{5}, RS =\tfrac{8}{5} ✓
(b) \dfrac{2}{x-1}-\dfrac{1}{x+3}=\dfrac{6}{x^2+2x-3}
x^2+2x-3=(x+3)(x-1). NPVs: x\ne1,\ -3
2(x+3)-1(x-1)=6
2x+6-x+1=6,  \boldsymbol{x=-1}
Verify: LS =\tfrac{2}{-2}-\tfrac{1}{2}=-\tfrac{3}{2}
RS =\tfrac{6}{-4}=-\tfrac{3}{2} ✓
(c) \dfrac{7}{x-4}+\dfrac{3}{4-x}=2
NPV: x\ne4
\tfrac{7}{x-4}-\tfrac{3}{x-4}=2
\tfrac{4}{x-4}=2
4=2(x-4)\Rightarrow4=2x-8\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{x=6}
(d) \dfrac{3}{x}+\dfrac{x}{x+2}=1
NPVs: x\ne0,\ -2.  LCD =x(x+2)
3(x+2)+x^2=x(x+2)
x^2+3x+6=x^2+2x  (x^2 cancels)
\boldsymbol{x=-6}
Verify: LS =-\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{-6}{-4}=1, RS =1 ✓

Rejected Values and Equations with No Solution

Multiplying by the LCD multiplies by an expression that equals 0 at each NPV, so the polynomial equation can have a root that the original equation does not. Such a root is extraneous and must be rejected. If every value you find is rejected, or the variable cancels and leaves a false statement (like 0=5), the equation has no solution.
Example 7

Keep it or reject it?

Solve.

(a) \dfrac{3x}{x-2}=1+\dfrac{6}{x-2}
NPV: x\ne2.  LCD =x-2
3x=(x-2)+6
2x=4
x=2, but 2 is an NPV: reject it.
The equation has no solution.
(b) \dfrac{2}{x+3}+\dfrac{1}{x-3}=\dfrac{6}{x^2-9}
NPVs: x\ne-3,\ 3
2(x-3)+1(x+3)=6
3x-3=6
x=3, an NPV: reject it.
No solution.
6

Solving with a Graphing Calculator

The solutions of an equation are the x-coordinates of the points where the graphs of y=\text{LS} and y=\text{RS} intersect.

Intersect method: Y_1= left side, Y_2= right side; graph, then use intersect at each crossing.
Zero method: Y_1= left side - right side; graph, then use zero at each x-intercept.

Use brackets: type \dfrac{5}{x+1} as 5/(X+1). Each NPV gives a vertical asymptote, where the graph breaks into separate pieces; the calculator may draw a near-vertical line there that is not part of the graph. Choose a window that shows every intersection.

Example 8

Algebra and the graphing calculator

(a) Solve \dfrac{5}{x+1}=\dfrac{2}{x-3} algebraically, then confirm with a graphing calculator.
NPVs: x\ne-1,\ 3
5(x-3)=2(x+1)\Rightarrow5x-15=2x+2\Rightarrow3x=17
\boldsymbol{x=\tfrac{17}{3}}   Calculator: the graphs intersect at x\approx5.67 ✓
(b) The graphs of y=\dfrac{2}{x}+1 and y=\dfrac{x}{3} are shown. Use a graphing calculator to solve \dfrac{2}{x}+1=\dfrac{x}{3}, to the nearest hundredth. State a suitable window.
Window: x from -10 to 10, y from -10 to 10.
Y_1=2/X+1, Y_2=X/3; intersect twice.
\boldsymbol{x\approx-1.37} or \boldsymbol{x\approx4.37} (neither is the NPV 0).
(Lesson 8 shows how to find these exactly.)
On the calculator. Y= enter both sides, ZOOM6 (ZStandard), then 2ndTRACE 5: intersect and ENTER for the first curve, the second curve, and a guess near the crossing.
✓

Key ideas

NPVs first. Factor every denominator of the original equation and set each factor with a variable to 0. No NPV can be a solution.
Clear the fractions. Multiply every term by the LCD, including terms with no denominator. One fraction on each side: cross-multiply, \frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}\Rightarrow ad=bc, with brackets around binomials.
Reject and verify. A root that is an NPV is extraneous: reject it. If every root is rejected (or you get 0=5), there is no solution. Verify in the original equation.
Graphing calculator. Y_1= LS, Y_2= RS and intersect, or Y_1= LS - RS and zero. Brackets around denominators; a window that shows every crossing.
