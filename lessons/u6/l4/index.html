The Mystery Machine

A number goes in, the machine does something to it, and a new number comes out. What comes out when 0.4 goes in?

4→\dfrac{1}{4}
\dfrac{2}{3}→\dfrac{3}{2}
\dfrac{1}{5}→5
-\dfrac{3}{7}→-\dfrac{7}{3}
0.4→?
?
What is the machine doing to each number?
2.5 (that is \tfrac{5}{2}). The machine turns every number upside down: it gives the reciprocal. 0.4=\tfrac{2}{5}, so out comes \tfrac{5}{2}=2.5. The twist: feed it 0 and the machine jams. \tfrac{0}{1} would turn into \tfrac{1}{0}, and no number times 0 makes 1, so 0 has no reciprocal. (Only 1 and -1 come out the same as they went in.)

Dividing Rational Expressions

To divide, keep the first expression, change \div to \times and flip the divisor. Then it is a multiplication, as in Lesson 3. The new part is the non-permissible values: the divisor’s numerator cannot be 0 either.

I can…
  • divide by multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor (only the expression right after \div flips)

  • divide rational expressions with polynomials and with two variables, leaving the answer in factored form
  • simplify mixed multiplication and division from left to right

Dividing by a Fraction

Dividing by a number is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal: \div\dfrac{3}{4} is the same as \times\dfrac{4}{3}. Only the expression right after the \div sign (the divisor) is turned upside down. After that, it is a multiplication, done as in Lesson 3.

\dfrac{A}{B}\div\dfrac{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{C}}{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{D}}=\dfrac{A}{B}\cdot\dfrac{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{D}}{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{C}}
Keep the first expression, change \div to \times, flip the divisor.
Example 1

Fractions first

Divide. Write each answer in lowest terms.

(a) \dfrac{8}{15}\div\dfrac{12}{25}
=\dfrac{8}{15}\times\dfrac{25}{12}
=\dfrac{2\cdot4}{3\cdot5}\times\dfrac{5\cdot5}{3\cdot4}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{10}{9}}
(b) -\dfrac{9}{14}\div6
=-\dfrac{9}{14}\times\dfrac{1}{6}
=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{3}{28}}
(c) \dfrac{4}{9}\div\dfrac{2}{3}\times\dfrac{5}{8}
Flip only \tfrac{2}{3}:
=\dfrac{4}{9}\times\dfrac{3}{2}\times\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{60}{144}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{5}{12}}
2

Non-Permissible Values in a Division

In \dfrac{A}{B}\div\dfrac{C}{D}, the denominators B and D cannot be 0. The divisor \dfrac{C}{D} cannot be 0 either, because division by zero is undefined; this happens when C=0. After flipping, C is in a denominator, which is another way to see it.

NPVs of \dfrac{A}{B}\div\dfrac{C}{D}: every value that makes B=0, D=0 or \textcolor{#c27a0e}{C=0} (the numerator of the divisor). Find them all from the original expression, before you flip.
Example 2

Monomials

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{12a^3}{5b^2}\div\dfrac{18a}{25b^5}
=\dfrac{12a^3}{5b^2}\cdot\dfrac{25b^5}{18a}=\dfrac{300a^3b^5}{90ab^2}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{10a^2b^3}{3}}
\boldsymbol{b\ne0} (denominators) and \boldsymbol{a\ne0} (the divisor’s numerator 18a)
(b) \dfrac{-14x^2y}{9z^3}\div\dfrac{21xy^4}{6z}
=\dfrac{-14x^2y}{9z^3}\cdot\dfrac{6z}{21xy^4}=\dfrac{-84x^2yz}{189xy^4z^3}=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{4x}{9y^3z^2}}
\boldsymbol{x\ne0,\ y\ne0,\ z\ne0}
Example 3

NPVs only

State the non-permissible values. Do not simplify.

(a) \dfrac{x+2}{x-5}\div\dfrac{x+7}{x}
Denominators: x\ne5,\ x\ne0
Divisor’s numerator: x\ne-7
NPVs: \boldsymbol{x\ne5,0,-7}
(b) \dfrac{x^2-9}{x^2+4x}\div\dfrac{x^2-x-6}{2x-1}
Denominators: x(x+4) and 2x-1: x\ne0,-4,\tfrac{1}{2}
Divisor’s numerator: (x-3)(x+2): x\ne3,-2
NPVs: \boldsymbol{x\ne0,-4,\tfrac{1}{2},3,-2} (five values)
3

Dividing Rational Expressions with Polynomials

1Factor every numerator and denominator.
2State the NPVs (both denominators and the divisor’s numerator).
3Multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.
4Divide out common factors and multiply. Leave the answer in factored form.

A polynomial divisor such as x^2+3x-10 is \dfrac{x^2+3x-10}{1}, so its reciprocal is \dfrac{1}{x^2+3x-10}.

Example 4

Factor, NPVs, flip

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{x+4}{x^2-2x}\div\dfrac{3x+12}{x^2-4}
NPVs: x\ne0,2; x\ne\pm2; x\ne-4, i.e. \boldsymbol{x\ne0,2,-2,-4}
=\dfrac{x+4}{x(x-2)}\cdot\dfrac{(x-2)(x+2)}{3(x+4)}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x+2}{3x}}
(b) \dfrac{5y^2}{y^2-36}\div\dfrac{10y}{y+6}
NPVs: \boldsymbol{y\ne6,-6,0}
=\dfrac{5y^2}{(y-6)(y+6)}\cdot\dfrac{y+6}{10y}
=\dfrac{5y^2}{10y(y-6)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{y}{2(y-6)}}
(c) \dfrac{x^2-25}{4x}\div(x^2+3x-10)
x^2+3x-10=(x+5)(x-2)
NPVs: \boldsymbol{x\ne0,-5,2}
=\dfrac{(x-5)(x+5)}{4x}\cdot\dfrac{1}{(x+5)(x-2)}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x-5}{4x(x-2)}}
Example 5

Trinomials and two variables

Simplify. State the non-permissible values (restrictions).

(a) \dfrac{x^2+x-12}{x^2-x-2}\div\dfrac{x^2+6x+8}{x^2-1}
=\dfrac{(x+4)(x-3)}{(x-2)(x+1)}\div\dfrac{(x+2)(x+4)}{(x-1)(x+1)}
NPVs: \boldsymbol{x\ne2,-1,1,-2,-4}
=\dfrac{(x+4)(x-3)}{(x-2)(x+1)}\cdot\dfrac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x+2)(x+4)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{(x-3)(x-1)}{(x-2)(x+2)}}
(b) \dfrac{2a^2-a-6}{a^2-9}\div\dfrac{4a^2-9}{6-2a}
=\dfrac{(2a+3)(a-2)}{(a-3)(a+3)}\div\dfrac{(2a-3)(2a+3)}{-2(a-3)}
NPVs: \boldsymbol{a\ne3,-3,\tfrac{3}{2},-\tfrac{3}{2}}
=\dfrac{(2a+3)(a-2)}{(a-3)(a+3)}\cdot\dfrac{-2(a-3)}{(2a-3)(2a+3)}=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{2(a-2)}{(a+3)(2a-3)}}
(c) \dfrac{x^2-y^2}{x^2+4xy+3y^2}\div\dfrac{2x^2-2xy}{x+3y}
=\dfrac{(x-y)(x+y)}{(x+y)(x+3y)}\cdot\dfrac{x+3y}{2x(x-y)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{1}{2x}}
Restrictions: \boldsymbol{x\ne-y,\ x\ne-3y} (denominators); \boldsymbol{x\ne0,\ x\ne y} (the divisor’s numerator 2x(x-y))

Mixed Multiplication and Division

Multiplication and division have equal priority, so work from left to right. The safe method: change each \div into \times by flipping only the expression right after it, then simplify the whole product at once.

!
\dfrac{4}{9}\div\dfrac{2}{3}\times\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{5}{12}, but \dfrac{4}{9}\div\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\times\dfrac{5}{8}\right)=\dfrac{16}{15}. Do not group the last two unless there are brackets. The NPVs come from every denominator and from the numerator of every divisor.
Example 6

Left to right

Simplify. State the non-permissible values.

(a) \dfrac{x^2-4x}{x+3}\div\dfrac{x^2-16}{x^2+3x}\cdot\dfrac{x+4}{2x}
=\dfrac{x(x-4)}{x+3}\cdot\dfrac{x(x+3)}{(x-4)(x+4)}\cdot\dfrac{x+4}{2x}
NPVs: \boldsymbol{x\ne-3,\ x\ne0} (denominators); \boldsymbol{x\ne4,-4} (divisor’s numerator)
=\dfrac{x^2}{2x}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x}{2}}
(b) \dfrac{m^2+m-6}{m^2-4}\cdot\dfrac{m^2+5m+6}{m^2+6m+9}\div\dfrac{m+2}{3m}
=\dfrac{(m+3)(m-2)}{(m-2)(m+2)}\cdot\dfrac{(m+2)(m+3)}{(m+3)^2}\cdot\dfrac{3m}{m+2}
NPVs: \boldsymbol{m\ne2,-2,-3} (denominators); \boldsymbol{m\ne0} (3m is a denominator); \boldsymbol{m\ne-2} (divisor’s numerator)
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3m}{m+2}}

Complex Fractions and Problems

A complex fraction has a fraction in its numerator, its denominator or both. The main fraction bar means divide.

\dfrac{\;\dfrac{A}{B}\;}{\;\dfrac{\textcolor{#c27a0e}{C}}{D}\;}=\dfrac{A}{B}\div\dfrac{\textcolor{#c27a0e}{C}}{D}
NPVs: B=0, D=0 or \textcolor{#c27a0e}{C=0}.

(Complex fractions with sums or differences inside them need the adding and subtracting skills of Lessons 5 and 6.) In problems, division answers questions such as “width = area \div length” and “how many times larger”.

Example 7

A complex fraction

Simplify \dfrac{\;\dfrac{x^2-9}{6x}\;}{\;\dfrac{x^2+x-12}{4x^2}\;}. State the non-permissible values.

=\dfrac{(x-3)(x+3)}{6x}\div\dfrac{(x+4)(x-3)}{4x^2}   NPVs: \boldsymbol{x\ne0} (denominators); \boldsymbol{x\ne-4,3} (the numerator of the bottom fraction)
=\dfrac{(x-3)(x+3)}{6x}\cdot\dfrac{4x^2}{(x+4)(x-3)}=\dfrac{4x^2(x+3)}{6x(x+4)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2x(x+3)}{3(x+4)}}
Example 8

The rectangle

A rectangle has area \dfrac{x^2+5x}{x-2} square centimetres and length \dfrac{x^2-25}{x^2-4} centimetres, where x>5.

(a) Write and simplify an expression for its width.
width = area \div length
=\dfrac{x(x+5)}{x-2}\cdot\dfrac{(x-2)(x+2)}{(x-5)(x+5)}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x(x+2)}{x-5}} centimetres
(b) Determine the width when x=8, to the nearest tenth.
\dfrac{8(10)}{3}=\dfrac{80}{3}\approx\boldsymbol{26.7} cm
(Check: length =\tfrac{39}{60}=0.65, area =\tfrac{104}{6}, and \tfrac{104}{6}\div0.65\approx26.7.)
Evaluate on the TI-84. Put brackets around the numerator and the denominator: 8×(8+2)÷(8−5)ENTER.
✓

Key ideas

Keep, change, flip. \dfrac{A}{B}\div\dfrac{C}{D}=\dfrac{A}{B}\cdot\dfrac{D}{C}. Only the expression right after \div flips; a polynomial divisor is over 1.
NPVs before you flip. Every denominator and the divisor’s numerator: B=0, D=0 or C=0. The simplified answer can hide them.
Polynomials. Factor, state the NPVs, multiply by the reciprocal, divide out common factors. Leave the answer in factored form.
Left to right. In a mixed product, flip only what follows each \div. A complex fraction’s main bar means \div: its bottom numerator gives NPVs too.
