The Sum of a Geometric Series

A geometric series is the sum of the terms of a geometric sequence: 2,\ 6,\ 18,\ 54 is a sequence; 2+6+18+54 is a series. S_n is the sum of the first n terms.

To find a formula, write S_n, multiply every term by r, and subtract. Almost every term cancels.

So S_n(r-1)=t_1(r^n-1). Dividing by r-1 gives the formula. Since t_1r^n=r(t_1r^{n-1})=rt_n, there is a second form that uses the last term instead of n.

Sum of a geometric series (r\ne 1)
S_n=\dfrac{t_1(r^n-1)}{r-1}
Use when you know n.
or
S_n=\dfrac{rt_n-t_1}{r-1}
Use when you know the last term t_n.
If r=1, every term equals t_1, so S_n=nt_1.
Example 1

Three ways to one sum

For the series 5+10+20+40+80, find the sum by adding, by the first formula, and by the second formula.

By adding
5+10+20+40+80=\boldsymbol{155}
By the first formula
t_1=5,\ r=2,\ n=5
S_5=\dfrac{5(2^5-1)}{2-1}=5(31)=\boldsymbol{155}
By the second formula
t_n=80
S_5=\dfrac{2(80)-5}{2-1}=\boldsymbol{155}

Finding the Sum

Find \textcolor{#0f7a80}{t_1} and \textcolor{#c27a0e}{r} (divide any term by the one before it), then decide which form fits. When r is negative, put it in brackets: (-3)^8, not -3^8. Give exact answers (fractions) unless a rounding is asked for.
!
Brackets on a negative r. (-3)^8=6561 (eight negatives multiply to a positive), but -3^8=-6561 (the power happens first, then the minus).
On the calculator, type the brackets too. Use the negative key (−) for -3: ((−)3)^8. For a whole sum, put the top and the bottom each in brackets.
Example 2

The sum when you know n

Determine each sum.

(a) The first 12 terms of 7+21+63+\cdots
t_1=7,\ r=3
S_{12}=\dfrac{7(3^{12}-1)}{3-1}
=\dfrac{7(531\,440)}{2}=\boldsymbol{1\,860\,040}
(b) The first 8 terms of 2-6+18-\cdots
t_1=2,\ r=-3
S_8=\dfrac{2\left((-3)^8-1\right)}{-3-1}
=\dfrac{2(6560)}{-4}=\boldsymbol{-3280}
(c) The first 7 terms of 128+32+8+\cdots (exact)
t_1=128,\ r=\tfrac{1}{4}
S_7=\dfrac{128\left(\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^7-1\right)}{\frac{1}{4}-1}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{5461}{32}}
(=170.656\,25)
Example 3

The sum when you know the last term

Determine each sum.

(a) 3+12+48+\cdots+12\,288
t_1=3,\ r=4,\ t_n=12\,288
S_n=\dfrac{rt_n-t_1}{r-1}=\dfrac{4(12\,288)-3}{4-1}=\dfrac{49\,149}{3}=\boldsymbol{16\,383}
(b) 1458-486+162-\cdots+2
t_1=1458,\ r=-\tfrac{1}{3},\ t_n=2
S_n=\dfrac{-\frac{1}{3}(2)-1458}{-\frac{1}{3}-1}=\dfrac{-\frac{4376}{3}}{-\frac{4}{3}}=\boldsymbol{1094}

Finding n, t_1 or r

Substitute what you know into a formula and solve for the unknown.

Finding n
Isolate the power (3^n=729) and find the exponent by trial, with a table of values. Or find the last term with the second formula, then its position with t_n=t_1r^{n-1}.
Finding t_1
Use a known term and r \left(t_1=\dfrac{t_k}{r^{k-1}}\right), or solve the sum formula for t_1.
Finding r
When n=3, S_3=t_1+t_1r+t_1r^2 gives a quadratic equation to factor (there may be two values of r). For larger n, try values of r in a table.

Finding n: climb the powers

Example 4

How many terms?

(a) How many terms of 4+20+100+\cdots add to 15\,624?
t_1=4,\ r=5: \dfrac{4(5^n-1)}{5-1}=15\,624
5^n-1=15\,624\Rightarrow 5^n=15\,625
Trial: 5^5=3125, 5^6=15\,625, so \boldsymbol{n=6}.
(b) The series 4-8+16-\cdots has a sum of 684. Determine the last term and the number of terms.
t_1=4,\ r=-2: \dfrac{-2t_n-4}{-2-1}=684
-2t_n-4=-2052\Rightarrow t_n=1024
4(-2)^{n-1}=1024\Rightarrow(-2)^{n-1}=256=(-2)^8
n-1=8, so 9 terms; the last term is 1024.
Find n with a table of values on the TI-84. For (a), Y= Y_1=4(5^X-1)\div(5-1) (the sum of X terms), then 2ndTBLSET TblStart = 1, ΔTbl = 1 and 2ndTABLE: 15\,624 is at X=6. For (b), a table of the terms 4(-2)^{X-1} finds 1024 at X=9.
Example 5

Finding t_1 and r

(a) In a geometric sequence, t_4=540 and r=3. Determine S_8.
t_1(3)^3=540\Rightarrow t_1=20
S_8=\dfrac{20(3^8-1)}{3-1}
=10(6560)=\boldsymbol{65\,600}
(b) A geometric series has t_1=6 and S_3=126. Determine all possible values of r.
6+6r+6r^2=126
r^2+r+1=21
r^2+r-20=0
(r+5)(r-4)=0
\boldsymbol{r=4} or \boldsymbol{r=-5}
Check: 6+24+96=126; 6-30+150=126.
(c) A geometric series has t_1=4 and S_6=1456. Use a table to find r (r>0), then find t_3.
Y_1=\dfrac{4(X^6-1)}{X-1}
X=2: 252; X=3: 1456
r=3, so t_3=4(3)^2=\boldsymbol{36}.

Terms from a Sum Formula

S_n is the sum of the first n terms, and S_{n-1} is the sum of the first n-1 terms. Their difference is the nth term.

t_n=S_n-S_{n-1} and t_1=S_1
Example 6

Terms from S_n

The sum of the first n terms of a series is S_n=2(5^n-1).

(a) Determine the first four terms.
S_1=8,\ S_2=48,\ S_3=248,\ S_4=1248
t_1=8,\ t_2=48-8=40,\ t_3=248-48=200,
t_4=1248-248=1000
The series is \boldsymbol{8+40+200+1000+\cdots} (r=5).
(b) Determine t_7 without finding the general term.
t_7=S_7-S_6=2(5^7-1)-2(5^6-1)
=156\,248-31\,248=\boldsymbol{125\,000}
(Check: 8(5)^6=125\,000.)

Geometric Series in Context

A dropped ball travels down and up. When it hits the ground for the kth time, it has fallen k times (the drop plus k-1 falls after bounces) and risen k-1 times. Find the two sums separately and add them.

Total distance at the kth hit = (sum of k falls, starting with the drop height) + (sum of k-1 rises, starting with the first bounce height)
A fraction ratio on the calculator: bracket it, 27×(2÷3)^5, or the power goes on the 3 only.
Example 7

A bouncing ball

A ball is dropped from a height of 27 m. Each bounce reaches \tfrac{2}{3} of the height before.

(a) How high is the 5th bounce, to the nearest centimetre?
27\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^5=\dfrac{32}{9}\approx\boldsymbol{3.56} m
(b) Determine the total vertical distance the ball has travelled when it hits the ground for the 6th time, to the nearest centimetre.
Falls (6 terms): 27+18+12+\cdots
\dfrac{27\left(\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^6-1\right)}{\frac{2}{3}-1}=\dfrac{665}{9}\approx 73.89
Rises (5 terms): 18+12+8+\cdots
\dfrac{18\left(\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^5-1\right)}{\frac{2}{3}-1}=\dfrac{422}{9}\approx 46.89
Total =\dfrac{1087}{9}\approx\boldsymbol{120.78} m

A phone tree

Each level calls r times as many people as the level before, so the levels form a geometric sequence, and the total called is a geometric series.

Example 8

A phone tree

A club uses a phone tree for news. The president calls 5 members (level 1). Each of them calls 5 new members (level 2), and so on.

(a) How many members are called in the first 4 levels?
5+25+125+625:
S_4=\dfrac{5(5^4-1)}{5-1}=\boldsymbol{780} members
(b) How many levels are needed to reach at least 12\,000 members?
Table of \dfrac{5(5^n-1)}{4}: S_5=3905, S_6=19\,530.
6 levels are needed.

Key ideas

Shift and subtract. Write S_n and rS_n, one place apart. The matching terms cancel, leaving rS_n-S_n=t_1r^n-t_1.
Two forms (r\ne1): S_n=\dfrac{t_1(r^n-1)}{r-1} when you know n; S_n=\dfrac{rt_n-t_1}{r-1} when you know the last term. If r=1, S_n=nt_1.
Work backwards. Isolate the power to find n; hop back from a known term for t_1; a quadratic (or a table) for r. Brackets on a negative r.
Terms from sums: t_n=S_n-S_{n-1}, t_1=S_1. Bouncing ball: at the kth hit, k falls plus k-1 rises.
