The Three Switches

Three light switches are downstairs. One of them turns on a lamp in a closed room upstairs; the other two do nothing. From downstairs you can't see the room.

Flip the switches as much as you like, then walk upstairs once.

How can you tell which switch works the lamp?

?
One trip up, no coming back down. Can you be sure?
Turn switch A on and wait ten minutes. Then turn A off, turn B on, and go upstairs.
Lamp on: it's B.  Lamp off but warm: it's A (it was on long enough to heat up).  Off and cold: it's C.
The twist: a lamp tells you more than “on or off”. It can also be warm, so one trip gives three different clues for three switches. (Use an old-style bulb: an LED barely warms up!)

Three Forms of a Quadratic Function

The same parabola can be written in three forms. Each form shows some features directly; the others take a little work.

FormEquationRead directly
Vertex formy=a(x-p)^2+qvertex (p,\ q), axis x=p, max/min value q
General formy=ax^2+bx+cy-intercept c
Factored formy=a(x-r)(x-s)zeros r and s (the x-intercepts)
In all three forms a is the same number: it gives the direction of opening and the shape. To change vertex form or factored form into general form, expand and collect like terms.
Example 1

Write each function in general form and state a, b and c.

(a) y=2(x-3)(x+1)
y=2(x^2-2x-3)
y=2x^2-4x-6;  a=2, b=-4, c=-6
(b) y=-(x+4)(x-2)
y=-(x^2+2x-8)
y=-x^2-2x+8;  a=-1, b=-2, c=8
(c) y=\frac12(x-6)(x+2)
y=\frac12(x^2-4x-12)
y=\frac12x^2-2x-6;  a=\frac12, b=-2, c=-6
(d) y=3(x-1)^2-5
y=3(x^2-2x+1)-5
y=3x^2-6x-2;  a=3, b=-6, c=-2

Factored Form: Zeros, Axis of Symmetry and Vertex

In y=a(x-r)(x-s), the zero product property gives the zeros r and s. The two x-intercepts are mirror images, so the axis of symmetry is halfway between them. The vertex lies on the axis: substitute that x-value to get its y-coordinate.

Key idea
y=a(x-r)(x-s):  zeros r and s;  axis x=\dfrac{r+s}{2};  vertex: substitute x=\dfrac{r+s}{2};  y-intercept: substitute x=0.

A factor such as (2x+3) gives the zero x=-\frac32 (set the factor equal to 0).

3

General Form: the y-Intercept and the Axis x=-\frac{b}{2a}

In y=ax^2+bx+c, substituting x=0 gives y=c, so the y-intercept is c. For the axis of symmetry, compare with y=ax^2+bx: it is the same parabola moved c units up or down, so it has the same axis. Its zeros come from x(ax+b)=0: x=0 and x=-\frac{b}{a}. The axis is halfway between them.

Key idea
y=ax^2+bx+c:  y-intercept c;  axis of symmetry x=-\dfrac{b}{2a};  vertex: substitute x=-\dfrac{b}{2a} into the equation;  a>0: minimum, a<0: maximum.
!
Two signs to watch. The formula starts with a negative: -\frac{b}{2a}, so a negative b gives a positive x. And when you substitute a negative x, square it in brackets: (-3)^2=9.

When the Zeros Are Not Integers

Many general-form functions do not factor. Two ways to find the x-intercepts:

  • Graphing calculator: graph the function and use the zero feature (once for each x-intercept). This gives decimal answers.
  • Exact values: find the vertex (p,\ q) with x=-\frac{b}{2a}. Since a is the same in every form, the function is y=a(x-p)^2+q. Then solve a(x-p)^2+q=0 with square roots, as in Lesson 3. (Lesson 6 shows another way to reach vertex form: completing the square.)
The zero feature. Put the function in Y=, graph it, then 2ndCALC2 (zero): give a left bound, a right bound and a guess around one x-intercept. Do it again for the other one. For y=2x^2-4x-5 (Example 6):
Example 6

Exact zeros through vertex form

Consider y=2x^2-4x-5.

(a) Determine the vertex and write the function in vertex form. Check by expanding.
Axis x=-\frac{-4}{2(2)}=1; y=2-4-5=-7.
Vertex (1,\ -7), and a=2: y=2(x-1)^2-7.
Check: 2(x^2-2x+1)-7=2x^2-4x-5 ✓
(b) Determine the x-intercepts exactly and to the nearest hundredth. Check with the calculator's zero feature.
2(x-1)^2=7\Rightarrow(x-1)^2=\frac72
x=1\pm\sqrt{\frac72}=1\pm\frac{\sqrt{14}}{2}
x\approx2.87 or x\approx-0.87 (the zero feature gives the same).

Using the Best Form

Choose the form that shows what you need: vertex form for the vertex and range, general form for the y-intercept, factored form for the zeros. When an unknown coefficient is involved, use the formula for the axis or substitute a known point.
Example 7

One function, three equations

The equations y=2(x-1)^2-8,  y=2x^2-4x-6  and  y=2(x+1)(x-3) are given.

(a) Show that all three describe the same function.
2(x-1)^2-8=2(x^2-2x+1)-8=2x^2-4x-6
2(x+1)(x-3)=2(x^2-2x-3)=2x^2-4x-6 ✓
(b) Complete the table, naming the form that shows each feature most directly.
FeatureValueBest form
Vertex(1,\ -8)vertex form
y-intercept-6general form
Zeros-1 and 3factored form
Range\{y\mid y\ge-8,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}vertex form
Vertex (1,\ -8) and range \{y\mid y\ge-8,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\} from vertex form; y-intercept -6 from general form; zeros -1 and 3 from factored form.
Example 8

An unknown coefficient

(a) The graph of y=x^2+bx+11 has axis of symmetry x=3. Determine b and the vertex.
-\frac{b}{2(1)}=3\Rightarrow b=-6
y=9-18+11=2
Vertex (3,\ 2)
(b) The function y=-2x^2+8x+c has a maximum value of 5. Determine c.
Axis x=-\frac{8}{2(-2)}=2, so the vertex is (2,\ 5).
5=-2(4)+8(2)+c=8+c
c=-3
Example 9

A pen against the barn

A farmer uses 40 m of fencing to make a rectangular pen against a long barn wall (no fence along the wall). If each side touching the wall is x m long, the area in square metres is A=x(40-2x).

(a) Write A in the form a(x-r)(x-s) and state the zeros. What do they mean?
A=x(-2)(x-20)=-2x(x-20)
Zeros 0 and 20: with x=0 or x=20 there is no pen (the area is 0).
(b) Determine the maximum area and the dimensions that give it.
Axis x=\frac{0+20}{2}=10
A=10(40-20)=200
Maximum area 200\text{ m}^2: 10 m by 20 m (the 20 m side along the wall).
(c) State the domain and range of A in this context.
D: \{x\mid 0<x<20,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
R: \{A\mid 0<A\le200,\ A\in\mathbb{R}\}
✓

Key ideas

Three forms, one a. Vertex form a(x-p)^2+q, general form ax^2+bx+c, factored form a(x-r)(x-s). To reach general form, expand and collect like terms.
Factored form. Zeros r and s; axis x=\frac{r+s}{2} (halfway between them); substitute it for the vertex; x=0 for the y-intercept.
General form. y-intercept c; axis x=-\frac{b}{2a}; substitute it for the vertex; a>0 minimum, a<0 maximum. Zeros that aren't integers: vertex form and square roots (exact), or the calculator's zero.
The best form. Vertex form for the vertex and range, general form for the y-intercept, factored form for the zeros. An unknown coefficient: the axis formula or a known point.
