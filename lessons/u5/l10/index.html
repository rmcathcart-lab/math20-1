Race to 21

Two players take turns counting up from 1. On your turn you say one, two or three numbers (“1”, or “1, 2”, or “1, 2, 3”). Whoever says 21 wins.

Would you rather go first or second? What is your plan to win?

?
Play a few rounds with the person beside you. Is there a number you always want to say?
Go first and say “1”. To say 21 you need to say 17 first: then your opponent can only reach 18, 19 or 20, and you finish. For the same reason you want 13, 9, 5 and 1: the winning numbers go up by 4.
After every turn of your opponent’s, make the two turns add to 4: they say one number, you say three; they say two, you say two.

Finding the Vertex Algebraically

The maximum or minimum value of a quadratic function is the y-coordinate of its vertex; it happens at the x-coordinate. There are three algebraic ways to find the vertex. Use whichever suits the form of the function.

Vertex formy=a(x-p)^2+q(complete the square if needed): vertex \textcolor{#c27a0e}{(p,\ q)}.
General formy=ax^2+bx+cthe axis is x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}; substitute this x to get the y-coordinate.
Factored formy=a(x-r)(x-s)the axis is midway between the zeros, x=\dfrac{r+s}{2}; substitute.
If a<0 the parabola opens down and the vertex is a maximum; if a>0 it opens up and the vertex is a minimum.

Completing the square on y=ax^2+bx+c once and for all gives the vertex formula \left(-\dfrac{b}{2a},\ \dfrac{4ac-b^2}{4a}\right). It is a shortcut when the numbers are fractions; substituting x=-\frac{b}{2a} always gives the same y-value.

Example 1

The vertex, and the maximum or minimum value

Determine the vertex of the graph of each function, and state the maximum or minimum value.

(a) y=3(x+2)^2-5
Vertex form: vertex \boldsymbol{(-2,\ -5)}.
a=3>0: minimum value -5 (at x=-2).
(b) y=-2x^2+12x-7
x=-\frac{12}{2(-2)}=3
y=-2(9)+36-7=11
Vertex \boldsymbol{(3,\ 11)}; a<0: maximum value 11.
(c) y=(x-3)(x+7)
Zeros 3 and -7: axis x=\frac{3+(-7)}{2}=-2.
y=(-2-3)(-2+7)=(-5)(5)=-25
Vertex \boldsymbol{(-2,\ -25)}; a=1>0: minimum value -25.
(d) y=2x^2+5x-1
x=-\frac{5}{2(2)}=-\frac{5}{4}
y=\frac{4ac-b^2}{4a}=\frac{4(2)(-1)-25}{8}=-\frac{33}{8}
Vertex \boldsymbol{\left(-\frac{5}{4},\ -\frac{33}{8}\right)}; minimum value -\frac{33}{8}.
Example 2

A ball is hit upward

A ball is hit upward. Its height in metres after t seconds is h(t)=-5t^2+30t+2.

(a) Complete the square to write h(t) in vertex form.
h(t)=-5(t^2-6t)+2
=-5(t^2-6t+9-9)+2
=-5(t-3)^2+45+2
\boldsymbol{h(t)=-5(t-3)^2+47}
(b) How high is the ball after 1 s?
h(1)=-5(1-3)^2+47=-20+47=\boldsymbol{27} m
(c) What is the maximum height, and when is it reached?
Vertex (3,\ 47), a<0: a maximum height of 47 m after 3 s.
(d) How high was the ball when it was hit?
h(0)=\boldsymbol{2} m
(e) When does the ball hit the ground? Answer to the nearest hundredth of a second.
-5(t-3)^2+47=0\Rightarrow(t-3)^2=\frac{47}{5}=9.4
t-3=\pm\sqrt{9.4}\Rightarrow t=3\pm3.066
\boldsymbol{t\approx6.07} s (t\approx-0.07 is rejected: it is before the hit).

Setting Up the Function

In most problems the function is not given: you build it.

1Let \textcolor{#12805a}{x} be one quantity (draw a diagram when there is a shape).
2Write every other quantity in terms of x.
3Write the function for the quantity to be made greatest or least.
4Find the vertex algebraically.
5Check that the x-value makes sense (lengths positive, counts whole).
6Answer every part of the question in a sentence, with units.
Example 3

Number problems

(a) Two numbers have a sum of 30. Determine the numbers whose product is as large as possible, and that product.
Let one number be x; the other is 30-x.
P(x)=x(30-x)=-x^2+30x
Zeros 0 and 30: axis x=15.
P(15)=15(15)=225
The numbers are 15 and 15; the maximum product is 225.
(b) One number is 4 more than three times another. Determine the numbers whose product is as small as possible, and that product.
Let the number be x; the other is 3x+4.
P(x)=x(3x+4)=3x^2+4x
x=-\frac{4}{2(3)}=-\frac{2}{3}; other: 3\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)+4=2
P=-\frac{2}{3}(2)=-\frac{4}{3}
The numbers are -\frac{2}{3} and 2; the minimum product is -\frac{4}{3}.
3

Area Problems

With a fixed length of fencing, the area of a rectangle depends on its shape. Write one side as \textcolor{#12805a}{x}, use the fencing total to write the other side in terms of x, and multiply. Only the sides that are fenced use fencing: a side along a wall or a river does not, and a dividing fence inside does.

Example 4

A pen beside a river

A farmer has 240 m of fencing to make a rectangular pen beside a river. No fence is needed along the river, and a fence perpendicular to the river divides the pen into two parts, as shown.

(a) Let x be the length of each fence perpendicular to the river. Show that the area is A(x)=240x-3x^2.
Three fences of length x use 3x m, so the side parallel to the river is 240-3x.
A(x)=x(240-3x)=240x-3x^2
(b) Complete the square to find the maximum area.
A(x)=-3(x^2-80x)
=-3(x^2-80x+1\,600-1\,600)
=-3(x-40)^2+4\,800
Maximum area 4\,800\text{ m}^2, when x=40.
(c) State the dimensions of the pen with the maximum area.
x=40 m; 240-3(40)=120 m.
The pen is 40 m by 120 m.
Check: 3(40)+120=240 m of fence.
Math HistoryCarthage · traditionally 814 BCE

Dido and the oxhide

Legend says that Dido, a princess from Tyre, was offered only as much land on the coast of North Africa as one oxhide could enclose. She cut the hide into thin strips, joined them into one long cord and enclosed a whole hill, where she founded Carthage. Virgil tells the story in the Aeneid.

Modern retellings make the sea one side of her land, just like today’s river: a fixed length of “fence” and none along the water.

Dido’s cord is 1\,000 m long and the sea is one side. Which rectangle encloses the most land?
A(x)=x(1\,000-2x): zeros 0 and 500, so x=250.
250 m by 500 m: 125\,000\text{ m}^2.
Example 5

A rectangle in a triangle

A rectangle is drawn inside a right triangle with legs 12 cm and 8 cm. Two sides of the rectangle lie along the legs, and its fourth corner is on the hypotenuse, as shown.

(a) Let the width of the rectangle be x cm. Use similar triangles to show that its height is y=8-\frac{2}{3}x.
The small triangle to the right of the rectangle has base 12-x and height y, and is similar to the whole triangle:
\dfrac{y}{12-x}=\dfrac{8}{12}, so y=\frac{2}{3}(12-x)=8-\frac{2}{3}x.
(b) Write the area A(x) of the rectangle and find the dimensions that give the greatest area.
A(x)=x\left(8-\frac{2}{3}x\right)=-\frac{2}{3}x^2+8x
Zeros 0 and 12: axis x=6.
y=8-4=4; A=6(4)=24\text{ cm}^2.
The rectangle is 6 cm by 4 cm.
(c) Compare the greatest area with the area of the triangle.
Triangle: \frac{1}{2}(12)(8)=48\text{ cm}^2.
The largest rectangle fills exactly half the triangle, with each side half of a leg.

Revenue Problems

Let x be the number of price changes, write the price and the number sold in terms of x, and multiply: R(x)=(\text{price})(\text{number sold}). Leaving R(x) as a product of two linear factors is useful: each factor gives a zero, and the maximum is midway between the zeros.

Example 6

Concert tickets

A theatre sells 900 concert tickets at \$40 each. For every \$5 decrease in price, it expects to sell 150 more tickets.

(a) Let x be the number of \$5 decreases. Write the revenue function.
Price: 40-5x; tickets: 900+150x.
\boldsymbol{R(x)=(40-5x)(900+150x)}
(b) Use the zeros to find the price that gives the maximum revenue.
40-5x=0\Rightarrow x=8;   900+150x=0\Rightarrow x=-6.
Axis: x=\frac{8+(-6)}{2}=1. One decrease: price $35.
(c) State the number of tickets sold and the maximum revenue. Compare with the current revenue.
900+150=1\,050 tickets; R(1)=35(1\,050)=\$36\,750.
Now: 40(900)=\$36\,000, so $750 more.
(d) Check part (b) with x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}.
R(x)=36\,000+6\,000x-4\,500x-750x^2
=-750x^2+1\,500x+36\,000
x=-\frac{1\,500}{2(-750)}=1\ \checkmark

When the Question Asks for a Given Value

Not every question in a problem asks for a maximum. “When is the ball 12 m high?” or “What are the dimensions?” means: set the function equal to the given value, write the equation in standard form, and solve it by factoring or by the quadratic formula. Two admissible answers are common (on the way up and on the way down); a negative discriminant means the value is never reached.

Example 7

A given value

(a) A stone is thrown straight up. Its height in metres after t seconds is h(t)=18t-5t^2. When is it 12 m high? Answer to the nearest tenth of a second and explain the two answers. Can the stone reach 20 m?
18t-5t^2=12\Rightarrow5t^2-18t+12=0
t=\frac{18\pm\sqrt{324-240}}{10}=\frac{18\pm\sqrt{84}}{10}
t\approx0.9 s (going up) and t\approx2.7 s (coming down).
20 m: 5t^2-18t+20=0 has b^2-4ac=324-400<0, no real roots. No: the maximum is h(1.8)=16.2 m.
(b) The two shorter sides of a right-triangular garden bed have a total length of 17 m, and the longest side is 13 m. Determine the lengths of the two shorter sides.
Let one side be x; the other is 17-x.
x^2+(17-x)^2=13^2
2x^2-34x+289=169
2x^2-34x+120=0\Rightarrow x^2-17x+60=0
(x-5)(x-12)=0, x=5 or x=12.
The shorter sides are 5 m and 12 m.

Key ideas

The vertex, three ways. Vertex form: read (p,\ q). General form: x=-\frac{b}{2a}, then substitute (or the vertex formula). Factored form: midway between the zeros. a<0: maximum; a>0: minimum.
Build the function. Let x be one quantity, write the others in terms of x, write the function to be made greatest or least, find its vertex, check it makes sense, answer in a sentence with units.
Areas and revenue. Only fenced sides use fencing; an inside fence does. Revenue =(\text{price})(\text{number sold}): keep it factored, and the maximum is midway between the zeros.
A given value. Set the function equal to it, standard form, factor or the quadratic formula. Often two answers (up and down); a negative discriminant means never.
