A new game gives you a login bonus every day for 30 days. You have to pick one plan.

Plan A: 30 gems every day.
Plan B: 1 gem on day 1, 3 gems on day 2, 5 gems on day 3, … (2 more each day).

The Graph of y=x^2

Every quadratic function's graph is the graph of y=x^2 moved, stretched or flipped. A quadratic function in vertex form (also called standard form) is y=a(x-p)^2+q, a\ne0. In this lesson we see what each of a, p and q does to the base graph y=x^2.

The domain of a function is the set of all its x-values, and the range is the set of all its y-values. We write them in set notation: \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\} means “all real numbers x”, and \{y\mid y\ge 0,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\} means “all real numbers y that are at least 0”.

Translations: y=(x-p)^2+q

The parameter q. In y=x^2+q, the number q is added to every y-value, so the graph moves q units up (q>0) or down (q<0). The vertex moves to (0,\ q).

The parameter p. In y=(x-3)^2, y=0 when x=3, y=1 when x=2 or 4, and so on: every y-value of y=x^2 now happens 3 units further right. So y=(x-p)^2 moves the graph p units right (p>0) or left (p<0). Watch the sign: y=(x+3)^2=(x-(-3))^2 has p=-3 and moves the graph 3 units left.

Key idea
y=(x-p)^2+q is the graph of y=x^2 translated p units horizontally and q units vertically.
Vertex (p,\ q);   axis of symmetry x=p;   the shape (and so the step pattern 1,\ 3,\ 5) does not change.
If the equation has a constant on the left, as in y-2=(x-3)^2, first move it right: y=(x-3)^2+2.

A translation does not change the domain, \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}. Since these parabolas open up, the minimum value is q and the range is \{y\mid y\ge q,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Vertical Stretches and Reflections: y=ax^2

In y=ax^2 every y-value of y=x^2 is multiplied by a. This is a vertical stretch by a factor of |a| about the x-axis: points on the x-axis do not move, and every other point moves away from (or toward) the x-axis.

  • |a|>1: the parabola is narrower (an expansion).   0<|a|<1: the parabola is wider (a compression).
  • a<0: the graph is also reflected in the x-axis. It opens down and the vertex is a maximum.

Putting It Together: y=a(x-p)^2+q

Key idea
Compared with y=x^2, the graph of y=a(x-p)^2+q has:
  • a vertical stretch by a factor of |a| about the x-axis, and a reflection in the x-axis if a<0;
  • a horizontal translation of p units (right if p>0, left if p<0);
  • a vertical translation of q units (up if q>0, down if q<0).
Vertex (p,\ q); axis x=p. If a>0 it opens up and q is the minimum value; if a<0 it opens down and q is the maximum value.
Order: stretch and reflect first, then translate.   Each point (x,\ y) of y=x^2 maps to (x+p,\ ay+q).

When describing a graph, list the stretch, then the reflection, then the translations. (Lesson 3 studies the domain, range and intercepts of these functions.)

Example 7

Stretch first, or translate first?

To graph y=2(x+4)^2-3 from y=x^2, Mila stretches first and then translates. Jonah translates first and then stretches. Follow the vertex (0,\ 0) and the point (1,\ 1) through each method. Who is correct?

Mila: stretch by 2, then 4 left and 3 down
(0,\ 0)\to(0,\ 0)\to(-4,\ -3)
(1,\ 1)\to(1,\ 2)\to(-3,\ -1)
Jonah: 4 left and 3 down, then stretch by 2
(0,\ 0)\to(-4,\ -3)\to(-4,\ -6)
(1,\ 1)\to(-3,\ -2)\to(-3,\ -4)
Who is correct?
The vertex of y=2(x+4)^2-3 is (-4,\ -3), and 2(-3+4)^2-3=-1, so (-3,\ -1) is on it: Mila is correct. Stretching about the x-axis after the vertical translation also doubles the -3, which moves the vertex to (-4,\ -6).

Sketching with the Step Pattern

For y=x^2, moving 1, 2, 3 units left or right of the vertex, y goes up 1, 4, 9 (steps of 1,\ 3,\ 5). For y=a(x-p)^2+q every one of these is multiplied by a.

Key idea
To sketch y=a(x-p)^2+q: plot the vertex (p,\ q). From it, move 1 right (and 1 left) and a\times1 vertically; then 1 more and a\times3; then 1 more and a\times5. Join the points with a smooth U-shaped curve.
For a fraction such as a=\frac{1}{2}, move 2 across and a\times4 vertically, so the points land on the grid.
Check a sketch. Enter the function in Y= and look at the table from the vertex: for y=2(x-3)^2-5, from x=3 the y-values go -5,\ -3,\ 3,\ 13: steps of 2,\ 6,\ 10. (ZOOM6 shows the graph.)

Key ideas

The base graph y=x^2. Vertex (0,\ 0), axis x=0, domain \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}, range \{y\mid y\ge 0,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}. From the vertex: up 1,\ 3,\ 5 for each unit across.
Translations. y=(x-p)^2+q: p right (left if negative), q up (down if negative). Vertex (p,\ q), axis x=p. Watch the sign: (x+3)^2 means 3 left.
Stretches and reflections. y=ax^2: every y times a. |a|>1 narrower, 0<|a|<1 wider; a<0 reflects in the x-axis (opens down, maximum).
All together. Stretch and reflect first, then translate: (x,\ y)\to(x+p,\ ay+q). Sketch from the vertex with steps a\times1, a\times3, a\times5.
