The Chocolate Bars

A chocolate maker moulds its bars in a growing pattern: Bar 1, Bar 2, Bar 3, and so on.

How many squares are in Bar 10? Which bar has exactly 100 squares?

?
Compare with the person beside you. How do you know?
Bar 10 has 10\times11=110 squares, and no bar has exactly 100! Bar n is n rows of n+1 squares: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, 42, 56, 72, 90, 110, … Bar 9 has 90 and Bar 10 has 110, so 100 is skipped.
The jumps grow (4, 6, 8, 10, …), so more and more numbers are skipped.

Unit 5 Review

The whole unit on one page: the three forms of a quadratic function and what each one shows at a glance, then mixed examples in the style of the unit test. One question runs through all of it: which form helps here? Vertex form gives the vertex, general form the y-intercept, factored form the zeros. Calculator allowed.

I can…
  • state the vertex, axis of symmetry, direction of opening, maximum or minimum value, domain, range and intercepts from any form
  • describe how y=x^2 is transformed, and sketch a parabola with the step pattern
  • change forms: complete the square, factor and expand, and write an equation from the vertex or the zeros and a point
  • solve a quadratic equation by square roots, factoring, the quadratic formula or graphing, and use the discriminant
  • solve maximum and minimum problems from a given model or one I write

Unit Summary

FormRead directly from the equationFind the rest
Vertex formy=a(x-p)^2+qVertex (p,\ q); axis of symmetry x=p; opens up if a>0 (minimum value q), down if a<0 (maximum value q).y-intercept: let x=0. x-intercepts: solve a(x-p)^2+q=0 by square roots.
General formy=ax^2+bx+cy-intercept (0,\ c); direction of opening from a.Axis x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}, then substitute for q (or complete the square). Zeros by factoring or the formula.
Factored formy=a(x-r)(x-s)Zeros r and s (x-intercepts (r,\ 0) and (s,\ 0)); direction of opening from a.Axis x=\dfrac{r+s}{2} (midway between the zeros), then substitute for q. Expand for general form.
Transformations of y=x^2 to y=a(x-p)^2+q. |a|>1: vertical stretch by a factor of |a|; 0<|a|<1: vertical compression; a<0: reflection in the x-axis. p: horizontal translation (p>0 right, p<0 left). q: vertical translation. Mapping: (x,\ y)\to(x+p,\ ay+q). Step pattern from the vertex: over 1, 2, 3, … the graph moves 1a, 3a, 5a, … vertically.
Domain and range. Domain \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}. Range \{y\mid y\ge q,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\} if a>0, \{y\mid y\le q,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\} if a<0.
Number of x-intercepts: a and q opposite signs → 2; q=0 → 1; same signs → 0.
Writing the equation. Vertex and a point: substitute the point into y=a(x-p)^2+q and solve for a. Zeros and a point: substitute into y=a(x-r)(x-s). In a context, choose a convenient origin (at the vertex, or at one end on the ground) and say where it is.
Completing the square y=ax^2+bx+c\to y=a(x-p)^2+q
  1. Factor a from the x^2 and x terms.
  2. Inside the bracket add and subtract (half the coefficient of x)².
  3. Move the subtracted number out of the bracket by multiplying it by a.
  4. Write the perfect square and simplify.
Check: the y-intercept must still be c.
Quadratic formula. ax^2+bx+c=0, a\ne0: x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}. Give exact answers in simplest radical form, or round as asked.
Discriminant b^2-4ac: >0 two distinct real roots (two x-intercepts); =0 one real (equal) root (vertex on the x-axis); <0 no real roots (no x-intercepts).
Choosing a method: factor if it factors; square roots for a(x-p)^2+q=0 or ax^2+c=0; the formula for anything else; graphing (zero or intersect) to check or for decimal answers.
Max/min problems. Given a model: the vertex gives the maximum or minimum (calculator: maximum/minimum); a zero gives when the value is 0; intersect with a horizontal line gives when it reaches a value. Creating a model: define the variable, write the quantity as a quadratic (area = length × width; revenue = price × number sold), find the vertex (complete the square, x=-\frac{b}{2a}, or midway between the zeros) and answer in context with units. State the domain and range that make sense.

Analyzing Quadratic Functions

Pick the form that answers the question fastest. Vertex form is the quickest for a sketch: plot the vertex, then use the step pattern 1a, 3a, 5a on both sides of the axis.

Example 1

Vertex form: transformations, characteristics and a sketch

Consider the function y=-2(x+3)^2+8.

(a) Describe how the graph of y=x^2 is transformed to give this graph.
Vertical stretch by a factor of 2, reflection in the x-axis, horizontal translation 3 units left, vertical translation 8 units up.
(b) State each characteristic.
Vertex(-3,\ 8)
Axis of symmetryx=-3
Direction of openingdown (a<0)
Maximum or minimum valuemaximum value 8
Domain\{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}
Range\{y\mid y\le8,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}
y-intercept-2(3)^2+8=-10
x-intercepts-5 and -1
x-intercepts: -2(x+3)^2+8=0\Rightarrow(x+3)^2=4\Rightarrow x+3=\pm2\Rightarrow \boldsymbol{x=-1} or \boldsymbol{x=-5}.
(c) Sketch the graph using the step pattern.
From (-3,\ 8): over 1, down 2; over 2, down 6 more; over 3, down 10 more.
Points (-4,\ 6), (-2,\ 6), (-5,\ 0), (-1,\ 0), (-6,\ -10), (0,\ -10).

The parabola lab: Hit the Target a whole-class game

Set a, p and q with the buttons and every characteristic updates. In the game a dashed target appears: read its vertex and one step off the vertex, call out a, p and q, and see if we hit it.

Example 2

General form to vertex form and factored form

The function y=2x^2-12x+10 is in general form.

(a) Write it in vertex form by completing the square.
y=2(x^2-6x)+10
y=2(x^2-6x+9-9)+10
y=2(x-3)^2-18+10
\boldsymbol{y=2(x-3)^2-8}
(b) Write it in factored form.
y=2(x^2-6x+5)
\boldsymbol{y=2(x-1)(x-5)}
(c) Use the most convenient form to state the vertex, the axis of symmetry, the y-intercept, the zeros and the range. Check the axis with x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}.
Vertex form: vertex \boldsymbol{(3,\ -8)}, axis \boldsymbol{x=3}, range \boldsymbol{\{y\mid y\ge-8,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}} (opens up, minimum value -8).
General form: y-intercept \boldsymbol{10}. Factored form: zeros \boldsymbol{1} and \boldsymbol{5} (and 3 is midway between them).
Check: x=-\dfrac{-12}{2(2)}=3\ \checkmark

Writing the Equation of a Quadratic Function

Know the vertex? Start from y=a(x-p)^2+q. Know the zeros? Start from y=a(x-r)(x-s). Either way one more point gives a.

Example 3

From the vertex or the zeros, and a point

Write the equation of each quadratic function.

(a) Vertex (2,\ -5), passing through (4,\ 3). Use vertex form.
y=a(x-2)^2-5
3=a(4-2)^2-5
8=4a\Rightarrow a=2
\boldsymbol{y=2(x-2)^2-5}
(b) Zeros -1 and 5, passing through (3,\ 16). Give factored and general form, and the vertex.
y=a(x+1)(x-5); 16=a(4)(-2)\Rightarrow a=-2
\boldsymbol{y=-2(x+1)(x-5)=-2x^2+8x+10}
Axis x=\frac{-1+5}{2}=2; y=-2(3)(-3)=18
Vertex \boldsymbol{(2,\ 18)}
Example 4

The park arch

A parabolic arch over a park entrance is 24 m wide at the ground and 9 m high at its centre.

(a) Place the origin at the left end of the arch on the ground. Write the equation of the arch in factored form.
Zeros 0 and 24, vertex (12,\ 9): y=a(x-0)(x-24)
9=a(12)(-12)\Rightarrow a=-\dfrac{9}{144}=-\dfrac{1}{16}
\boldsymbol{y=-\tfrac{1}{16}x(x-24)}
(b) How high is the arch 3 m in from one end, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
y=-\frac{1}{16}(3)(3-24)=\frac{63}{16}=3.9375. About 3.9 m.
(c) Write the equation of the same arch with the origin on the ground directly below the top of the arch.
Vertex (0,\ 9), zeros \pm12 (the shape, and so a, does not change): \boldsymbol{y=-\tfrac{1}{16}x^2+9}.
Check: -\frac{1}{16}(144)+9=0\ \checkmark

Move the origin, keep the arch

The arch stays where it is; only the axes move. The equation changes, but a does not.

Example 5

Choose a method

Solve. Choose an efficient method and say which one you used.

(a) 3(x-2)^2=27
Square roots: (x-2)^2=9
x-2=\pm3
\boldsymbol{x=5} or \boldsymbol{x=-1}
(b) 2x^2+3x=2
Factoring: 2x^2+3x-2=0
(2x-1)(x+2)=0
\boldsymbol{x=\frac{1}{2}} or \boldsymbol{x=-2}
(c) x^2-6x+4=0 (exact)
Formula (a=1, b=-6, c=4):
x=\dfrac{6\pm\sqrt{36-16}}{2}=\dfrac{6\pm\sqrt{20}}{2}
=\dfrac{6\pm2\sqrt{5}}{2}=\boldsymbol{3\pm\sqrt{5}}
(d) 3x^2+2x-4=0 (to the nearest hundredth)
Formula: x=\dfrac{-2\pm\sqrt{4+48}}{6}=\dfrac{-2\pm\sqrt{52}}{6}=\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{13}}{3}
\boldsymbol{x\approx0.87} or \boldsymbol{x\approx-1.54}

Example 6

The discriminant

(a) Use the discriminant to determine the nature of the roots of (i) 4x^2-12x+9=0 and (ii) 2x^2+x+5=0.
(i) b^2-4ac=144-144=0: one real (equal) root. (ii) b^2-4ac=1-40=-39<0: no real roots.
(b) For what values of k does x^2+kx+9=0 have equal roots? two distinct real roots?
b^2-4ac=k^2-36. Equal roots: k^2-36=0\Rightarrow \boldsymbol{k=\pm6}.
Two distinct real roots: k^2>36\Rightarrow \boldsymbol{k<-6} or \boldsymbol{k>6}.
(c) For what values of k does kx^2-6x+3=0 have no real roots? Describe the graph of y=kx^2-6x+3 for these values.
b^2-4ac=36-12k<0\Rightarrow \boldsymbol{k>3}.
The parabola opens up (k>0) and its vertex is above the x-axis, so it has no x-intercepts.

Turn the k dial

As k changes, the parabola moves and the discriminant changes with it: two crossings, one touch, or none.

Maximum and Minimum Problems

A vertex answers “how high?” or “how much?”, a zero answers “when is it 0?”, and an intersection with a horizontal line answers “when does it reach this value?”. Answer in a sentence, with units.

Example 7

The balcony throw

A ball is thrown upward from a balcony. Its height, h metres, after t seconds is h=-4.9t^2+19.6t+1.5. Use a graphing calculator (graph the function, then use maximum, zero and intersect).

(a) What is the maximum height, and when is it reached?
Maximum: (2,\ 21.1). The ball reaches 21.1 m after 2 s.
(Algebra: t=-\frac{19.6}{2(-4.9)}=2.)
(b) When does the ball hit the ground, to the nearest hundredth of a second?
Zero: \boldsymbol{t\approx4.08} s. (The other zero, t\approx-0.08, is inadmissible.)
(c) For how long is the ball higher than 15 m, to the nearest hundredth of a second?
Graph y=15 too and use intersect: t\approx0.88 and t\approx3.12.
3.116-0.884\approx\boldsymbol{2.23} s (subtract the unrounded values)
(d) State the domain and range of the function in this context.
Domain \boldsymbol{\{t\mid 0\le t\le4.08,\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}}
Range \boldsymbol{\{h\mid 0\le h\le21.1,\ h\in\mathbb{R}\}}
On the calculator. Type -4.9x^2+19.6x+1.5 in Y_1 and 15 in Y_2, set the WINDOW to 0\le x\le5, -5\le y\le25, then 2ndCALC: 4 maximum gives (2, 21.1), 2 zero gives 4.0751…, and 5 intersect gives 0.8842… and 3.1157…
Example 8

Theatre tickets

A theatre sells 400 tickets a night at \$12 each. A survey shows that for every \$0.50 increase in price, 10 fewer tickets are sold. What price gives the maximum revenue, and what is that revenue?

Let x be the number of \$0.50 increases. Price =12+0.5x; tickets =400-10x.
R=(12+0.5x)(400-10x)=4\,800-120x+200x-5x^2=-5x^2+80x+4\,800
R=-5(x^2-16x)+4\,800=-5(x^2-16x+64-64)+4\,800=-5(x-8)^2+320+4\,800=-5(x-8)^2+5\,120
Vertex (8,\ 5\,120): 8 increases, so the price is 12+0.5(8)=\boldsymbol{\$16} (with 320 tickets sold).
The maximum revenue is $5 120. (Check: the zeros are x=-24 and x=40; midway is x=8\ \checkmark)

Key ideas

Three forms. Vertex form → vertex (p,\ q), axis, max/min, range. General form → y-intercept c, axis x=-\frac{b}{2a}. Factored form → zeros, axis midway. Domain is always \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}.
Change forms. Complete the square (multiply the subtracted number by a), factor, or expand. Write an equation from the vertex or the zeros and one point: substitute and solve for a.
Solve. Square roots, factoring, the formula, or graphing to check. Discriminant b^2-4ac: positive → 2 roots, zero → 1, negative → none.
Max/min. Vertex → the maximum or minimum; zero → when it is 0; intersect → when it reaches a value. Build the model, find the vertex, answer in context with units and a sensible domain.
