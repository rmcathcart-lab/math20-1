The Doughnut Line

Draw a doughnut from above: its outline is two circles, one inside the other.

Now lay a ruler anywhere on the drawing and draw one long straight line.

How many points can the line share with the outline? Which numbers are possible?

?
Sketch a doughnut and try it. Can you get every number from 0 up?
0, 1, 2, 3 or 4: every number up to 4, but never 5 or more (a line meets a circle at most twice).
0: miss the doughnut. 2: cut through the dough only. 4: cut straight through the hole.
The sneaky ones are 1 and 3: the line has to just touch a circle without crossing it, grazing the outside edge (1), or crossing the outside and grazing the edge of the hole (3). Most people only find the even numbers!

The Discriminant

The quadratic formula hides a shortcut. The expression under its radical, b^2-4ac, is the discriminant: its sign tells how many real roots an equation has, and how many times the graph meets the x-axis, without solving and without graphing.

I can…
  • use b^2-4ac to decide whether a quadratic equation has two distinct, two equal or no real roots, and how many x-intercepts its graph has
  • decide whether the roots are rational or irrational, and factor when they are rational
  • find the values of an unknown coefficient that give a certain number of roots (an equation or an inequality)
  • show that an equation has real roots for every value of a parameter
  • use the discriminant to decide whether a quantity can reach a certain value

1

What the Radical in the Formula Tells Us

In x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}, the value under the radical decides what kind of roots there are. If it is positive, the + and the - give two different numbers; if it is 0, they give the same number; if it is negative, there is no real square root.

2

The Discriminant and the Nature of the Roots

The expression b^2-4ac is the discriminant of ax^2+bx+c=0. It tells the nature of the roots (how many real roots, and whether they are equal) without solving the equation or drawing the graph.
Key idea
DiscriminantRoots of ax^2+bx+c=0Graph of y=ax^2+bx+c
b^2-4ac>0two distinct (unequal) real rootstwo x-intercepts
b^2-4ac=0two equal real roots (one distinct root, a double root)one x-intercept: the vertex is on the x-axis
b^2-4ac<0no real roots (also called non-real roots)no x-intercepts

Always put the equation in standard form before reading a, b and c. “Real roots” (with no other word) means b^2-4ac\ge0: two distinct or two equal.

Negative b? Brackets! The calculator squares before it negates: (−)5x² gives -25. For 3x^2-5x-2=0, type (-5)^2-4(3)(-2) with the brackets: 49.

3

Rational or Irrational Roots

When a, b and c are rational and the discriminant is a perfect square (0,\ 1,\ 4,\ 9,\ 16,\ \dots), \sqrt{b^2-4ac} is rational, so the roots are rational; with integer coefficients the trinomial then factors over the integers. When the discriminant is positive but not a perfect square, the roots are irrational and the trinomial does not factor over the integers.
Perfect square? Take the square root: a whole number means yes. \sqrt{529}=23 (perfect square), but \sqrt{57}\approx7.55 (not).

Finding an Unknown Coefficient

Write the discriminant in terms of the unknown, then use >0 (two distinct real roots), =0 (equal roots), <0 (no real roots) or \ge0 (real roots) and solve. Reverse the inequality sign when you divide by a negative number. If the unknown is in the x^2 coefficient, it cannot make that coefficient 0 (the equation would not be quadratic).
Example 4

The unknown is c

For what values of k does 2x^2-6x+k=0 have

(a) two distinct real roots?
b^2-4ac=36-8k
36-8k>0
-8k>-36, so k<\frac92
(b) two equal real roots?
36-8k=0
k=\frac92
(c) no real roots?
36-8k<0
k>\frac92
(d) Determine the equal root when k=\frac92.
2x^2-6x+\frac92=0; multiply by 2: 4x^2-12x+9=0\Rightarrow(2x-3)^2=0\Rightarrow x=\frac32. (Or x=\frac{-b}{2a}=\frac64=\frac32, since the radical is 0.)

When the unknown is b

When the unknown is b, the discriminant contains k^2. For example, k^2=36 when k=\pm6; k^2>36 when k<-6 or k>6; and k^2<36 when -6<k<6 (the numbers between -6 and 6 are the ones whose squares are less than 36).

k^2>36: outsidek^2=36: k=\pm6k^2<36: between
Example 5

The unknown is b, or a

(a) For what values of k does x^2+kx+9=0 have equal roots? Determine the root for each value. For what values of k are there two distinct real roots? No real roots?
b^2-4ac=k^2-36
Equal: k^2=36, k=\pm6.
k=6: x^2+6x+9=(x+3)^2, root x=-3.
k=-6: x^2-6x+9=(x-3)^2, root x=3.
Two distinct: k^2>36, so k<-6 or k>6.
None: k^2<36, so -6<k<6.
(b) For what values of k does kx^2+6x-3=0 have no real roots?
36-4(k)(-3)=36+12k<0
12k<-36, so k<-3 (these values are not 0, so the equation stays quadratic).
(c) For what values of k does (k-1)x^2+4x+2=0 have real roots?
16-4(k-1)(2)\ge0
16-8k+8\ge0\Rightarrow24\ge8k\Rightarrow k\le3
and k\ne1 (otherwise it is not quadratic): k\le3, k\ne1.
5

Showing That the Roots Are Always Real

To show that an equation has real roots for every value of a parameter, write its discriminant and show it can never be negative. A square is never negative: (k-2)^2\ge0, and k^2+16>0.
Example 6

For every value of k

(a) Show that x^2+kx-4=0 has two distinct real roots for every real number k.
b^2-4ac=k^2-4(1)(-4)=k^2+16
k^2\ge0, so k^2+16\ge16>0 for every k.
The discriminant is always positive: two distinct real roots.
(b) Show that the roots of x^2-(k+2)x+2k=0 are real and rational for every integer k. When are they equal?
b^2-4ac=(k+2)^2-8k=k^2-4k+4=(k-2)^2
This is never negative, so the roots are real, and it is a perfect square, so they are rational. (In fact x=2 and x=k.)
Equal when (k-2)^2=0: k=2.
6

Problems: Is It Possible?

To ask whether a quantity given by a quadratic can reach a certain value, set the expression equal to that value, write the equation in standard form, and look at the discriminant: a negative discriminant means the value is never reached; zero means it is reached exactly once (at the vertex: the maximum or minimum).
Example 7

A ball thrown upward

A ball is thrown upward. Its height after t seconds is h=-5t^2+20t+1 metres. Use the discriminant to answer each question.

(a) Does the ball reach a height of 25 m?
-5t^2+20t+1=25
-5t^2+20t-24=0
400-4(-5)(-24)=400-480=-80<0
No real roots: it never reaches 25 m.
(b) Does it reach 16 m? How many times?
-5t^2+20t-15=0
400-300=100>0
Yes, twice (going up and coming down; t=1 s and t=3 s).
(c) Determine its maximum height.
Height H is reached when -5t^2+20t+(1-H)=0 has real roots:
400+20(1-H)\ge0
420\ge20H, so H\le21.
Maximum height 21 m (equal roots, at t=2 s).
✓

Key ideas

The discriminant b^2-4ac. Positive: two distinct real roots, two x-intercepts. Zero: two equal roots, the vertex on the axis. Negative: no real roots, no x-intercepts.
Standard form first. Then read a, b, c (brackets for negatives on the calculator). A perfect square discriminant: rational roots, and the trinomial factors.
Finding k. Write b^2-4ac in terms of k; use >0, =0, <0 or \ge0. Flip the sign when dividing by a negative; k^2=36 gives \pm6; k can't make a=0.
Always real; is it possible? A discriminant like k^2+16 or (k-2)^2 is never negative. Set a quantity equal to a value: negative means never, zero means once (the maximum or minimum).
