The Busy Fly

Two trains on the same track are 100 km apart, rolling toward each other at 50 km/h each. A fly zips from the front of one train to the other at 75 km/h, turns around, zips back, and keeps going until the trains stop nose to nose.

How far does the fly travel?

?
Is there a shortcut? Compare with the person beside you.
75 km. Don't follow the zigzags! The gap of 100 km closes at 50+50=100 km/h, so the trains meet after exactly 1 hour. The fly flies the whole hour at 75 km/h: 75 km. (Adding up every zigzag also gives 75 km, but that takes an infinite series. A famous story says John von Neumann answered this instantly; when told most people sum the series, he said that is what he had done.)

The Quadratic Formula

Many quadratic equations, such as 3x^2-12x+7=0, do not factor. Completing the square always works, and doing it once with the letters \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{a}, \textcolor{#c27a0e}{b} and \textcolor{#12805a}{c} gives a formula that solves every quadratic equation.

Deriving the Quadratic Formula

Many quadratic equations, such as 3x^2-12x+7=0, cannot be factored over the integers. Completing the square (Lesson 6) always works: rewrite the equation as (x-p)^2=k and take the square root of both sides. Doing this once with the letters a, b and c gives a formula that solves every quadratic equation.
The quadratic formula
The roots of \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{a}x^2+\textcolor{#c27a0e}{b}x+\textcolor{#12805a}{c}=0,\ a\ne0, are x=\dfrac{-\textcolor{#c27a0e}{b}\pm\sqrt{\textcolor{#7b4fc4}{\textcolor{#c27a0e}{b}^2-4\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{a}\textcolor{#12805a}{c}}}}{2\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{a}}

The whole numerator, -b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}, is divided by 2a. The expression \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{b^2-4ac} under the radical is the discriminant (Lesson 8).

Example 1

Completing the square, then with letters

(a) Solve 3x^2-12x+7=0 by completing the square. Give the exact roots in simplest form.
3x^2-12x=-7
x^2-4x=-\dfrac{7}{3}  (divide every term by 3)
x^2-4x+4=-\dfrac{7}{3}+4  (add \left(\dfrac{-4}{2}\right)^2=4 to both sides)
(x-2)^2=\dfrac{5}{3}
x-2=\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{3}}=\pm\dfrac{\sqrt5}{\sqrt3}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}=\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{15}}{3}
\boldsymbol{x=2\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{15}}{3}=\dfrac{6\pm\sqrt{15}}{3}}
(b) Follow the same steps for ax^2+bx+c=0,\ a\ne0. Fill in each line.
Start with the general equation.
ax^2+bx+c=0
Subtract c, then divide every term by a.
x^2+\dfrac{b}{a}x=-\dfrac{c}{a}
Add \left(\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2=\dfrac{b^2}{4a^2} to both sides.
x^2+\dfrac{b}{a}x+\dfrac{b^2}{4a^2}=\dfrac{b^2}{4a^2}-\dfrac{c}{a}
Factor the left side as a perfect square; write the right side over the common denominator 4a^2.
\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2=\dfrac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}
Take the square root of both sides (\sqrt{4a^2}=2a; the \pm covers the sign of a).
x+\dfrac{b}{2a}=\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}
Subtract \dfrac{b}{2a} from both sides.
\boldsymbol{x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}}
Every line is filled in above: the last line is the quadratic formula. (Tap a ? to show one line at a time.)
Math HistoryIndia · 628 CE

A formula written in words

In 628 CE the Indian astronomer Brahmagupta wrote a rule for solving ax^2+bx=c, entirely in words (there were no letters or symbols yet): “To the absolute number multiplied by four times the square, add the square of the middle term; the square root of the same, less the middle term, being divided by twice the square is the value.”

About 200 years later in Baghdad, al-Khwarizmi solved the same kind of equation by completing a real, drawn square. His book al-jabr gave us the word algebra.

The picture follows Brahmagupta's rule for al-Khwarizmi's famous example, “a square and ten roots equal thirty-nine”, x^2+10x=39. Now use the rule yourself to solve 2x^2+3x=27.
27\times4\times2=216,  216+3^2=225,  \sqrt{225}=15,  15-3=12,  12\div(2\times2)=\boldsymbol{3}. Check: 2(9)+3(3)=27 ✓
The rule is x=\dfrac{-b+\sqrt{b^2+4ac}}{2a} for ax^2+bx=c: our formula with only the + root. (The other root, -4.5, is negative.)

Solving with the Quadratic Formula

1Write the equation in standard form ax^2+bx+c=0 and read a, b and c with their signs.
2Substitute, using brackets for negative values: (-3)^2=9, but -3^2=-9.
3Simplify b^2-4ac first. If it is negative, there are no real roots (a negative number has no real square root).
4Write the radical in simplest form (Unit 2), then divide out a factor common to all the terms: -b, the coefficient of the radical, and 2a.
5For decimal answers, evaluate the + root and the - root separately with a calculator and round only at the end.
Example 2

Exact and decimal roots

Solve. Give exact roots in simplest form and roots to the nearest tenth.

(a) x^2+4x-3=0
a=1,\ b=4,\ c=-3
x=\dfrac{-4\pm\sqrt{16-4(1)(-3)}}{2(1)}
=\dfrac{-4\pm\sqrt{28}}{2}=\dfrac{-4\pm2\sqrt7}{2}
\boldsymbol{=-2\pm\sqrt7}
x\approx0.6 or x\approx-4.6
(b) 9x^2-6x-4=0
a=9,\ b=-6,\ c=-4
x=\dfrac{6\pm\sqrt{36-4(9)(-4)}}{2(9)}
=\dfrac{6\pm\sqrt{180}}{18}=\dfrac{6\pm6\sqrt5}{18}
\boldsymbol{=\dfrac{1\pm\sqrt5}{3}}
x\approx1.1 or x\approx-0.4
(c) x(x+6)=2(2x+7)
x^2+6x=4x+14
x^2+2x-14=0
x=\dfrac{-2\pm\sqrt{4+56}}{2}=\dfrac{-2\pm2\sqrt{15}}{2}
\boldsymbol{=-1\pm\sqrt{15}}
x\approx2.9 or x\approx-4.9

Example 3

Two methods, one answer

Solve 6x^2+x-15=0  (a) with the quadratic formula,  (b) by decomposition.

(a) The quadratic formula
x=\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{1-4(6)(-15)}}{2(6)}=\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{361}}{12}=\dfrac{-1\pm19}{12}
\boldsymbol{x=\tfrac{18}{12}=\tfrac{3}{2}} or \boldsymbol{x=\tfrac{-20}{12}=-\tfrac{5}{3}}
(b) Decomposition
Product ac=-90, sum 1: 10 and -9.
6x^2+10x-9x-15=2x(3x+5)-3(3x+5)
(2x-3)(3x+5)=0, so \boldsymbol{x=\tfrac{3}{2}} or \boldsymbol{x=-\tfrac{5}{3}}.

The formula always works. Here b^2-4ac=361=19^2 is a perfect square, so the roots are rational and the equation could also be factored.

Zeros of y=ax^2+bx+c

The zeros of y=ax^2+bx+c are the roots of ax^2+bx+c=0, and they are the x-coordinates of the x-intercepts of its graph. When b^2-4ac is negative, there are no zeros: the parabola never meets the x-axis.

Example 4

Zeros and x-intercepts

(a) Determine the zeros of y=2x^2+6x-3, exactly and to the nearest hundredth, and state the x-intercepts of its graph. Check with a graphing calculator: graph the function, then use 2ndTRACE (CALC), 2: zero.
Solve 2x^2+6x-3=0:
x=\dfrac{-6\pm\sqrt{36-4(2)(-3)}}{2(2)}=\dfrac{-6\pm\sqrt{60}}{4}=\dfrac{-6\pm2\sqrt{15}}{4}=\dfrac{-3\pm\sqrt{15}}{2}
Zeros: \boldsymbol{\tfrac{-3+\sqrt{15}}{2}\approx0.44} and \boldsymbol{\tfrac{-3-\sqrt{15}}{2}\approx-3.44}.
x-intercepts about (0.44,\ 0) and (-3.44,\ 0). The calculator's zero feature gives x\approx0.436 and x\approx-3.436 ✓
(b) Show that y=x^2-2x+6 has no zeros. What does this mean for its graph?
b^2-4ac=(-2)^2-4(1)(6)=4-24=-20
x=\dfrac{2\pm\sqrt{-20}}{2} is not a real number.
There are no real roots, so the parabola never meets the x-axis (it opens up with its vertex above the axis).
Check (a) with the zero feature. Y_1=2X^2+6X-3, ZOOM6, then 2ndTRACE2: a left bound, a right bound and a guess around each intercept (0, 1, guess 0.5; then -4, -3, guess -3.5).

Finding and Correcting Errors

The most common mistakes with the formula: using b instead of -b; squaring a negative b without brackets; a sign error in -4ac when a or c is negative; dividing only one term of the numerator by 2a; not putting the equation in standard form first.
Example 5

Find the two errors

A student solved 2x^2-4x-3=0 as shown. Find the two errors, explain each one, and give the correct roots.

Line 1: x=\dfrac{-(-4)\pm\sqrt{-4^2-4(2)(-3)}}{2(2)}
Line 2: x=\dfrac{4\pm\sqrt{-16+24}}{4}=\dfrac{4\pm\sqrt8}{4}
Line 3: x=\dfrac{4\pm2\sqrt2}{4}
Line 4: x=1\pm2\sqrt2
Error 1 (Lines 1–2): b^2=(-4)^2=16, not -16. The discriminant is 16+24=40.
Error 2 (Line 4): only the 4 was divided by 4; every term of the numerator must be divided: \tfrac{4\pm2\sqrt2}{4}=\tfrac{2\pm\sqrt2}{2}.
Correct: x=\dfrac{4\pm\sqrt{40}}{4}=\dfrac{4\pm2\sqrt{10}}{4}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2\pm\sqrt{10}}{2}}  (x\approx2.58 or x\approx-0.58)

Equations That Need Rearranging First

The formula needs standard form. Expand and collect terms; clear fractions by multiplying every term by the LCD; divide out a common factor to make the numbers smaller. If the variable is in a denominator, state the restriction (e.g. x\ne0), multiply both sides by it, and check that no root breaks the restriction.
Example 7

Standard form first

Write each equation in standard form, then solve. Give exact roots and roots to the nearest tenth.

(a) (2x+1)(x-3)=(x+2)(x-1)
2x^2-5x-3=x^2+x-2
x^2-6x-1=0
x=\dfrac{6\pm\sqrt{36+4}}{2}=\dfrac{6\pm2\sqrt{10}}{2}=\boldsymbol{3\pm\sqrt{10}}
x\approx6.2 or x\approx-0.2
(b) \dfrac{x^2}{3}-\dfrac{x}{2}-1=0
Multiply every term by 6: 2x^2-3x-6=0
x=\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt{9+48}}{4}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt{57}}{4}}
(57=3\times19 has no square factor.)
x\approx2.6 or x\approx-1.1
(c) 3x-\dfrac{2}{x}=4,\ x\ne0
Multiply by x: 3x^2-2=4x
3x^2-4x-2=0
x=\dfrac{4\pm\sqrt{16+24}}{6}=\dfrac{4\pm2\sqrt{10}}{6}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2\pm\sqrt{10}}{3}}

Problems Solved with the Formula

Set up the equation as in Unit 4, Lesson 5. When it does not factor, use the formula, then reject inadmissible roots (negative lengths or times) and round as the question asks.
Example 8

The formula in context

(a) A ball is thrown upward. Its height after t seconds is h=-4.9t^2+15t+2 metres. When does it hit the ground, to the nearest tenth of a second?
Ground: h=0.  -4.9t^2+15t+2=0
t=\dfrac{-15\pm\sqrt{225-4(-4.9)(2)}}{2(-4.9)}=\dfrac{-15\pm\sqrt{264.2}}{-9.8}
t=\dfrac{-15-16.254}{-9.8}\approx3.19 or t=\dfrac{-15+16.254}{-9.8}\approx-0.13 (reject: before the throw)
It hits the ground after about 3.2 s.
(b) A rectangle is 6 cm longer than it is wide, and its area is 24\text{ cm}^2. Determine its exact dimensions and exact perimeter, then give each to the nearest hundredth.
Width w, length w+6:  w(w+6)=24
w^2+6w-24=0
w=\dfrac{-6\pm\sqrt{36+96}}{2}=\dfrac{-6\pm2\sqrt{33}}{2}=-3\pm\sqrt{33}
Reject -3-\sqrt{33}<0. Width \boldsymbol{-3+\sqrt{33}\approx2.74} cm, length \boldsymbol{3+\sqrt{33}\approx8.74} cm.
Perimeter =2(-3+\sqrt{33}+3+\sqrt{33})=\boldsymbol{4\sqrt{33}\approx22.98} cm.
✓

Key ideas

The formula. ax^2+bx+c=0 has roots x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}. The whole numerator is divided by 2a.
Standard form, signs, brackets. Rearrange first (expand, clear fractions, divide out a common factor). Read a, b, c with their signs; (-3)^2=9, but -3^2=-9.
Simplify. b^2-4ac first: negative means no real roots. Simplify the radical, then divide a common factor out of every term. Decimals: each root separately, round at the end.
Choose and check. No x term: square roots. Factors easily: factor. Otherwise: the formula. In a problem, reject negative lengths and times.
