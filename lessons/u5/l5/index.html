The Rope Around the Earth

A rope is tied snugly around the Earth’s equator: about 40 000 km of rope.

Now make the rope just 1 metre longer, and lift it evenly so it floats the same height above the ground all the way around.

How big is the gap under the rope? Could an ant crawl under it? A cat? You?

?
Everyone guess first: ant, cat or you? Then try to work it out.
About 16 cm: a cat can squeeze under! A circle’s circumference is C=2\pi r. Lifting the rope a height h makes it 2\pi(r+h)=2\pi r+2\pi h long, so the extra 1 m is 2\pi h: h=\frac{1}{2\pi}\approx0.16 m.
The twist: the size of the Earth cancels out. Around a basketball, the Earth or the Sun, one extra metre of rope always lifts it about 16 cm.

Writing a Quadratic Equation from Its Vertex and a Point

The vertex fixes where a parabola sits, but not how wide it is. One more point on the graph fixes a. Then we read the information from graphs, use symmetry and the zeros, and write equations for real arches and throws, where we choose where the origin goes.

The Vertex and One Other Point

The vertex (p,\ q) fixes the position of a parabola, but not its shape: every parabola y=a(x-p)^2+q has the same vertex whatever the value of a. One more point on the graph fixes a.

To write the equation from the vertex and a point
1Write y=a(x-p)^2+q with the vertex values for p and q.
2Substitute the point’s coordinates for x and y.
3Solve for a.
4Write the equation with a, p and q (keep x and y as letters).

To get the general form y=ax^2+bx+c, expand the square, multiply by a and collect like terms. A y-intercept of k is the point (0,\ k).

Example 1

Vertex form, then general form

A parabola has the given vertex and passes through the given point. Write its equation in vertex form, then in general form.

(a) vertex (3,\ -5), point (5,\ 7)
y=a(x-3)^2-5
7=a(5-3)^2-5
12=4a, so a=3
Vertex form: y=3(x-3)^2-5
y=3(x^2-6x+9)-5
General form: y=3x^2-18x+22
(b) vertex (-4,\ 6), point (-2,\ -2)
y=a(x+4)^2+6
-2=a(-2+4)^2+6
-8=4a, so a=-2
Vertex form: y=-2(x+4)^2+6
y=-2(x^2+8x+16)+6
General form: y=-2x^2-16x-26
Example 2

When a is a fraction

Write the equation of each parabola in vertex form. The value of a is often a fraction: leave it as an exact fraction.

(a) vertex (6,\ 1), y-intercept 13
The y-intercept is the point (0,\ 13).
13=a(0-6)^2+1
12=36a, so a=\frac{1}{3}
y=\frac{1}{3}(x-6)^2+1
(b) vertex (-1,\ 0), passing through (3,\ -6)
y=a(x+1)^2+0
-6=a(3+1)^2
-6=16a, so a=-\frac{3}{8}
y=-\frac{3}{8}(x+1)^2

Reading the Vertex and a Point from a Graph

On a graph, read the vertex and one other point where the curve crosses grid lines exactly (a lattice point). Choose a point far from the vertex if you can: a small reading error matters less.

Once you have the equation, you can analyze the graph exactly: the y-intercept (set x=0), the x-intercepts (set y=0 and solve with square roots), the axis, the domain and the range.

A graphing calculator check. Graph your equation and see that it passes through the points you used: for Example 3, y=-\frac12(x+3)^2+6 at x=-1 should give 4 (2ndCALC 1:value). The zero gives an x-intercept to check your exact answer.
Example 3

From a graph

The graph of a quadratic function is shown, with its vertex (-3,\ 6) and the point (-1,\ 4) marked.

(a) Determine the equation of the function in vertex form.
y=a(x+3)^2+6
4=a(-1+3)^2+6\Rightarrow-2=4a\Rightarrow a=-\frac{1}{2}
y=-\frac{1}{2}(x+3)^2+6
(b) Determine the y-intercept.
x=0: y=-\frac{1}{2}(9)+6=\frac{3}{2}. The y-intercept is \frac{3}{2}, or 1.5.
(c) Determine the x-intercepts as exact values and to the nearest hundredth.
0=-\frac{1}{2}(x+3)^2+6
\frac{1}{2}(x+3)^2=6\Rightarrow(x+3)^2=12
x+3=\pm\sqrt{12}=\pm2\sqrt{3}
x=-3+2\sqrt{3}\approx0.46 and x=-3-2\sqrt{3}\approx-6.46
(d) State the equation of the axis of symmetry, the domain and the range.
Axis of symmetry x=-3.
Domain \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}.
Range \{y\mid y\le6,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\} (it opens down, maximum 6).

Using the Axis of Symmetry

A parabola is symmetric about its axis x=p, so points at the same height are the same distance from the axis.

Key idea
If the axis is x=p and one x-intercept is r, the other x-intercept is 2p-r (the axis is midway: p=\frac{r+s}{2}).
If the equation y=a(x-p)^2+q has p known but a and q unknown, substitute two points to get two equations, then subtract to eliminate q.

A point whose x-coordinate equals p is the vertex.

Example 4

The axis of symmetry

(a) The graph of y=a(x+1)^2+q passes through (1,\ -2) and (3,\ 10). Determine a and q, and state the minimum or maximum value of the function.
(1,\ -2): -2=a(2)^2+q=4a+q
(3,\ 10): 10=a(4)^2+q=16a+q
Subtract: 12=12a, so a=1
Then q=-2-4(1)=-6
y=(x+1)^2-6: a>0, so it has a minimum value of -6 (at x=-1).
(b) A parabola has axis of symmetry x=3 and one x-intercept at -2. Determine the other x-intercept.
-2 is 5 units left of the axis x=3, so the other intercept is 5 units right of it: x=3+5=8.
(Or 2p-r=2(3)-(-2)=8.)
(c) A quadratic function has a minimum value of -12, its axis of symmetry is x=4, and its y-intercept is 20. Determine its equation in vertex form.
The vertex is (4,\ -12) and (0,\ 20) is on the graph.   20=a(0-4)^2-12\Rightarrow32=16a\Rightarrow a=2.   y=2(x-4)^2-12

The Zeros and a Point: Factored Form

When the x-intercepts r and s are known, start from the factored form and use one more point to find a.

Factored form: y=a(x-r)(x-s), where r and s are the zeros
Substitute a point (not a zero) to find a.
The axis is x=\frac{r+s}{2}; substitute it to find the vertex. A graph that only touches the x-axis at r has vertex (r,\ 0): use y=a(x-r)^2.
Example 5

From the zeros

Write the equation of each parabola in factored form, then determine its vertex.

(a) zeros -1 and 5, passing through (3,\ 12)
y=a(x+1)(x-5)
12=a(3+1)(3-5)=-8a
a=-\frac{3}{2}:   y=-\frac{3}{2}(x+1)(x-5)
Axis x=\frac{-1+5}{2}=2
y=-\frac{3}{2}(3)(-3)=\frac{27}{2}
Vertex \left(2,\ \frac{27}{2}\right)
(b) x-intercepts 2 and 6, y-intercept -9
y=a(x-2)(x-6)
(0,\ -9): -9=a(-2)(-6)=12a
a=-\frac{3}{4}:   y=-\frac{3}{4}(x-2)(x-6)
Axis x=4; y=-\frac{3}{4}(2)(-2)=3
Vertex (4,\ 3), so also y=-\frac{3}{4}(x-4)^2+3.
(c) The graph touches the x-axis only at x=-3 and passes through (-1,\ 8).
The vertex is (-3,\ 0): y=a(x+3)^2.   8=a(2)^2\Rightarrow a=2.   y=2(x+3)^2, vertex (-3,\ 0).
5

Modelling with a Parabola: Choosing the Origin

Arches, bridge cables, jets of water and the paths of thrown or kicked objects are close to parabolas. To model one:

1Sketch the shape and place the axes. Two good choices: the origin at one end of the base (the zeros are 0 and the width), or the origin on the ground under the vertex (the vertex is (0,\ q), so y=ax^2+q).
2Write the vertex and one other point (or the zeros) from the information, and find a.
3Use the equation to answer the question; state the answer with units and round as asked.

Different origins give different equations, but the same answers to the questions.

Example 6

A stone arch

A stone arch over a garden path is a parabola 24 m wide at its base and 18 m high at its centre.

(a) Place the origin at the left end of the base. Determine an equation for the arch.
Zeros 0 and 24; the vertex is midway: (12,\ 18).
y=a(x-12)^2+18;   (0,\ 0): 0=144a+18\Rightarrow a=-\frac{1}{8}
y=-\frac{1}{8}(x-12)^2+18
(b) How high is the arch 3 m in from one end of its base? Answer to the nearest tenth of a metre.
x=3: y=-\frac{1}{8}(3-12)^2+18=-\frac{81}{8}+18=7.875. The arch is about 7.9 m high there.
(c) Write the equation again with the origin on the ground directly under the top of the arch, and use it to check (b).
Vertex (0,\ 18), zero (12,\ 0): 0=144a+18\Rightarrow a=-\frac{1}{8},   y=-\frac{1}{8}x^2+18.
3 m in from an end is x=9 (or -9): y=-\frac{81}{8}+18=7.875 ✓
Example 7

A thrown ball

A ball is thrown from a height of 2 m. Its path is a parabola. It reaches its greatest height, 14 m, when it is 10 m away horizontally from where it was released. Place the origin on the ground below the release point.

(a) Determine the equation of the path.
Vertex (10,\ 14); release point (0,\ 2).
2=a(0-10)^2+14
-12=100a, so a=-0.12
y=-0.12(x-10)^2+14
(b) How far away horizontally does the ball land? Answer to the nearest tenth of a metre.
0=-0.12(x-10)^2+14
(x-10)^2=\frac{14}{0.12}\approx116.67
x-10\approx\pm10.80, so x\approx20.8 (reject x\approx-0.8: behind the thrower).
It lands about 20.8 m away.
Check: graph and use zero. A window from x=0 to 25 and y=-2 to 16 shows the whole flight; 2ndCALC 2:zero with bounds 20 and 22 gives x\approx20.80.
✓

Key ideas

Vertex and a point. Write y=a(x-p)^2+q, substitute the point for x and y, solve for a (leave fractions exact), and write the equation with x and y as letters. Expand for general form.
From a graph. Read the vertex and a lattice point, far from the vertex if you can. Then x=0 gives the y-intercept, and y=0 with square roots gives the x-intercepts.
Symmetry and zeros. The other intercept is 2p-r. With p known, two points and subtracting eliminate q. From the zeros: y=a(x-r)(x-s), axis x=\frac{r+s}{2}; touching at r: y=a(x-r)^2.
Modelling. Sketch, place the axes (an end of the base, or under the top), find a, answer with units. Different origins give different equations but the same answers; reject a root that makes no sense.
