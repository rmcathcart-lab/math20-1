The Gateway Arch

The Gateway Arch in St. Louis is a giant arch of shining stainless steel. Little trams carry visitors up inside its legs to a window at the very top.

Which is bigger: its height, or the width between its legs at the ground?

?
Vote first: height or width? By how much?
Neither: they are exactly the same. The arch is 192 m (630 ft) tall and 192 m wide. Measure the picture: it is drawn to scale.
Almost everyone votes “taller”: our eyes stretch vertical lengths (the vertical–horizontal illusion), and the legs get thinner as they climb, which makes it look even taller. Bonus: it looks like a parabola, but it is a different curve, a catenary (the shape of a hanging chain, flipped upside down).

1

Vertex, Axis of Symmetry and Direction of Opening

In vertex form y=a(x-p)^2+q the key features can be read without graphing. The vertex is (p,\ q) and the axis of symmetry is the vertical line x=p through it. The sign of a gives the direction of opening, and the vertex is then the lowest or highest point, so q is the minimum or maximum value of the function.

Key idea
y=a(x-p)^2+q
vertex (p,\ q);  axis of symmetry x=p
a>0: opens up, minimum value q
a<0: opens down, maximum value q
!
Watch the sign of p. The bracket is (x-p), so (x+3)^2=(x-(-3))^2 gives p=-3. If the equation is not in this exact form (for example y+5=\dots or 2y=\dots), first solve for y.

Domain and Range

A quadratic function accepts any x-value, so its domain is always \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}. Its range starts at the y-coordinate of the vertex and goes up (opens up) or down (opens down).

Key idea
Domain: \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}
Range, a>0: \{y\mid y\ge q,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}
Range, a<0: \{y\mid y\le q,\ y\in\mathbb{R}\}
opens up: y\ge q
opens down: y\le q

3

The y-Intercept and Its Mirror Point

The y-intercept is where x=0: substitute 0 for x and evaluate. Because the parabola is symmetric about x=p, the point the same distance on the other side of the axis has the same y-value. The mirror of the y-intercept (0,\ c) is (2p,\ c). This gives a third point for a sketch.

Example 4

y=-\frac{1}{2}(0+4)^2+10=-8+10=2

(c) y=3(x-1)^2+2
y=3(0-1)^2+2=5
y-intercept 5

4

How Many x-Intercepts?

Picture the vertex and the direction of opening. A parabola that opens up with its vertex below the x-axis must cross the axis twice; if its vertex is above the axis it never reaches it. If the vertex is on the axis (q=0) the parabola just touches it once.

Key idea: the number of x-intercepts of y=a(x-p)^2+q
2a and q have opposite signs
1q=0 (the vertex)
0a and q have the same sign

5

Finding the x-Intercepts by Solving a(x-p)^2+q=0

At an x-intercept y=0. In vertex form x appears only once, inside the square, so isolate the square and take square roots. Remember both signs.

To find the x-intercepts
  1. Set y=0.
  2. Isolate (x-p)^2.
  3. If (x-p)^2 equals a negative number, there are no x-intercepts.
  4. Otherwise x-p=\pm\sqrt{\ \ }, so x=p\pm\sqrt{\ \ }. Give exact answers in simplest radical form, then round if asked.
A graphing calculator check. Graph the function and use the zero feature, once for each x-intercept: 2ndCALC2, then a left bound, a right bound and a guess around one intercept. For Example 6(c), y=-3(x-2)^2+15: x\approx4.24 and x\approx-0.24.

Matching Graphs, Symmetry and Contexts

To match an equation to a graph, check the vertex first (careful with the sign of p), then the direction of opening, then the width: a larger |a| gives a narrower parabola. Symmetry also helps: two points with the same y-value are the same distance from the axis, so the two x-intercepts are equally far from x=p.

Example 9

Use symmetry

(a) The graph of y=a(x+3)^2+q has an x-intercept at 2. Determine the other x-intercept.
The axis is x=-3. The intercept 2 is 5 units to the right of it, so the other is 5 units to the left: x=-8.
(b) The point (1,\ -6) is on the graph of y=2(x-4)^2+q. Name another point on the graph with a y-coordinate of -6.
The axis is x=4. The point is 3 units to the left of it, so its mirror point is 3 units to the right: (7,\ -6).
Example 10

A footbridge arch

The underside of a footbridge arch is modelled by h=-0.08(x-10)^2+8, where h is the height in metres above the path and x is the horizontal distance in metres from the left foot of the arch.

(a) What is the maximum height of the arch, and how far from the left foot is it?
Vertex (10,\ 8) with a<0: the maximum height is 8 m, 10 m from the left foot.
(b) How wide is the arch at the path?
0=-0.08(x-10)^2+8\Rightarrow(x-10)^2=100
x-10=\pm10, so x=0 or x=20. The arch is 20 m wide.
(c) How high is the arch 3 m from the left foot?
h=-0.08(3-10)^2+8=-0.08(49)+8=4.08 m
(d) State the domain and range of h in this context.
Only the arch itself makes sense:
D: \{x\mid 0\le x\le20,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
R: \{h\mid 0\le h\le8,\ h\in\mathbb{R}\}
✓

Key ideas

Read it from y=a(x-p)^2+q. Vertex (p,\ q), axis x=p. a>0: opens up, minimum q; a<0: opens down, maximum q. Watch the sign of p; solve for y first.
Domain, range, y-intercept. D: \{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}. R: y\ge q (up) or y\le q (down). y-intercept: x=0; its mirror point is (2p,\ c).
How many x-intercepts? a and q of opposite signs: 2. q=0: 1 (the vertex). The same sign: 0.
Solve a(x-p)^2+q=0. Isolate the square, then x=p\pm\sqrt{\ \ } (a negative: none). Match graphs by vertex, opening, width. Symmetric points are equally far from x=p.
