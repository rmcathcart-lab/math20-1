Dividing Radicals and Rationalizing Monomial Denominators

Last lesson radicals were multiplied: \sqrt a\cdot\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}. Today that rule runs backward to divide them. Then we clear radicals out of denominators by multiplying by a clever form of 1, like \textcolor{#c27a0e}{\tfrac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}}.

I can…
  • divide radicals with the same index: coefficients by coefficients, radicands by radicands, then write the result in simplest form
  • divide every term of a numerator by a one-term (monomial) denominator
  • rationalize the denominator of \frac{a}{\sqrt b} and \frac{a}{m\sqrt b}, and rewrite a fraction under a radical
  • rationalize cube-root and fourth-root denominators by building a perfect cube (or fourth power)
  • simplify quotients with variables and state the restrictions, and solve problems with exact answers
AN2 Solve problems that involve operations on radicals and radical expressions with numerical and variable radicands.

Dividing Radicals

Radicals with the same index can be divided: divide the coefficients, divide the radicands, then write the result in simplest form. This is the multiplication rule \sqrt a\cdot\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab} from Lesson 4 run backward.
Dividing radicals
\dfrac{\textcolor{#a33357}{m}\sqrt[n]{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{a}}}{\textcolor{#a33357}{k}\sqrt[n]{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{b}}}=\dfrac{\textcolor{#a33357}{m}}{\textcolor{#a33357}{k}}\sqrt[n]{\dfrac{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{a}}{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{b}}}
k\ne0, b\ne0; when n is even, a\ge0 and b>0
Example 1

Divide

Divide and write in simplest form.

(a) \dfrac{\sqrt{48}}{\sqrt{3}}
\sqrt{\dfrac{48}{3}}=\sqrt{16}=4
(b) \dfrac{12\sqrt{42}}{4\sqrt{6}}
\dfrac{12}{4}\sqrt{\dfrac{42}{6}}=3\sqrt7
(c) \dfrac{10\sqrt{54}}{5\sqrt{3}}
2\sqrt{18}=2(3\sqrt2)=6\sqrt2
(d) \dfrac{\sqrt[3]{250}}{\sqrt[3]{2}}
\sqrt[3]{125}=5
(e) \dfrac{-18\sqrt[3]{80}}{6\sqrt[3]{5}}
-3\sqrt[3]{16}=-3(2\sqrt[3]{2})=-6\sqrt[3]{2}
(f) \dfrac{6\sqrt{35}}{9\sqrt{5}}
\dfrac{6}{9}\sqrt{7}=\dfrac{2\sqrt7}{3}

When the radicands do not divide evenly

Either reduce the fraction under the radical, or write each radical in simplest form first and divide out the like radicals (just as \frac{7x}{3x}=\frac73). Both routes give the same answer.

Example 2

Radicands that don't divide evenly

Simplify.

(a) \dfrac{\sqrt{98}}{\sqrt{18}}
\sqrt{\dfrac{98}{18}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{49}{9}}=\dfrac73  (or \dfrac{7\sqrt2}{3\sqrt2}=\dfrac73)
(b) \dfrac{4\sqrt{90}}{3\sqrt{40}}
\dfrac{4(3\sqrt{10})}{3(2\sqrt{10})}=\dfrac{12\sqrt{10}}{6\sqrt{10}}=2
(c) \dfrac{15\sqrt{72}}{10\sqrt{50}}
\dfrac{15(6\sqrt2)}{10(5\sqrt2)}=\dfrac{90}{50}=\dfrac95
(d) \dfrac{6\sqrt[3]{54}}{\sqrt[3]{16}}
\dfrac{6(3\sqrt[3]{2})}{2\sqrt[3]{2}}=\dfrac{18}{2}=9
2

Dividing Each Term of a Numerator

A fraction bar groups the whole numerator, so a one-term (monomial) denominator divides every term of the numerator: \dfrac{a+b}{c}=\dfrac{a}{c}+\dfrac{b}{c}.
!
Divide every term. Dividing only one of the terms is a common error: \frac{6+4}{2} is 3+2=5, not 3+4.
Example 3

Divide each term

Divide each term of the numerator by the denominator, and simplify.

(a) \dfrac{\sqrt{70}-\sqrt{42}}{\sqrt{7}}
\dfrac{\sqrt{70}}{\sqrt7}-\dfrac{\sqrt{42}}{\sqrt7}=\sqrt{10}-\sqrt6
(b) \dfrac{12\sqrt{15}+8\sqrt{6}}{4\sqrt{3}}
\dfrac{12\sqrt{15}}{4\sqrt3}+\dfrac{8\sqrt6}{4\sqrt3}=3\sqrt5+2\sqrt2
(c) \dfrac{6\sqrt{40}-9\sqrt{24}}{3\sqrt{2}}
2\sqrt{20}-3\sqrt{12}
=2(2\sqrt5)-3(2\sqrt3)
=4\sqrt5-6\sqrt3
3

Rationalizing a Monomial Denominator

A radical expression is in simplest form when
  1. no radicand has a perfect-square (or perfect-cube, …) factor,
  2. no radical is in a denominator,
  3. no fraction is under a radical,
  4. like radicals are combined, and
  5. the fraction is reduced.

Rationalizing the denominator: multiply by a form of 1

Removing the radical from a denominator is called rationalizing the denominator. Multiply the numerator and the denominator by the radical in the denominator: this multiplies by 1, so the value does not change.

Rationalizing a monomial denominator (b>0)
\dfrac{a}{\sqrt b}\cdot\textcolor{#c27a0e}{\dfrac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt b}}=\dfrac{a\sqrt b}{b}
\dfrac{a}{m\sqrt b}\cdot\textcolor{#c27a0e}{\dfrac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt b}}=\dfrac{a\sqrt b}{mb}
!
Three habits. For m\sqrt b, multiply by \sqrt b only (not by m\sqrt b): the m is already rational. Simplify a radical in the denominator first: it keeps the numbers small. A fraction under a radical is split first: \sqrt{\dfrac ab}=\dfrac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt b}.
Think To rationalize \frac{5}{\sqrt3}, why can't we just multiply the denominator by \sqrt3?
That multiplies the whole fraction by \frac{1}{\sqrt3} and changes its value. Multiplying the top and the bottom by \sqrt3 multiplies by \frac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}=1.
Example 4

Rationalize the denominator

Rationalize the denominator and simplify.

(a) \dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3}}
\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}}=\dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{3}
(b) \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{7}}
\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{7}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{\sqrt{7}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{7}
(c) \dfrac{14}{3\sqrt{7}}
\dfrac{14\sqrt{7}}{3(7)}=\dfrac{14\sqrt{7}}{21}=\dfrac{2\sqrt{7}}{3}
(d) \dfrac{10}{\sqrt{20}}
\dfrac{10}{2\sqrt{5}}=\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{5}}=\dfrac{5\sqrt{5}}{5}=\sqrt{5}
(e) \dfrac{-9}{2\sqrt{6}}
\dfrac{-9\sqrt{6}}{2(6)}=\dfrac{-9\sqrt{6}}{12}=-\dfrac{3\sqrt{6}}{4}
(f) \sqrt{\dfrac{5}{8}}
\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{8}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2\sqrt{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{4}

When the numerator has two terms

Multiply every term of the numerator by the radical. Reduce only by a factor that divides every term of the numerator and the denominator.

Example 5

Two-term numerators

(a) Simplify \dfrac{8\sqrt{15}-4\sqrt{6}}{2\sqrt{3}} by dividing each term.
\dfrac{8\sqrt{15}}{2\sqrt3}-\dfrac{4\sqrt6}{2\sqrt3}=4\sqrt5-2\sqrt2
(b) Simplify the same expression by rationalizing first.
\dfrac{(8\sqrt{15}-4\sqrt6)\sqrt3}{2(3)}=\dfrac{8\sqrt{45}-4\sqrt{18}}{6}=\dfrac{24\sqrt5-12\sqrt2}{6}
=4\sqrt5-2\sqrt2  (the same answer)
(c) \dfrac{4+\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{2}}
\dfrac{(4+\sqrt6)\sqrt2}{2}=\dfrac{4\sqrt2+\sqrt{12}}{2}=\dfrac{4\sqrt2+2\sqrt3}{2}=2\sqrt2+\sqrt3
(d) \dfrac{3\sqrt{5}-10}{2\sqrt{5}}
\dfrac{(3\sqrt5-10)\sqrt5}{2(5)}=\dfrac{15-10\sqrt5}{10}=\dfrac{3-2\sqrt5}{2}
(5 divides every term)
4

Cube-Root and Higher-Index Denominators

To rationalize \sqrt[3]{b}, multiply by the cube root that makes the radicand a perfect cube. Multiplying by \sqrt[3]{b} itself is not enough: \sqrt[3]{2}\cdot\sqrt[3]{2}=\sqrt[3]{4} is still irrational, but \sqrt[3]{2}\cdot\sqrt[3]{4}=\sqrt[3]{8}=2. For a fourth root, make a perfect fourth power.
Build a perfect cube
\dfrac{5}{\sqrt[3]{2}}\cdot\textcolor{#c27a0e}{\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{4}}{\sqrt[3]{4}}}=\dfrac{5\sqrt[3]{4}}{\sqrt[3]{8}}=\dfrac{5\sqrt[3]{4}}{2}
Ask: “what times the radicand makes a perfect cube?”
Example 6

Cube-root and fourth-root denominators

Rationalize the denominator and simplify.

(a) \dfrac{4}{\sqrt[3]{3}}
\dfrac{4}{\sqrt[3]{3}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{9}}{\sqrt[3]{9}}=\dfrac{4\sqrt[3]{9}}{3}
(b) \dfrac{10}{\sqrt[3]{25}}
\dfrac{10\sqrt[3]{5}}{\sqrt[3]{125}}=\dfrac{10\sqrt[3]{5}}{5}=2\sqrt[3]{5}
(c) \dfrac{9}{2\sqrt[3]{6}}
\dfrac{9\sqrt[3]{36}}{2\sqrt[3]{216}}=\dfrac{9\sqrt[3]{36}}{12}=\dfrac{3\sqrt[3]{36}}{4}
(d) \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{3}{4}}
\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{3}}{\sqrt[3]{4}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{2}}{\sqrt[3]{2}}=\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{6}}{2}
(e) \dfrac{12}{\sqrt[3]{18}}
18\times12=216=6^3: \dfrac{12\sqrt[3]{12}}{\sqrt[3]{216}}=\dfrac{12\sqrt[3]{12}}{6}=2\sqrt[3]{12}
(f) \dfrac{6}{\sqrt[4]{27}}
27\times3=81=3^4: \dfrac{6\sqrt[4]{3}}{\sqrt[4]{81}}=\dfrac{6\sqrt[4]{3}}{3}=2\sqrt[4]{3}
5

Quotients with Variables

State the restrictions first. An even-index radical needs a radicand \ge0, and a denominator cannot be 0, so a variable radicand in a denominator must be >0 (even index) or \ne0 (odd index). Then divide or rationalize exactly as with numbers.
Example 7

Variables and restrictions

Simplify. State the restrictions on the variable.

(a) \dfrac{\sqrt{45x^5}}{\sqrt{5x}}
x>0
\sqrt{\dfrac{45x^5}{5x}}=\sqrt{9x^4}=3x^2
(b) \dfrac{\sqrt{14a^3}+\sqrt{21a}}{\sqrt{7a}}
a>0
\sqrt{2a^2}+\sqrt3=a\sqrt2+\sqrt3
(c) \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{x}}
x>0
\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{x}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{x}}=\dfrac{6\sqrt{x}}{x}
(d) \dfrac{3x}{\sqrt{6x}}
x>0
\dfrac{3x\sqrt{6x}}{6x}=\dfrac{\sqrt{6x}}{2}
(e) \sqrt{\dfrac{5}{8y}}
y>0
\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2\sqrt{2y}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{2y}}{\sqrt{2y}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{10y}}{2(2y)}=\dfrac{\sqrt{10y}}{4y}
(f) \dfrac{4}{\sqrt[3]{2m}}
m\ne0. Multiply by \sqrt[3]{4m^2}:
\dfrac{4\sqrt[3]{4m^2}}{\sqrt[3]{8m^3}}=\dfrac{4\sqrt[3]{4m^2}}{2m}=\dfrac{2\sqrt[3]{4m^2}}{m}

Problems with Quotients

In a measurement problem, dividing by a radical is common: a length is an area divided by another length. Give exact answers in simplest form, with units.

Example 8

Exact answers in problems

(a) The diagonal of a square is \sqrt2 times its side. A square has a diagonal of 12 cm. Determine the exact side length and the area.
s=\dfrac{12}{\sqrt2}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt2}=\dfrac{12\sqrt2}{2}=6\sqrt2 cm
A=(6\sqrt2)^2=36(2)=72 cm^2
(b) A rectangular banner has an area of (6\sqrt{30}+9\sqrt{10}) m^2 and a height of 3\sqrt5 m. Determine its length and its perimeter.
\ell=\dfrac{6\sqrt{30}+9\sqrt{10}}{3\sqrt5}=2\sqrt6+3\sqrt2 m
P=2(2\sqrt6+3\sqrt2)+2(3\sqrt5)
=4\sqrt6+6\sqrt2+6\sqrt5 m
(c) Solve x\sqrt{6}+\sqrt{8}=\sqrt{50}. Write x in simplest form.
x\sqrt6=\sqrt{50}-\sqrt8=5\sqrt2-2\sqrt2=3\sqrt2
x=\dfrac{3\sqrt2}{\sqrt6}=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{3}=\sqrt3
✓

Key ideas

Divide radicals (same index). Coefficients by coefficients, radicands by radicands: \dfrac{m\sqrt a}{k\sqrt b}=\dfrac mk\sqrt{\dfrac ab}. Then simplest form. If the radicands don't divide evenly, reduce inside or simplify each radical first.
One-term denominator: divide every term. \dfrac{a+b}{c}=\dfrac ac+\dfrac bc. Reduce only by a factor that divides every term, top and bottom.
Rationalize: multiply by a form of 1. \dfrac{a}{m\sqrt b}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt b}=\dfrac{a\sqrt b}{mb}, because \sqrt b\cdot\sqrt b=b. Simplify the denominator first; split a fraction under a radical.
Cube roots and variables. Multiply by what makes a perfect cube (\sqrt[3]2\cdot\sqrt[3]4=\sqrt[3]8=2) or fourth power. State restrictions first: an even-index radical in a denominator needs >0; an odd-index one needs \ne0.
