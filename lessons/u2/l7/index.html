Unit Summary

In 5\sqrt[3]{2} the index is 3, the radicand is 2 and the coefficient is 5. A mixed radical has a coefficient other than 1 (5\sqrt[3]{2}); an entire radical has coefficient 1 (\sqrt[3]{250}). Every answer in this unit is exact and in simplest form.

SkillRuleExample

Entire \to mixedTake out the largest perfect nth-power factor: \sqrt[n]{a^nb}=a\sqrt[n]{b}.\sqrt{72}=\sqrt{36}\sqrt{2}=6\sqrt{2}

Choosing the operation

Before you start, look at what joins the radicals. Plus or minus: simplify every radical, then combine like radicals. Times: multiply coefficients and radicands, or distribute every term. Divided by: divide, and if a radical is left in the denominator, rationalize it (one term: multiply by the radical; two terms: multiply by the conjugate). Then check the answer is in simplest form.

Is it finished? The simplest form checklist

Simplest form checklist. (1) No perfect-power factor left in any radicand. (2) No fraction under a radical. (3) No radical in a denominator. (4) Like radicals combined and fractions reduced. (5) Restrictions stated for variables (we keep variables non-negative so that \sqrt{x^2}=x).

The inspector works through the list in working order: it splits a fraction under a root first, so every radical has a whole radicand to simplify. If a fix creates something new (rationalizing can make new radicands), it checks again.

Rational and irrational

A rational number can be written as a fraction of two integers (its decimal ends or repeats). \sqrt[n]{a} is rational only when a is a perfect nth power; otherwise it is irrational (its decimal never ends or repeats), which is why we keep the radical as the exact answer. A rational number plus or times a non-zero irrational number is irrational, but two irrational numbers can give a rational result: \sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{8}=4, and a binomial times its conjugate is always rational.

Converting, Comparing and Restrictions

To compare radicals without a calculator, make them all entire radicals with the same index: the coefficient goes in as its nth power. Then the larger radicand is the larger number.
Example 1

Order without a calculator

Without a calculator, arrange each set from least to greatest.

(a) 4\sqrt{3},\ \ 3\sqrt{5},\ \ 2\sqrt{13},\ \ 7,\ \ 5\sqrt{2}
Entire radicals: 4\sqrt{3}=\sqrt{48}, 3\sqrt{5}=\sqrt{45}, 2\sqrt{13}=\sqrt{52}, 7=\sqrt{49}, 5\sqrt{2}=\sqrt{50}.
Order: \boldsymbol{3\sqrt{5},\ 4\sqrt{3},\ 7,\ 5\sqrt{2},\ 2\sqrt{13}}
(b) 2\sqrt[3]{7},\ \ 3\sqrt[3]{2},\ \ \sqrt[3]{60},\ \ 4
2\sqrt[3]{7}=\sqrt[3]{56}, 3\sqrt[3]{2}=\sqrt[3]{54}, \sqrt[3]{60}, 4=\sqrt[3]{64}.
Order: \boldsymbol{3\sqrt[3]{2},\ 2\sqrt[3]{7},\ \sqrt[3]{60},\ 4}

Restrictions

Even index: the radicand must be \ge0. Odd index: any real number works. A radical in a denominator also cannot be 0, so its radicand must be >0. Dividing an inequality by a negative number flips the sign.

Variable radicands

Divide each exponent by the index: the quotient goes outside, the remainder stays inside. Going the other way (mixed to entire), each outside factor goes in raised to the index.

Example 2

Restrictions and variable radicands

(a) State the restriction on the variable for which each radical is defined (a real number).

(i) \sqrt{3x+12}
3x+12\ge0
\boldsymbol{x\ge-4}
(ii) \sqrt{10-4x}
10-4x\ge0
-4x\ge-10
\boldsymbol{x\le\tfrac{5}{2}} (sign flips)
(iii) \sqrt[3]{x-9}
Odd index: no restriction (x is any real number).
(iv) \dfrac{5}{\sqrt{x-1}}
x-1\ge0 and \sqrt{x-1}\ne0, so \boldsymbol{x>1}

(b) Simplify. Assume a,\ b,\ x,\ y\ge0.

(i) \sqrt{75x^4y^7}
=\sqrt{25x^4y^6}\cdot\sqrt{3y}
=\boldsymbol{5x^2y^3\sqrt{3y}}
(ii) \sqrt[3]{-40a^7b^3}
=\sqrt[3]{-8a^6b^3}\cdot\sqrt[3]{5a}
=\boldsymbol{-2a^2b\sqrt[3]{5a}}
(iii) Write 3x\sqrt{2x} as an entire radical.
=\sqrt{(3x)^2\cdot2x}=\sqrt{9x^2\cdot2x}
=\boldsymbol{\sqrt{18x^3}}

Adding, Subtracting and Multiplying

Add and subtract: simplify every radical first, then treat each like radical as a unit (like x) and add the coefficients. Unlike radicals stay separate.
Example 3

Simplify, then combine

Simplify. Assume x\ge0.

(a) 2\sqrt{50}-3\sqrt{18}+\sqrt{32}
=2(5\sqrt{2})-3(3\sqrt{2})+4\sqrt{2}
=10\sqrt{2}-9\sqrt{2}+4\sqrt{2}
=\boldsymbol{5\sqrt{2}}
(b) \sqrt[3]{54}+4\sqrt[3]{16}-\sqrt[3]{250}
=3\sqrt[3]{2}+4(2\sqrt[3]{2})-5\sqrt[3]{2}
=3\sqrt[3]{2}+8\sqrt[3]{2}-5\sqrt[3]{2}
=\boldsymbol{6\sqrt[3]{2}}
(c) \sqrt{12x^3}-x\sqrt{27x}+5\sqrt{3x^3}
=2x\sqrt{3x}-3x\sqrt{3x}+5x\sqrt{3x}
=\boldsymbol{4x\sqrt{3x}}

Multiplying: every term times every term

An area model keeps track: one box for each pair of terms. Inside each box, multiply coefficients with coefficients and radicands with radicands, and remember \left(\sqrt{a}\right)^2=a. Then collect like terms.

Example 4

Expand and simplify

(a) 3\sqrt{2}\left(4\sqrt{6}-\sqrt{10}\right)
=12\sqrt{12}-3\sqrt{20}
=12(2\sqrt{3})-3(2\sqrt{5})
=\boldsymbol{24\sqrt{3}-6\sqrt{5}}
(b) \left(2\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{5}\right)\left(\sqrt{3}+4\sqrt{5}\right)
=2(3)+8\sqrt{15}-\sqrt{15}-4(5)
=6+7\sqrt{15}-20
=\boldsymbol{-14+7\sqrt{15}}
(c) \left(3\sqrt{2}-2\right)^2
=(3\sqrt{2})^2-2(3\sqrt{2})(2)+2^2
=18-12\sqrt{2}+4
=\boldsymbol{22-12\sqrt{2}}
(d) \left(5+2\sqrt{6}\right)\left(5-2\sqrt{6}\right)
Conjugates: =5^2-(2\sqrt{6})^2
=25-24=\boldsymbol{1}
(The middle terms cancel, so the product is rational.)

Dividing and Rationalizing

To get a radical out of a denominator, multiply by a form of 1. One term: multiply top and bottom by the radical that makes the radicand a perfect power (\sqrt{3}\cdot\sqrt{3}=3, \sqrt[3]{9}\cdot\sqrt[3]{3}=3). Two terms: multiply by the conjugate, so the middle terms cancel: (a+\sqrt{b})(a-\sqrt{b})=a^2-b.
Example 5

Divide and rationalize

Simplify. Write each answer with a rational denominator.

(a) \dfrac{10\sqrt{42}}{5\sqrt{6}}
=\dfrac{10}{5}\sqrt{\dfrac{42}{6}}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{7}}
(b) \dfrac{\sqrt{45}-6\sqrt{10}}{3\sqrt{5}}
=\dfrac{3\sqrt{5}}{3\sqrt{5}}-\dfrac{6\sqrt{10}}{3\sqrt{5}}
=\boldsymbol{1-2\sqrt{2}}
(c) \dfrac{15}{2\sqrt{3}}
=\dfrac{15}{2\sqrt{3}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}}=\dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{6}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{2}}
(d) \dfrac{6}{\sqrt[3]{9}}
9=3^2, so multiply by \sqrt[3]{3} to make \sqrt[3]{27}:
\dfrac{6}{\sqrt[3]{9}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{3}}{\sqrt[3]{3}}=\dfrac{6\sqrt[3]{3}}{3}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt[3]{3}}
(e) \sqrt{\dfrac{5}{8}}
=\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{8}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2\sqrt{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{4}}
Example 6

Conjugates

Rationalize the denominator and simplify.

(a) \dfrac{8}{\sqrt{7}-\sqrt{3}}
=\dfrac{8}{\sqrt{7}-\sqrt{3}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{7}+\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{7}+\sqrt{3}}
=\dfrac{8(\sqrt{7}+\sqrt{3})}{7-3}
=2(\sqrt{7}+\sqrt{3})=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{7}+2\sqrt{3}}
(b) \dfrac{3+\sqrt{5}}{3-\sqrt{5}}
=\dfrac{(3+\sqrt{5})(3+\sqrt{5})}{(3-\sqrt{5})(3+\sqrt{5})}
=\dfrac{9+6\sqrt{5}+5}{9-5}=\dfrac{14+6\sqrt{5}}{4}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{7+3\sqrt{5}}{2}}
(c) \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2\sqrt{3}+\sqrt{2}}
=\dfrac{\sqrt{6}(2\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2})}{(2\sqrt{3})^2-(\sqrt{2})^2}
=\dfrac{2\sqrt{18}-\sqrt{12}}{12-2}=\dfrac{6\sqrt{2}-2\sqrt{3}}{10}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{3}}{5}}

Problem Solving

In a measurement problem, decide the operation from the formula: a perimeter adds lengths (combine like radicals), an area multiplies them (conjugates give a rational answer), and a diagonal uses the Pythagorean theorem. Give exact answers in simplest form, with units.
Example 7

An exact rectangle

A rectangle has length \left(2\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{3}\right) m and width \left(2\sqrt{5}-\sqrt{3}\right) m.

2\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{3}2\sqrt{5}-\sqrt{3}
(a) Determine the exact perimeter.
P=2\left[(2\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{3})+(2\sqrt{5}-\sqrt{3})\right]
=2(4\sqrt{5})=\boldsymbol{8\sqrt{5}} m
(b) Determine the exact area.
A=(2\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{3})(2\sqrt{5}-\sqrt{3})
=20-3=\boldsymbol{17} m² (conjugates)
(c) Determine the exact length of the diagonal, d.
d^2=(2\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{3})^2+(2\sqrt{5}-\sqrt{3})^2
=(20+4\sqrt{15}+3)+(20-4\sqrt{15}+3)=46
d=\boldsymbol{\sqrt{46}} m (46 has no perfect-square factor, so this is simplest form).

Rational or irrational?

Simplify first, then decide. A root of a perfect power is rational; anything with a radical left over (in simplest form) is irrational. Two irrationals can multiply to a rational number, and conjugates always do.

Example 8

Classify, and write in the form a+b\sqrt{5}

(a) Classify each number as rational or irrational.

(i) \sqrt{196}
=14, rational
(ii) \sqrt[3]{-27}
=-3, rational
(iii) \sqrt{40}
=2\sqrt{10}, irrational
(iv) (\sqrt{7}-\sqrt{2})(\sqrt{7}+\sqrt{2})
=7-2=5, rational
(v) \dfrac{\sqrt{50}}{\sqrt{2}}
=\sqrt{25}=5, rational
(vi) 2+\sqrt{3}
rational plus irrational: irrational
(b) The expression \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{5}-1}-\sqrt{20} can be written in the form a+b\sqrt{5}. Determine a and b.
\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{5}-1}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{5}+1}{\sqrt{5}+1}=\dfrac{4(\sqrt{5}+1)}{5-1}=\sqrt{5}+1, and \sqrt{20}=2\sqrt{5}.
So the expression is \sqrt{5}+1-2\sqrt{5}=1-\sqrt{5}:   \boldsymbol{a=1}, \boldsymbol{b=-1}.

Key ideas

Mixed and entire. Out: take the largest perfect nth power out of the radicand. In: raise the coefficient to the index. To compare, make every number an entire radical with the same index.
Choose the operation. +/-: simplify, then combine like radicals only. \times: coefficients together, radicands together, every term times every term.
Rationalize. One term: multiply by the radical that makes a perfect power. Two terms: multiply by the conjugate; (a+\sqrt{b})(a-\sqrt{b})=a^2-b.
Finish in simplest form. No perfect-power factors, no fractions under a root, no radicals in a denominator, like radicals combined, fractions reduced, restrictions stated.
