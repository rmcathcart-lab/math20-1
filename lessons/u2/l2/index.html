Radicals with Variables and Their Restrictions

Letters under the root sign. First the question which values of x are allowed? (a square root of a negative is not a real number). Then pulling powers out of the root: the part that comes out goes in front, the radicand keeps the rest.

  • remember that a radical in a denominator also cannot be 0, so its radicand must be >0

Warm-up

The Root Machine

Drop in ball x: the machine subtracts 6 and takes the square root, \sqrt{x-6}. Not a real number? It JAMS.

1
What comes out for ball 10? For ball 22?
Ball 10: \sqrt{10-6}=\sqrt4=2. Ball 22: \sqrt{16}=4.
2
Ball 14 gives \sqrt8. Write it as a mixed radical. What does ball 6 give?
\sqrt8=\sqrt{4\cdot2}=2\sqrt2. Ball 6: \sqrt0=0, no jam.
3
Which balls jam the machine? Which numbers would?
Balls 5 and −2: x-6 is negative. Any number less than 6 jams it.
!
The big one. Flip the switch: now the machine takes the cube root, \sqrt[3]{x-6}. Which balls jam it? Guess first.
A 5 and −2 again
B only −2
C none of them
D all of them
C, none. A cube root of a negative is negative: \sqrt[3]{-1}=-1 because (-1)^3=-1, and ball −2 gives \sqrt[3]{-8}=-2.
Two equal factors can never multiply to a negative, but three can. Today: which x-values each radical allows.

Restrictions on the Variable

A radical with a variable radicand, such as \sqrt{x-6}, is a real number only for some values of x. The values that are allowed are written as a restriction.
Even index
\sqrt{\ \ },\ \sqrt[4]{\ \ },\ \dots
The radicand must be \ge 0. Set the radicand \ge0 and solve.
Odd index
\sqrt[3]{\ \ },\ \sqrt[5]{\ \ },\ \dots
No restriction: the variable can be any real number, x\in\mathbb{R}.
In a denominator
\dfrac{8}{\sqrt{x+2}}
It also cannot equal 0, so the radicand must be >0.
!
Dividing or multiplying both sides of an inequality by a negative number reverses the sign. -3x\ge-9 becomes x\le3, not x\ge3.

Which x-values are allowed?

Solve the radicand's inequality, shade the answer on a number line, then test it with a probe: the probe turns red wherever the radicand is negative.

Why does the sign reverse?

Multiplying by a negative number flips every point to the other side of 0, and the order of the points flips with it.

Example 1

State the restriction

State the restriction on the variable.

(a) \sqrt{x-6}
x-6\ge0
\boldsymbol{x\ge6}
(b) \sqrt{2x+5}
2x+5\ge0\ \Rightarrow\ 2x\ge-5
\boldsymbol{x\ge-\dfrac52}
(c) \sqrt{9-3x}
9-3x\ge0\ \Rightarrow\ -3x\ge-9
\boldsymbol{x\le3} (sign reverses)
(d) \sqrt[3]{x-4}
Odd index: \boldsymbol{x\in\mathbb{R}} (no restriction).
(e) \sqrt[4]{-x}
-x\ge0\ \Rightarrow\ \boldsymbol{x\le0}
(f) \sqrt{12x}
12x\ge0\ \Rightarrow\ \boldsymbol{x\ge0}
Example 2

Squares, cubes and denominators

For what values of x is each expression defined? Explain.

(a) \sqrt{x^2+4}
x^2\ge0, so x^2+4\ge4>0 for every x: \boldsymbol{x\in\mathbb{R}}.
(b) \sqrt{x^2}
x^2\ge0 for every x: \boldsymbol{x\in\mathbb{R}}.
(c) \sqrt{x^3}
x^3 is negative when x is negative, so \boldsymbol{x\ge0}.
(d) \dfrac{8}{\sqrt{x+2}}
x+2\ge0 and \sqrt{x+2}\ne0, so x+2>0: \boldsymbol{x>-2}.

Where is the radicand above zero?

Graph the radicand. The allowed x-values are where the graph is on or above the x-axis (only above for a denominator).

Simplifying Powers Under a Radical

A power comes out of a root by dividing its exponent by the index: \sqrt{x^8}=x^4 because (x^4)^2=x^8, and \sqrt[3]{x^{12}}=x^4 because (x^4)^3=x^{12}. When the exponent does not divide evenly, the remainder stays inside:
\sqrt{x^7}=\sqrt{x^6\cdot x}=\textcolor{#0f7c80}{x^3}\sqrt{\textcolor{#7b4fc4}{x}}\sqrt[3]{a^{11}}=\sqrt[3]{a^9\cdot a^2}=\textcolor{#0f7c80}{a^3}\sqrt[3]{\textcolor{#7b4fc4}{a^2}}
Key idea
\sqrt[n]{x^m}: divide m by n. The quotient is the exponent outside; the remainder is the exponent left inside.

Groups of the index escape

Under a square root, pairs of equal factors come out as one; under a cube root, groups of three.

A caution about even roots

Try x=-5: \sqrt{x^2}=\sqrt{25}=5, which is not x. In general \sqrt{x^2}=|x| (the absolute value of x, studied in Unit 7). To avoid this, for an even index we state that the variables are non-negative (x\ge0); then \sqrt{x^2}=x. For an odd index no restriction is needed: \sqrt[3]{x^3}=x for every x.
Example 3

Powers out of the root

Simplify. State the restrictions where needed.

(a) \sqrt{x^8}
\boldsymbol{x^4},\ \ x\ge0
(b) \sqrt{a^7}
\sqrt{a^6\cdot a}=\boldsymbol{a^3\sqrt a},\ \ a\ge0
(c) \sqrt[3]{y^9}
\boldsymbol{y^3} (no restriction)
(d) \sqrt[3]{m^{11}}
11\div3=3\text{ R }2: \boldsymbol{m^3\sqrt[3]{m^2}}
(e) \sqrt[4]{x^9}
9\div4=2\text{ R }1: \boldsymbol{x^2\sqrt[4]{x}},\ \ x\ge0
(f) \sqrt{x^4y^5}
\boldsymbol{x^2y^2\sqrt y},\ \ x\ge0,\ y\ge0

Simplifying Radicals with Variable Radicands

Simplify the number and each variable separately, then multiply everything that came out and everything that stayed in:
\sqrt{18x^3}=\sqrt{9\cdot2\cdot x^2\cdot x}=\textcolor{#0f7c80}{3x}\sqrt{\textcolor{#7b4fc4}{2x}},\quad x\ge0
Unless told otherwise, for an even index state that each variable is \ge0. A negative number under an odd root comes out as negative: \sqrt[3]{-8x^4}=-2x\sqrt[3]{x}.

Sort it: out or in?

Example 4

Numbers and variables together

Write each radical in simplest form. State the restrictions where needed.

(a) \sqrt{45a^5}
\sqrt{9\cdot5\cdot a^4\cdot a}
=\boldsymbol{3a^2\sqrt{5a}},\ \ a\ge0
(b) \sqrt{72x^4y^7}
\sqrt{36\cdot2\cdot x^4\cdot y^6\cdot y}
=\boldsymbol{6x^2y^3\sqrt{2y}},\ \ x,y\ge0
(c) \sqrt[3]{-54m^7}
\sqrt[3]{-27\cdot2\cdot m^6\cdot m}
=\boldsymbol{-3m^2\sqrt[3]{2m}} (no restriction)
(d) \sqrt{98x^2y^3}
\sqrt{49\cdot2\cdot x^2\cdot y^2\cdot y}
=\boldsymbol{7xy\sqrt{2y}},\ \ x,y\ge0
(e) \sqrt[3]{40a^4b^9}
\sqrt[3]{8\cdot5\cdot a^3\cdot a\cdot b^9}
=\boldsymbol{2ab^3\sqrt[3]{5a}}
(f) \sqrt[4]{48x^9}
\sqrt[4]{16\cdot3\cdot x^8\cdot x}
=\boldsymbol{2x^2\sqrt[4]{3x}},\ \ x\ge0
4

Mixed to Entire with Variables

Raise everything in front of the radical to the power of the index, then multiply it into the radicand. As with numbers, a negative sign in front goes inside only for an odd index.
\textcolor{#0f7c80}{3x}\sqrt{\textcolor{#7b4fc4}{2x}}=\sqrt{(3x)^2\cdot2x}=\sqrt{9x^2\cdot2x}=\sqrt{18x^3},\quad x\ge0
Example 5

Everything goes inside

Write each mixed radical as an entire radical. Assume variables under an even index are \ge0.

(a) 4a\sqrt{a}
\sqrt{16a^2\cdot a}=\boldsymbol{\sqrt{16a^3}}
(b) 2x^2\sqrt{5y}
\sqrt{4x^4\cdot5y}=\boldsymbol{\sqrt{20x^4y}}
(c) -3m\sqrt[3]{2m^2}
\sqrt[3]{(-3m)^3\cdot2m^2}=\sqrt[3]{-27m^3\cdot2m^2}
=\boldsymbol{\sqrt[3]{-54m^5}}
(d) ab^2\sqrt[3]{4a}
\sqrt[3]{a^3b^6\cdot4a}=\boldsymbol{\sqrt[3]{4a^4b^6}}
(e) -2y\sqrt{y}
Even index: sign stays out.
-\sqrt{4y^2\cdot y}=\boldsymbol{-\sqrt{4y^3}}
(f) 5x\sqrt{3}
\sqrt{25x^2\cdot3}=\boldsymbol{\sqrt{75x^2}}
!
Raise the whole coefficient, not just the number. In 3x^2\sqrt{2y}, both the 3 and the x^2 are squared: (3x^2)^2=9x^4, not 9x^2 or 3x^4.

Putting It Together

Before you call an answer finished, run it through the simplest-form checklist.

Example 6

Squares, cubes and triangles

Give each answer in simplest form, with its restriction.

(a) A square has an area of 48x^6\ \text{cm}^2. Determine its side length.
s=\sqrt{48x^6}=\sqrt{16\cdot3\cdot x^6}
=\boldsymbol{4x^3\sqrt3} cm, x>0
(b) A cube has a volume of 54a^4\ \text{m}^3. Determine its edge length.
e=\sqrt[3]{54a^4}=\sqrt[3]{27\cdot2\cdot a^3\cdot a}
=\boldsymbol{3a\sqrt[3]{2a}} m (a length, so a>0)
(c) A right triangle has legs 3x and 6x. Determine the length of the hypotenuse.
c=\sqrt{(3x)^2+(6x)^2}=\sqrt{9x^2+36x^2}
=\sqrt{45x^2}=\boldsymbol{3x\sqrt5},\ \ x>0
Example 7

Allowed, evaluated, or jammed?

Consider the expression \sqrt{20-4x}.

(a) State the restriction on x.
20-4x\ge0\Rightarrow-4x\ge-20
\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{x\le5}
(b) Evaluate the expression exactly, in simplest form, for x=-5 and for x=2.
x=-5:\ \sqrt{20+20}=\sqrt{40}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{10}}
x=2:\ \sqrt{20-8}=\sqrt{12}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt3}
(c) Can the expression be evaluated for x=6? Explain.
No: 6>5; the radicand would be 20-24=-4, and \sqrt{-4} is not a real number.
✓

Key ideas

Restrictions. Even index: solve radicand \ge0 (flip the sign if you divide by a negative). Odd index: x\in\mathbb{R}. In a denominator: radicand >0.
Divide the exponent by the index. \sqrt{x^7}=x^3\sqrt x: the quotient goes outside, the remainder stays inside. For an even index state x\ge0, because \sqrt{x^2}=|x|.
Number and variables separately. \sqrt{18x^3}=\sqrt{9\cdot2\cdot x^2\cdot x}=3x\sqrt{2x}. A negative under an odd root comes out negative.
Mixed → entire. Raise everything in front to the index and multiply it in: 3x\sqrt{2x}=\sqrt{9x^2\cdot2x}=\sqrt{18x^3}. A negative sign goes in only for an odd index.
