Solutions of a Quadratic Inequality in Two Variables

A quadratic inequality in two variables has one of the forms

y<ax^2+bx+cy\le ax^2+bx+cy>ax^2+bx+cy\ge ax^2+bx+ca\ne0

A solution is an ordered pair (x,\ y) that makes the inequality true. There are infinitely many, so we show them as a shaded solution region. As with lines in Lesson 4, the graph of the related equation y=ax^2+bx+c is the boundary. A parabola splits the plane into two regions: the region inside the parabola (the side it opens toward) and the region outside it. Every point of one region is a solution, and no point of the other is.

Example 1

Which points are solutions?

Consider the inequality y\ge x^2-2x-3. The boundary y=x^2-2x-3 is graphed.

(a) For each point, find x^2-2x-3 and decide whether the point is a solution. Plot the points, marking each solution.
Pointx^2-2x-3Is y\ge x^2-2x-3?
(0,\ 0)-3yes: 0\ge-3
(4,\ 1)5no: 1<5
(1,\ -5)-4no: -5<-4
(-2,\ 5)5yes: 5=5
(2,\ 1)-3yes: 1\ge-3

Solutions: (0,\ 0), (-2,\ 5), (2,\ 1), (3,\ 0) (dots). Not solutions: (4,\ 1), (1,\ -5), (0,\ -4) (crosses).
(b) Describe where the solutions lie.
All the solutions are on the parabola or inside it (above it). The points that are not solutions are outside it (below it). The whole region on or above the parabola is the solution region (shaded).
(c) Which of the points are solutions of y>x^2-2x-3? Explain the difference.
Only (0,\ 0) and (2,\ 1). The points (-2,\ 5) and (3,\ 0) are on the boundary: they make y=x^2-2x-3 true, so they satisfy \ge but not >.

2

Graphing the Solution Region

To graph a quadratic inequality in two variables
1Graph the boundary y=ax^2+bx+c: find the vertex, the x-intercepts (factor) and the y-intercept, and use symmetry. Draw it solid for \le, \ge and dashed for <, >.
2Choose a test point that is not on the parabola ((0,\ 0) is easiest when it is not on it) and substitute it into the inequality.
3If the test point makes the inequality true, shade the region (inside or outside) that contains it; if false, shade the other region.

One test point is enough, because every point on the same side of the parabola gives the same answer. Use the test point to check the “above/below” rule, not instead of thinking about it.

Example 2

Factor, test, shade

Graph the solution region of each inequality.

(a) y\le x^2-6x+5
y=(x-1)(x-5): x-intercepts 1 and 5.
Axis x=3; vertex (3,\ -4); y-intercept 5.
Solid (the sign is \le). Test (0,\ 0): 0\le5 true.
Shade the side with (0,\ 0): below (outside) the parabola.
(b) y>-x^2-2x+8
y=-(x^2+2x-8)=-(x+4)(x-2): x-intercepts -4, 2.
Axis x=-1; vertex (-1,\ 9); y-intercept 8.
Dashed (the sign is >). Test (0,\ 0): 0>8 false.
Shade the side without (0,\ 0): above (outside) the parabola.
Example 3

Use the form you are given

Graph the solution region of each inequality. Use the form the equation is given in.

(a) y\ge 2(x-1)^2-8
Vertex (1,\ -8), opens up, step pattern 2,\ 6,\ \dots
x-intercepts: (x-1)^2=4, x=-1 or 3; y-intercept -6.
Solid. Test (0,\ 0): 0\ge-6 true.
Shade above (inside) the parabola.
(b) y<-\frac{1}{2}(x+2)(x-4)
x-intercepts -2 and 4; axis x=1.
Vertex: y=-\frac{1}{2}(3)(-3)=4.5, so (1,\ 4.5); y-intercept 4.
Dashed. Test (0,\ 0): 0<4 true.
Shade below (outside) the parabola.
3

Rearranging First, and Choosing a Test Point

If y is not alone on the left, solve for y first, as you would solve an equation, with one change: multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality sign. For example, -y>3 becomes y<-3. (Dividing by a positive number keeps the sign.)

When c=0 the parabola passes through (0,\ 0), so (0,\ 0) cannot be the test point: it gives an equality, which tells you nothing about the regions. Use a point such as (1,\ 0), (0,\ 1) or a point straight above the vertex.

Example 5

When (0,\ 0) is on the parabola

(a) Explain why (0,\ 0) cannot be used as the test point for y\le x^2-4x, the inequality from Example 4(b).
(0,\ 0) is on the boundary: 0^2-4(0)=0. It is in neither region, so it cannot tell us which side to shade.
(b) Graph the solution region of y\le x^2-4x.
y=x(x-4): x-intercepts 0 and 4; axis x=2; vertex (2,\ -4). Solid.
Test (2,\ 0), a point above the vertex: is 0\le2^2-4(2)=-4?  0\le-4 is false.
(2,\ 0) is inside, so shade outside: the region below the parabola.

Writing an Inequality from a Graph

To write the inequality for a shaded region
1Write the equation of the boundary parabola: from the vertex (p,\ q) and a point, use y=a(x-p)^2+q; from the x-intercepts r, s and a point, use y=a(x-r)(x-s). Substitute the point to find a.
2Choose the sign: shaded above \Rightarrow > or \ge; shaded below \Rightarrow < or \le; solid \Rightarrow include “=”; dashed \Rightarrow do not.
3Check with a point in the shaded region.
Example 6

Read the graph, write the inequality

Write the inequality whose solution region is shown, in general form.

(a) Vertex (2,\ -3); the point (0,\ 1) is on the boundary.
y=a(x-2)^2-3; at (0,\ 1): 1=4a-3, a=1.
y=(x-2)^2-3=x^2-4x+1.
Dashed, shaded above: y>x^2-4x+1.
Check (2,\ 0): 0>4-8+1=-3 ✓
(b) x-intercepts -3 and 1; y-intercept 6.
y=a(x+3)(x-1); at (0,\ 6): 6=a(3)(-1), a=-2.
y=-2(x^2+2x-3)=-2x^2-4x+6.
Solid, shaded below: y\le-2x^2-4x+6.
Check (0,\ 0): 0\le6 ✓

Regions in Context

Regions bounded by parabolas describe real situations: the space under an arch, under the path of a ball or a jet of water, or inside a reflector. A point (x,\ y) is inside the region when it satisfies the inequality, and an object fits when all its corner points do.

Example 8

The tunnel arch

The opening of a tunnel is a parabolic arch. With the origin at the centre of the road, the arch is y=-0.375x^2+6, where x and y are in metres.

(a) Write the inequalities for the points on or under the arch and on or above the road. How wide is the road under the arch?
y\le-0.375x^2+6  and  y\ge0.
Road: -0.375x^2+6=0\Rightarrow x^2=16\Rightarrow x=\pm4. The road is 8 m wide.
(b) A truck 3.2 m wide and 4.5 m tall drives along the centre of the road. Will it fit? Show the test.
Its top corners are (\pm1.6,\ 4.5). Test (1.6,\ 4.5):
-0.375(1.6)^2+6=-0.96+6=5.04, and 4.5\le5.04 is true.
Both corners are under the arch (by symmetry), so it fits, with about 0.5 m to spare.
(c) How tall can a 4 m wide truck in the centre be?
Corners at x=\pm2: y\le-0.375(4)+6=4.5. At most 4.5 m tall.
✓

Key ideas

Solid or dashed, above or below. Solid for \le, \ge; dashed for <, >. With y alone: >, \ge above; <, \le below.
Graph it. (1) the boundary from its vertex and intercepts; (2) one test point not on the parabola; (3) true: shade its side, false: shade the other side.
Rearrange first. Multiplying or dividing by a negative reverses the sign. If (0,\ 0) is on the parabola, test another point, such as one above the vertex.
From a graph, and in context. y=a(x-p)^2+q or y=a(x-r)(x-s); a point gives a. Then the sign from the shading and the boundary. An object fits when all its corners satisfy the inequality.
