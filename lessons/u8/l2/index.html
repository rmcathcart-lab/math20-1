The Marble Race

Two marbles are let go at the same moment, from the same height. Marble A rolls down a straight ramp: the shortest path to the finish. Marble B rolls down a curved ramp that dips first: a longer path.

Which marble reaches the finish first?

?
Vote first: A, B, or a tie?
Marble B wins, and not by a little. Its ramp drops steeply right at the start, so B picks up speed immediately and covers most of its longer path fast. A speeds up slowly the whole way down its gentle slope. A longer path can be the quicker one! The fastest ramp of all is a curve called a cycloid, the brachistochrone (“shortest time” in Greek): Johann Bernoulli posed it as a challenge in 1696, and Isaac Newton solved it overnight. The twist: on a cycloid ramp, a marble let go from any starting point takes exactly the same time to reach the bottom, so a marble started halfway down ties one started at the top.

Quadratic–Quadratic Systems

A quadratic–quadratic system is a pair of quadratic equations, such as y=a_1x^2+b_1x+c_1 and y=a_2x^2+b_2x+c_2. Its solutions are the points of intersection of the two parabolas. Two different parabolas meet in two points, one point or no points. If the two equations describe the same parabola (they may look different, as y=(x-1)^2 and y=x^2-2x+1 do), every point on it is a solution: the system has infinitely many solutions.

Example 1

The graphs of y=x^2-1 and y=-x^2+2x+3 are shown.

(a) State the solutions of the system.
\boldsymbol{(-1,\ 0)} and \boldsymbol{(2,\ 3)}
(b) Verify each solution in both equations.
(-1,\ 0): (-1)^2-1=0\ \checkmark;  -1-2+3=0\ \checkmark
(2,\ 3): 4-1=3\ \checkmark;  -4+4+3=3\ \checkmark

Solving Algebraically

Write both equations as y=\dots Setting the right sides equal (substitution) is the same as subtracting one equation from the other (elimination): y is eliminated and one equation in x is left.

To solve y=a_1x^2+b_1x+c_1,  y=a_2x^2+b_2x+c_2
  1. Set the right sides equal (or subtract the equations).
  2. Collect all terms on one side and solve for x (factor, or use the quadratic formula).
  3. Substitute each x into the simpler original equation to find y.
  4. Verify in the other equation and write the ordered pairs.
Example 3

Solve and verify

Solve each system algebraically and verify one solution.

(a) \begin{aligned}y&=x^2-2x-4\\ y&=-x^2+4x+4\end{aligned}
x^2-2x-4=-x^2+4x+4
2x^2-6x-8=0
x^2-3x-4=0
(x-4)(x+1)=0, so x=4 or x=-1
x=4: y=16-8-4=4
x=-1: y=1+2-4=-1
Solutions: \boldsymbol{(4,\ 4)} and \boldsymbol{(-1,\ -1)}
Check (4,\ 4): -16+16+4=4\ \checkmark
(b) \begin{aligned}y&=3x^2+2x-1\\ y&=x^2-3x+2\end{aligned}
Subtract: 2x^2+5x-3=0
(2x-1)(x+3)=0, so x=\frac{1}{2} or x=-3
x=\frac{1}{2}: y=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{3}{2}+2=\frac{3}{4}
x=-3: y=9+9+2=20
Solutions: \boldsymbol{\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\ \tfrac{3}{4}\right)} and \boldsymbol{(-3,\ 20)}
Check (-3,\ 20): 27-6-1=20\ \checkmark

When the x^2-Terms Cancel

If the two equations have the same x^2-coefficient, eliminating y also eliminates x^2, and what is left is linear:

  • a linear equation mx=n with m\ne0: exactly one solution;
  • a false statement such as 0=5: no solution (one parabola is a vertical translation of the other);
  • a true statement 0=0: infinitely many solutions (the equations describe the same parabola).
Example 5

One, none or infinitely many?

Solve each system, or explain why it has no solution or infinitely many solutions.

(a) \begin{aligned}y&=x^2+4x+1\\ y&=x^2-2x+7\end{aligned}
Subtract: 6x-6=0
x=1
y=1+4+1=6
One solution: \boldsymbol{(1,\ 6)}
Check: 1-2+7=6\ \checkmark
(b) \begin{aligned}y&=2x^2-3x+1\\ y&=2x^2-3x-4\end{aligned}
Subtract: 0=5, false.
No solution: the second parabola is the first one translated 5 units down.
(c) \begin{aligned}y&=2(x-1)^2+3\\ y&=2x^2-4x+5\end{aligned}
Expand: 2(x^2-2x+1)+3=2x^2-4x+5
The equations are identical (subtracting gives 0=0): infinitely many solutions.

The Number of Solutions

When the x^2-terms do not cancel, the equation left after eliminating y is quadratic, and its discriminant counts the solutions.

Without solving, determine the number of solutions of each system.

(a) y=x^2+2x+3,  y=-x^2-2x+1
2x^2+4x+2=0:  16-4(2)(2)=0

2x^2-2x+3=0:  4-4(2)(3)=-20<0
No solution.

Example 7

Choosing k

For which values of k does the system y=x^2+k and y=-x^2+4x have exactly one solution? two solutions? no solution? Determine the solution in the one-solution case.
x^2+k=-x^2+4x\Rightarrow 2x^2-4x+k=0
Discriminant: (-4)^2-4(2)(k)=16-8k
One: 16-8k=0\Rightarrow \boldsymbol{k=2}.  Two: 16-8k>0\Rightarrow \boldsymbol{k<2}.  None: \boldsymbol{k>2}.
When k=2: 2x^2-4x+2=0\Rightarrow (x-1)^2=0, x=1, y=1+2=3. The solution is \boldsymbol{(1,\ 3)}.

Fractional and Decimal Coefficients

Clear fractions or decimals after eliminating y: multiply the resulting equation by a number that makes every coefficient an integer. Then factor or use the formula as usual. In a context, check that each solution makes sense.

Example 8

Clear them, then solve

(a) Solve y=\frac{1}{2}x^2-2x+1 and y=-\frac{1}{4}x^2+x+10.
\frac{1}{2}x^2-2x+1=-\frac{1}{4}x^2+x+10
\frac{3}{4}x^2-3x-9=0
Multiply by \frac{4}{3}: x^2-4x-12=0
(x-6)(x+2)=0
x=6: y=18-12+1=7
x=-2: y=2+4+1=7
Solutions: \boldsymbol{(6,\ 7)} and \boldsymbol{(-2,\ 7)}
(b) Two water jets in a fountain follow the paths h=-x^2+6x and h=-0.5x^2+2x+2, where x is the horizontal distance and h\ge0 the height, both in metres. Where do the jets cross? Answer to the nearest hundredth.
-x^2+6x=-0.5x^2+2x+2
-0.5x^2+4x-2=0; multiply by -2: x^2-8x+4=0
x=\dfrac{8\pm\sqrt{64-16}}{2}=4\pm2\sqrt{3}
x\approx0.54: h\approx2.93;  x\approx7.46: h\approx-10.93
Reject the second point: the height is negative (the first jet lands at x=6). The jets cross at about \boldsymbol{(0.54,\ 2.93)}: 0.54 m out and 2.93 m up.
✓

Key ideas

Where parabolas meet. The solutions are the intersection points: two, one or none for different parabolas, and infinitely many when both equations describe the same parabola.
Subtract, solve, substitute, verify. Eliminate y, solve the equation in x, substitute each x into an original equation, and verify in the other one.
Count the solutions. A quadratic left over: b^2-4ac says two, one or none. Same x^2-coefficient: mx=n gives one, 0=n none, 0=0 infinitely many.
Fractions and decimals. After eliminating y, multiply to make every coefficient an integer, then solve. In a context, reject solutions that make no sense.
