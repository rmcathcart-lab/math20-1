The Cat, the Turtle and the Table

A cat sits on a table and a turtle sits on the floor. From the top of the turtle to the top of the cat is 170 cm.

They swap places. Now, from the top of the cat to the top of the turtle is 130 cm.

How tall is the table?

?
Make a guess first. Then: how can you be sure?
150 cm. Put the two pictures on top of each other: two tables, and the cat and the turtle cancel out.
(\text{table}+\text{cat}-\text{turtle})+(\text{table}+\text{turtle}-\text{cat})=170+130, so 2\times\text{table}=300 and the table is 150 cm tall.
You never find out how tall the cat or the turtle is: only that the cat is 20 cm taller. A 50 cm cat and a 30 cm turtle work, and so do a 40 cm cat and a 20 cm turtle.

Problems Involving Systems

Many real problems have two unknown quantities linked by two conditions. Each condition becomes an equation, and together they form a system: a ball's path and a hillside, a plane's route and a radar's range, a company's revenue and its cost. The points where the graphs meet answer the question.

I can…
  • model a problem with a system: define the variables, write one equation per condition, solve, check, and answer in a sentence
  • solve linear–quadratic models (a path and a slope, a line and a circle) and quadratic–quadratic models (two thrown objects, revenue and cost)
  • interpret the solutions in context: break-even points, a profit interval, the greatest gap, and reject solutions that make no sense
  • use the discriminant to find when a line is tangent to a curve, and to find unknown constants
  • find the parameters of a model from two data points
RF6 Solve, algebraically and graphically, problems that involve systems of linear-quadratic and quadratic-quadratic equations in two variables.
Today: the five steps for modelling with a system, number and area problems, a hillside and a radar circle, two thrown balls, break-even and profit, tangents and unknown constants, and a model from data.
1

Modelling with a System of Equations

Many problems have two unknown quantities linked by two conditions. Each condition gives one equation, and together the equations form a system. When one equation is linear and the other quadratic, it is a linear–quadratic system; when both are quadratic, it is a quadratic–quadratic system. Every solution (x,\ y) of the system must fit both conditions.
Key idea: solving a problem with a system
1Define the variables, with units.
2Write one equation for each condition.
3Solve by substitution or elimination (or graph both equations and use intersect).
4Check each solution in the original conditions and reject any that make no sense (a negative length, a time before the start, a count that is not a whole number).
5Answer the question in a sentence, with units.
Example 1

Number problems

Write a system of equations and solve it.

(a) The difference of two numbers is 4. The larger number plus the square of the smaller number is 46. Determine the numbers.
Let x be the smaller number and y the larger.
y-x=4\Rightarrow y=x+4
y+x^2=46
Substitute: x+4+x^2=46
x^2+x-42=0
(x+7)(x-6)=0, so x=-7 or x=6.
x=6: y=10;   x=-7: y=-3.
The numbers are 6 and 10, or −7 and −3.
Check: 10+36=46 ✓;   -3+49=46 ✓
(b) Two positive integers have a sum of 15, and the sum of their squares is 117. Determine the integers.
x+y=15 and x^2+y^2=117
y=15-x:   x^2+(15-x)^2=117
x^2+225-30x+x^2=117
2x^2-30x+108=0\Rightarrow x^2-15x+54=0
(x-6)(x-9)=0, so x=6 or x=9.
x=6: y=9;   x=9: y=6 (the same pair).
The integers are 6 and 9.
Check: 36+81=117 ✓

The five steps on an area problem

A dog run is fenced on three sides against a garage wall. Change the width and both the shape and its area change: the area is a parabola, and the required area is a horizontal line.

Example 2

The dog run

A rectangular dog run is built against the wall of a garage, so fencing is needed on only three sides. The width x is measured out from the wall, and 20 m of fencing is used.

(a) Show that the area is A=20x-2x^2.
The side parallel to the wall is 20-2x.
A=x(20-2x)=20x-2x^2
(b) The run must have an area of 48\text{ m}^2. Solve the linear–quadratic system y=20x-2x^2, y=48 to determine the possible dimensions.
20x-2x^2=48\Rightarrow2x^2-20x+48=0\Rightarrow x^2-10x+24=0
(x-4)(x-6)=0, so x=4 or x=6.
x=4: 4 m by 20-8=12 m.   x=6: 6 m by 8 m. Both use 20 m and enclose 48\text{ m}^2.
(Graphically: the line y=48 meets the parabola at (4,\ 48) and (6,\ 48).)
(c) Could the run have an area of 60\text{ m}^2? Explain using the system y=20x-2x^2, y=60.
20x-2x^2=60\Rightarrow x^2-10x+30=0;   b^2-4ac=100-120=-20<0.
No real solution: the line y=60 misses the parabola. The largest area is at the vertex, x=5: A=50\text{ m}^2, so 60\text{ m}^2 is impossible.

Linear–Quadratic Models

A straight path or edge (a hillside, a road, a ramp, a cost that grows at a constant rate) is modelled by a line; a curved path (a thrown object, an arch) by a parabola. Their points of intersection are where the two meet. Substitution usually leads to a quadratic equation; factor it or use the quadratic formula.

A circle centred at the origin with radius r has equation x^2+y^2=r^2 (every point is r units from (0,\ 0), by the distance formula). It is also quadratic, and it is solved with a line the same way.
Example 3

A golf ball on a hillside

A golf ball is hit from a tee at the bottom of a hill. Its path is y=-0.05x^2+2x and the hillside is the line y=0.5x, where x is the horizontal distance from the tee and y is the height above the tee, both in metres.

(a) Where does the ball land on the hill?
-0.05x^2+2x=0.5x
-0.05x^2+1.5x=0
-0.05x(x-30)=0
x=0 (the tee) or x=30
y=0.5(30)=15
It lands at (30,\ 15).
(b) How far up the slope from the tee does it land, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
Distance from (0,\ 0) to (30,\ 15):
d=\sqrt{30^2+15^2}
=\sqrt{1125}
\approx33.5 m up the slope.
(c) What is the greatest vertical height of the ball above the hillside?
Vertical gap =(-0.05x^2+2x)-0.5x
=-0.05x^2+1.5x
Maximum at x=-\dfrac{1.5}{2(-0.05)}=15:
-0.05(225)+1.5(15)=11.25 m.
Check (a) by graphing. Enter Y_1=-0.05X^2+2X and Y_2=0.5X, a window of 0\le X\le35, 0\le Y\le25, then 2ndTRACE 5:intersect with a guess near 30 (a guess near 0 finds the tee).

A line and a circle

A radar station sees everything within its range: a circle. A plane on a straight route is a line. Substitute the line into the circle, and the solutions are where the plane enters and leaves the range.

Example 4

Radar range

A radar station at the origin detects aircraft within 10 km, so its range is the circle x^2+y^2=100 (units in kilometres). A plane flies along the line y=x+2.

(a) Determine the points where the plane enters and leaves the radar range.
x^2+(x+2)^2=100
2x^2+4x+4=100
x^2+2x-48=0
(x+8)(x-6)=0
x=-8: y=-6;   x=6: y=8
Points (-8,\ -6) and (6,\ 8).
(b) How far does the plane fly inside the range, to the nearest tenth of a kilometre?
d=\sqrt{(6-(-8))^2+(8-(-6))^2}
=\sqrt{196+196}
=\sqrt{392}\approx19.8 km
(c) Would a plane flying along y=x+15 be detected? Explain.
x^2+(x+15)^2=100
2x^2+30x+125=0
b^2-4ac=900-1000=-100<0
No intersection, so the plane stays outside the range and is not detected.

Quadratic–Quadratic Models

Two objects moving under gravity, two curved paths, or a revenue and a cost that are both quadratic give a quadratic–quadratic system. Set the two expressions equal (or subtract the equations) and solve. If the squared terms are the same, they cancel and there is at most one solution (unless the two equations are the same).

Example 5

Two balls in the air

At the same moment, Ava throws a ball upward from a balcony and Ben throws one upward from the ground. After t seconds the heights, in metres, are

Ava: h=-5t^2+10t+15Ben: h=-5t^2+20t
(a) When are the two balls at the same height, and what is that height?
-5t^2+10t+15=-5t^2+20t
The -5t^2 terms cancel: 15=10t, so t=1.5 s.
h=-5(1.5)^2+20(1.5)=-11.25+30=18.75 m
(Check with Ava's: -11.25+15+15=18.75 ✓)
After 1.5 s both balls are 18.75 m high.
(b) Which ball lands first, and how much sooner?
Ava: -5t^2+10t+15=0\Rightarrow t^2-2t-3=0
(t-3)(t+1)=0, t=3 s (-1 rejected).
Ben: -5t(t-4)=0, t=4 s (t=0 is the throw).
Ava's ball lands first, 1 s sooner.

Revenue, cost and profit

A business breaks even when its revenue (the money that comes in) equals its cost (the money that goes out). Where the revenue graph is above the cost graph, the business makes a profit: the vertical gap P=R-C.

Example 6

Phone cases: break-even and profit

A company makes x hundred phone cases. Its revenue and its cost, both in hundreds of dollars, are

R=-2x^2+60xC=x^2+6x+135
(a) The company breaks even when R=C. Determine the break-even quantities.
-2x^2+60x=x^2+6x+135
0=3x^2-54x+135
0=x^2-18x+45
(x-3)(x-15)=0
x=3 or x=15
It breaks even at 300 and at 1500 cases.
(b) For what quantities does the company make a profit?
Profit means R>C: the revenue parabola is above the cost parabola between the break-even points.
Test x=9: R=378, C=270, so R>C.
A profit for more than 300 and fewer than 1500 cases.
(c) Profit is P=R-C. Determine the greatest profit and the quantity that gives it.
P=-3x^2+54x-135
x=-\dfrac{54}{2(-3)}=9
P=-243+486-135=108
The greatest profit is $10 800, from 900 cases.
Break-even on the calculator. Y_1=-2X^2+60X, Y_2=X^2+6X+135, a window of 0\le X\le20, 0\le Y\le500, then 5:intersect twice, with a guess near each crossing (3, then 15).

Using the Discriminant: Tangents and Unknown Constants

Substituting one equation into the other gives a quadratic equation ax^2+bx+c=0. Its discriminant b^2-4ac counts the solutions of the system.

Key idea
b^2-4ac>0two solutions
the graphs cross twice
b^2-4ac=0one solution
the line is tangent to the parabola: it just touches it
b^2-4ac<0no solution
the graphs miss

When one equation contains an unknown constant such as k, write the discriminant in terms of k and use the condition you need.

Example 7

Tangent lines and unknown constants

(a) Determine the values of k for which the line y=kx-3 is tangent to the parabola y=x^2+1, and the point of tangency for each.
x^2+1=kx-3\Rightarrow x^2-kx+4=0
Tangent: b^2-4ac=0
(-k)^2-4(1)(4)=0\Rightarrow k^2=16\Rightarrow k=\pm4
k=4: x^2-4x+4=0\Rightarrow x=2, y=5; point (2,\ 5).
k=-4: x^2+4x+4=0\Rightarrow x=-2, y=5; point (-2,\ 5).
(b) For what values of n does the system y=x^2-4x+7 and y=2x+n have two solutions?
x^2-4x+7=2x+n\Rightarrow x^2-6x+(7-n)=0
Two solutions: b^2-4ac>0, with a=1, b=-6, c=7-n
(-6)^2-4(1)(7-n)>0
36-28+4n>0
4n>-8\Rightarrow n>-2

Finding a Model from Data

If a model has two unknown parameters, two known data points give two equations: a system. For h=a(t-p)^2+q with a known, subtracting the two equations eliminates q and leaves an equation in p only, because the p^2 terms cancel.

Example 8

A ball thrown from a roof

A ball is thrown upward from a roof. Its height in metres after t seconds is h=-5(t-p)^2+q. After 1 s the ball is 30 m above the ground, and after 4 s it is 45 m above the ground.

(a) Write a system of two equations in p and q, and solve it.
30=-5(1-p)^2+q   and   45=-5(4-p)^2+q
Subtract the first from the second:
15=-5(4-p)^2+5(1-p)^2
15=-5(16-8p+p^2)+5(1-2p+p^2)
15=-80+40p+5-10p=30p-75
p=3;   q=30+5(1-3)^2=50.
(b) How high is the roof, what is the maximum height, and when does the ball hit the ground (to the nearest hundredth of a second)?
h=-5(t-3)^2+50
Roof: t=0, h=-5(9)+50=5 m.
Maximum: 50 m, after 3 s (the vertex).
Ground: -5(t-3)^2+50=0\Rightarrow(t-3)^2=10
t=3\pm\sqrt{10}; t=3+\sqrt{10}\approx6.16 s (3-\sqrt{10}<0 is rejected).
✓

Key ideas

Five steps. (1) Define the variables, with units. (2) One equation per condition. (3) Solve: substitution, elimination or intersect. (4) Check in the original conditions; reject what makes no sense. (5) Answer in a sentence, with units.
Linear–quadratic. A line (a slope, a route, a constant cost) and a parabola or a circle x^2+y^2=r^2. Substitute the line into the curve; the solutions are where they meet. A vertical gap between two graphs is their difference.
Quadratic–quadratic. Set the two expressions equal. Equal squared terms cancel. Break-even: R=C; profit between the break-even points; the greatest profit at the vertex of P=R-C.
The discriminant of the combined equation counts the solutions: >0 two, =0 one (tangent), <0 none. With an unknown constant, write b^2-4ac in terms of it. Two data points give two equations for two parameters.
