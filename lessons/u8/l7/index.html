The Fake Coin

I have 9 coins that look exactly the same. One of them is a fake, and it is a little heavier than the others. All I have is a balance scale: no weights, it just tips toward the heavier side.

What is the fewest number of weighings that is guaranteed to find the fake coin?

?
Guess first. Then plan your weighings with your neighbour.
Two weighings. Split the coins into three piles of 3 and weigh one pile against another. If one side drops, the fake is in that pile; if they balance, it is in the pile you left off. Now take the 3 suspects and weigh one coin against one coin: the heavier side is the fake, and if they balance, it is the coin you kept in your hand.
Most people guess 3 or 4 because they split the coins in half. But a balance has three outcomes (left, right, balance), so two weighings can tell apart 3 × 3 = 9 coins. Three weighings would handle 27 coins.

The Sign of a Product

Once one side is 0 and the other side is factored, only the signs of the factors matter.

Signs of a product
AB>0 when A and B have the same sign (both positive or both negative)
AB<0 when A and B have opposite signs
AB=0 when A=0 or B=0

Case analysis turns this into pairs of linear inequalities. Within a case both inequalities must hold (“and”: keep the overlap); the solution is the result of Case 1 or Case 2. A case can have no solution. Solutions are written in set notation, for example \{x\mid x<-2\text{ or }x>3,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}, and can be shown on a number line (closed dot \bullet for \le or \ge, open dot \circ for < or >).

Example 1

Case analysis

Solve by case analysis. Show each solution on a number line.

(a) (x-3)(x+2)>0
The product is positive: same signs.
Case 1: x-3>0 and x+2>0: x>3 and x>-2, so x>3
Case 2: x-3<0 and x+2<0: x<3 and x<-2, so x<-2
\{x\mid x<-2\text{ or }x>3,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
(b) x^2+4x-21\le0
(x+7)(x-3)\le0: opposite signs (or 0).
Case 1: x+7\ge0 and x-3\le0: x\ge-7 and x\le3, so -7\le x\le3
Case 2: x+7\le0 and x-3\ge0: x\le-7 and x\ge3: no number does both.
\{x\mid -7\le x\le3,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
2

Sign Charts

Case analysis gets long. A sign chart does the same reasoning in a table. A linear factor such as x-5 is 0 at its zero (x=5), negative to the left of it and positive to the right (when the coefficient of x is positive). The zeros of the factors split the number line into intervals, and in each interval every factor keeps one sign.

Solving with a sign chart
  1. Move every term to one side so the other side is 0.
  2. Factor fully.
  3. Find the zero of each factor and write the zeros in order across the top of the chart, with an interval between and around them.
  4. Fill in a row for each factor: -, 0 or +.
  5. Product row: an even number of negative factors gives +, an odd number gives -.
  6. Read the intervals whose sign matches the inequality (>0: +; <0: -). Include the zeros for \le or \ge; leave them out for < or >.

Complete the sign chart

(a) Complete the sign chart and solve x^2-3x-10>0.
xx<-2-2-2<x<55x>5

(b) Use the same chart to solve x^2-3x-10\le0.

Squared Factors and Quadratics That Do Not Factor

A squared factor such as (x-5)^2 is never negative: it is 0 at x=5 and positive everywhere else, so it does not change sign. If ax^2+bx+c has no real zeros (discriminant b^2-4ac<0), its graph never crosses the x-axis, so it has one sign for every x: test any one value. If it has real zeros but does not factor, find the zeros with the quadratic formula and use them as the boundaries of the intervals.

Example 5

Squares, no zeros, and the formula

Solve.

(a) x^2-10x+25>0
(x-5)^2>0
True for every x except x=5 (where it is 0).
\{x\mid x\ne5,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
(b) 4x^2+12x+9\le0
(2x+3)^2\le0
A square is never negative, so only (2x+3)^2=0 works.
x=-\frac32 is the only solution: \{x\mid x=-\tfrac{3}{2}\}.
(c) x^2-4x+7<0
b^2-4ac=16-28=-12<0: no real zeros, so the sign never changes.
Test x=0: 7>0, so x^2-4x+7 is always positive.
It is never <0: no solution.
(d) x^2-2x-4\ge0 (exact, then to the nearest hundredth)
Zeros: x=\dfrac{2\pm\sqrt{4+16}}{2}=\dfrac{2\pm2\sqrt5}{2}=1\pm\sqrt5
x^2-2x-4=(x-(1-\sqrt5))(x-(1+\sqrt5)): positive outside the zeros (a>0).
\{x\mid x\le1-\sqrt5\text{ or }x\ge1+\sqrt5,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
x\le-1.24 or x\ge3.24
Zeros to the nearest hundredth. Graph y=x^2-2x-4 and use 2ndCALC2 (zero) once for each zero: give a left bound, a right bound and a guess.

Writing an Inequality from Its Solution

Work backwards. Each endpoint r gives a factor (x-r); an endpoint \frac pq gives (qx-p). With a positive leading coefficient, the quadratic is negative between its zeros and positive outside them.

Key idea
Solution between r_1 and r_2: (x-r_1)(x-r_2)<0 (or \le0)
Solution outside (x<r_1 or x>r_2): (x-r_1)(x-r_2)>0 (or \ge0)
Closed dots (endpoints included) → \le or \ge; open dots → < or >.
Example 7

Write the inequality

Write a quadratic inequality, in the form ax^2+bx+c<0 (or >, \le, \ge) with integer coefficients and a>0, that has the given solution.

(a) \{x\mid -4<x<3,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
Between, open: (x+4)(x-3)<0
x^2+x-12<0
(b) \{x\mid x\le-6\text{ or }x\ge-1,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
Outside, closed: (x+6)(x+1)\ge0
x^2+7x+6\ge0
(c) \{x\mid -\frac12\le x\le3,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
x=-\frac12 gives (2x+1): (2x+1)(x-3)\le0
2x^2-5x-3\le0
(d) The solution shown on the number line.
x<0 or x>7 (outside, open)
x(x-7)>0, so x^2-7x>0

Multiplying by -1 and reversing the sign gives another correct inequality: (a) is also -x^2-x+12>0.

5

Problems

Write the inequality from the words (“at least” → \ge, “more than” → >, “at most” → \le), move every term to one side, solve, and answer in context. A time or a price cannot be negative.

Example 8

The model rocket

A model rocket is launched from the ground. Its height after t seconds is h=40t-5t^2 metres.

(a) When is the rocket at least 60 m high?
40t-5t^2\ge60
-5t^2+40t-60\ge0
Divide by -5 (reverse): t^2-8t+12\le0
(t-2)(t-6)\le0
2\le t\le6: from 2 s to 6 s after launch.
(b) For how many seconds is it at least 60 m high?
6-2=4 seconds
(c) For what times is the rocket in the air?
40t-5t^2>0
5t(8-t)>0
Both factors positive for 0<t<8 (for t>8 the height would be negative).
In the air for 0<t<8.
(d) Show that the rocket never reaches 90 m.
40t-5t^2\ge90\Rightarrow t^2-8t+18\le0
b^2-4ac=64-72=-8<0: no zeros, and t=0 gives 18>0, so t^2-8t+18 is always positive. No solution: it never reaches 90 m (its maximum is 80 m at t=4).
Example 9

Ticket prices and profit

A theatre group estimates that if a ticket costs x dollars, its profit for one show is P=-20x^2+1000x-8000 dollars.

(a) For what ticket prices does the group make a profit?
-20x^2+1000x-8000>0
Divide by -20 (reverse): x^2-50x+400<0
(x-10)(x-40)<0
10<x<40: any price between $10 and $40.
(b) For what prices is the profit at least \$3000? Give the prices to the nearest cent.
-20x^2+1000x-11\,000\ge0\Rightarrow x^2-50x+550\le0
Zeros: x=\dfrac{50\pm\sqrt{2500-2200}}{2}=25\pm5\sqrt3
25-5\sqrt3\le x\le25+5\sqrt3
From $16.34 to $33.66.
Example 10

A value of k

For what values of k does x^2+(k-2)x+4=0 have no real roots?
No real roots: b^2-4ac<0.
(k-2)^2-4(1)(4)<0\Rightarrow k^2-4k+4-16<0\Rightarrow k^2-4k-12<0
(k-6)(k+2)<0: \{k\mid -2<k<6,\ k\in\mathbb{R}\}
Check k=0: x^2-2x+4=0 has b^2-4ac=4-16<0 ✓
✓

Key ideas

Signs of a product. Same signs: +; opposite signs: -. Case analysis: both inequalities in a case must hold (keep the overlap); the solution is Case 1 or Case 2.
Sign chart. One side 0, factor, the zeros across the top, a row per factor, then the product (an even number of - gives +). Zeros in for \le, \ge. Negative a: a -1 row, or \times(-1) and reverse.
Squares and no zeros. (x-r)^2 is 0 at r and positive elsewhere: no sign change. b^2-4ac<0: one sign everywhere, test one value. Doesn't factor: zeros from the formula.
Backwards and problems. Between: (x-r_1)(x-r_2)<0; outside: >0; closed dots \le, \ge. Words: at least \ge, more than >, at most \le; a time or price can't be negative.
