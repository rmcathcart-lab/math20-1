What Comes Next?

A famous sequence starts like this. Each row is made from the row just above it.

1 → 11 → 21 → 1211 → 111221 → ?

What is the next row?

?
Write down your guess first. What rule turns each row into the next one?
312211. Read each row out loud, then write down what you said:
1 is “one 1” → 11 · 11 is “two 1s” → 21 · 21 is “one 2, one 1” → 1211 · 1211 is “one 1, one 2, two 1s” → 111221 · 111221 is “three 1s, two 2s, one 1” → 312211.
It’s called the look-and-say sequence. Twist: no digit bigger than 3 ever shows up, however far you go, and each row is about 30% longer than the one before.

Unit 8 Review

Systems of equations, inequalities in two variables and quadratic inequalities in one variable on one page, then mixed examples that combine them. One question runs the whole unit: what does the solution look like? A system’s solutions are points where two graphs meet, an inequality in two variables has a shaded region of points, and an inequality in one variable has a set of x-values on a number line.

I can…
  • solve linear–quadratic and quadratic–quadratic systems algebraically (and with intersect), and verify in both equations
  • use the discriminant to tell how many solutions a system has, and find k for a tangent line
  • graph linear and quadratic inequalities in two variables with a solid or dashed boundary and a test point, and write one from its graph
  • solve quadratic inequalities in one variable with roots and test points, a sign chart or a graph, in set notation
  • solve problems with systems and inequalities, with restrictions, and answer in context

Number of solutions of a system. After eliminating y, the quadratic equation’s discriminant tells how many times the graphs meet: b^2-4ac\gt0: two solutions; =0: one (a line meeting a parabola this way is tangent to it); \lt0: none. Two parabolas can also be the same parabola: infinitely many solutions.
Solving and checking. A solution of a system is a point (x,\ y) on both graphs: check it in both equations. A solution of an inequality in two variables is a region of points; a solution of an inequality in one variable is a set of x-values on a number line. In a word problem, define the variables, reject values that make no sense (negative times or lengths, fractions of items) and answer in context.
Quadratic inequalities in one variable. a\gt0: ax^2+bx+c\lt0 between the roots, \gt0 outside them. Include the roots for \le,\ \ge. Multiply by a negative number \Rightarrow reverse the sign. No real roots (b^2-4ac\lt0): the expression has one sign everywhere (test one value): all real numbers or no solution.

Systems of Equations

Eliminate y, solve, find y, verify. Set the two expressions for y equal, collect everything on one side and solve the quadratic (factor or the quadratic formula). Each x gives a point: find its y and check it in both equations.
Example 1

A line and a parabola

(a) Solve the system y=x^2-4x+1 and y=2x-4 algebraically, and verify.
x^2-4x+1=2x-4\Rightarrow x^2-6x+5=0\Rightarrow(x-1)(x-5)=0
x=1: y=2(1)-4=-2;  x=5: y=2(5)-4=6.
Solutions \boldsymbol{(1,\ -2)} and \boldsymbol{(5,\ 6)}.
Verify in the parabola: 1-4+1=-2 ✓; 25-20+1=6 ✓
(b) Determine k so that the line y=2x+k is tangent to y=x^2-4x+1, and find the point of tangency.
x^2-4x+1=2x+k\Rightarrow x^2-6x+(1-k)=0. Tangent: one solution, so b^2-4ac=0.
36-4(1-k)=0\Rightarrow32+4k=0\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{k=-8}.
x^2-6x+9=0\Rightarrow x=3, y=2(3)-8=-2. Point of tangency \boldsymbol{(3,\ -2)}.
Check (a) with intersect. Enter both sides in Y=, set a window that shows both points, then 2ndCALC5 (intersect): ENTER for the first curve, ENTER for the second, then type a guess near the point you want and ENTER.
Example 2

Two parabolas

(a) Solve y=2x^2+x-3 and y=x^2+3x+5.
2x^2+x-3=x^2+3x+5
x^2-2x-8=0\Rightarrow(x-4)(x+2)=0
x=4: y=16+12+5=33;  x=-2: y=4-6+5=3
\boldsymbol{(-2,\ 3)} and \boldsymbol{(4,\ 33)}. Check in the first: 32+4-3=33 ✓, 8-2-3=3 ✓
(b) How many solutions does the system y=x^2+2, y=-x^2+2x have? Explain.
x^2+2=-x^2+2x\Rightarrow2x^2-2x+2=0
b^2-4ac=4-16=-12\lt0
No real roots: the parabolas do not meet. No solution.
Example 3

Break-even and profit

A company’s revenue and cost for making and selling x hundred items are R=-2x^2+60x and C=10x+200, both in hundreds of dollars.

(a) Determine the break-even points (where R=C).
-2x^2+60x=10x+200
-2x^2+50x-200=0\Rightarrow x^2-25x+100=0
(x-5)(x-20)=0
x=5: C=250;  x=20: C=400.
Break even at 500 items ($25 000) and 2000 items ($40 000).
(b) For what production levels does the company make a profit?
Profit: R\gt C,  -2x^2+50x-200\gt0
x^2-25x+100\lt0 (dividing by -2 reverses the sign), (x-5)(x-20)\lt0
\boldsymbol{5\lt x\lt20}: between 500 and 2000 items.

How many meeting points? the discriminant counts them

Once y is eliminated, every system in this unit becomes one quadratic equation, and its discriminant b^2-4ac says how many points the graphs share: two, one (tangent) or none.

Inequalities in Two Variables

1Graph the boundary (replace the sign with =): solid for \le,\ \ge, dashed for \lt,\ \gt.
2Substitute a test point that is not on the boundary ((0,\ 0) if possible).
3True: shade the side with the test point. False: shade the other side.

Solved for y: y\gt\dots is above the boundary and y\lt\dots is below. Dividing by a negative while isolating y reverses the sign.

Example 4

A boundary line

(a) Graph 3x-2y\lt6.
-2y\lt-3x+6\Rightarrow y\gt\tfrac{3}{2}x-3 (sign reversed).
Boundary y=\tfrac{3}{2}x-3: intercepts (2,\ 0), (0,\ -3); dashed (\lt).
Test (0,\ 0): 0-0\lt6 true. Shade the side containing the origin (above the line).
(b) The graph shows the solution region of a linear inequality. The boundary passes through (0,\ 2) and (4,\ 0). Write the inequality.
Slope =\dfrac{0-2}{4-0}=-\dfrac{1}{2}, y-intercept 2: y=-\tfrac{1}{2}x+2.
Solid line, region below: \boldsymbol{y\le-\tfrac{1}{2}x+2} (or x+2y\le4). Check (0,\ 0): 0\le2 ✓
Example 5

A boundary parabola

(a) Graph y\ge x^2+2x-3.
x^2+2x-3=(x+3)(x-1): x-intercepts -3, 1; y-intercept -3; axis x=-1, vertex (-1,\ -4).
Solid (\ge). Test (0,\ 0): 0\ge-3 true.
Shade above (inside) the parabola.
(b) Is (-2,\ 1) a solution? Is (1,\ -1)?
(-2,\ 1): 4-4-3=-3; 1\ge-3: yes.
(1,\ -1): 1+2-3=0; -1\ge0 is false: no.
Example 6

The school play

A school play needs ticket sales of at least \$480. Adult tickets cost \$12 and student tickets cost \$8. Let x be the number of adult tickets and y the number of student tickets.

(a) Write an inequality, with any restrictions, and graph it.
12x+8y\ge480, x\ge0, y\ge0, whole numbers.
Boundary intercepts (40,\ 0) and (0,\ 60), solid. Test (0,\ 0): 0\ge480 false: shade the side away from the origin (in the first quadrant).
(b) Do 20 adult and 25 student tickets reach the goal? Do 24 and 24?
12(20)+8(25)=440\lt480: no.
12(24)+8(24)=480\ge480: yes (on the boundary).
Solid line from (0,\ 60) to (40,\ 0), shaded away from the origin, first quadrant only.

Quadratic Inequalities in One Variable

One side 0, factor, roots on a number line. The roots split the number line into intervals. Find the sign in each one with a test value, a sign chart, or the graph of y=ax^2+bx+c (above the x-axis: positive; below: negative). Write the answer in set notation.

Sign chart and graph

(b) Use the graph in Example 5 to solve x^2+2x-3\lt0. Explain.
x^2+2x-3\lt0 asks where the graph of y=x^2+2x-3 is below the x-axis (y\lt0).
That is between the x-intercepts -3 and 1, not including them.

Signs from the chart, signs from the graph

The sign chart and the graph tell the same story: the product is positive exactly where the parabola is above the x-axis.

Key ideas

Systems. Eliminate y, solve the quadratic, find each y (from the linear equation), verify in both. Graphically: intersect.
How many? The discriminant of the combined equation: \gt0 two, =0 one (tangent), \lt0 none. For a tangent, set b^2-4ac=0 and solve for k.
Two variables. Boundary solid for \le,\ \ge, dashed for \lt,\ \gt; a test point off the boundary picks the side. The solution is a region.
One variable. One side 0, factor, roots, then test values, a sign chart or the graph. a\gt0: \lt0 between the roots, \gt0 outside. Set notation.
