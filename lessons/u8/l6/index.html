The Painted Cube

A big wooden cube is painted red on all six of its outside faces. When the paint is dry, the cube is sawed into 27 equal small cubes (3 by 3 by 3).

How many of the small cubes have red paint on exactly two faces?

?
Make a guess first. Then count.
12. A small cube has paint on exactly two faces when it sits in the middle of an edge of the big cube, and a cube has 12 edges.
The other 15 cubes: the 8 corner cubes have paint on three faces, the 6 cubes in the centres of the faces have paint on one, and 1 cube, hidden in the very middle, has no paint at all. Check: 8+12+6+1=27.

Quadratic Inequalities and Their Graphs

A quadratic inequality in one variable has one of the forms ax^2+bx+c<0, ax^2+bx+c\le0, ax^2+bx+c>0 or ax^2+bx+c\ge0, with a\ne0. Its solution is the set of all x-values that make it true, usually an interval or two. The graph of the related function y=ax^2+bx+c shows them: ax^2+bx+c>0 exactly where the graph is above the x-axis, and ax^2+bx+c<0 where it is below. The x-intercepts (the roots of ax^2+bx+c=0) are where it changes from one to the other.

From the graph of y=ax^2+bx+c
> 0the x-values where the graph is above the x-axis
< 0the x-values where the graph is below the x-axis
≥ or ≤also include the x-intercepts
Writing the solution
Between the roots\{x\mid -3<x<4,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
Outside the roots\{x\mid x\le-3\ \text{or}\ x\ge4,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
On a number line, an open dot means the endpoint is not included (<, >) and a closed dot means it is (\le, \ge).

Two separate pieces are joined by “or” (an x cannot be in both). Never write 4<x<-3: it says x is bigger than 4 and less than -3 at once.

Example 1

Read the solution from the graph

The graph of y=x^2-x-12 is shown. Use it to solve each equation or inequality. Show (b) and (c) on a number line.

(a) x^2-x-12=0
The x-intercepts: x=-3 or x=4.
(b) x^2-x-12<0
The graph is below the x-axis between the intercepts: \{x\mid -3<x<4,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
(c) x^2-x-12\ge0
On or above the axis, outside the intercepts: \{x\mid x\le-3\ \text{or}\ x\ge4,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
Example 2

A parabola that opens down, and a line

The graph of y=-x^2+2x+8 is shown. Use it to solve each inequality.

(a) -x^2+2x+8>0
This parabola opens down, so it is above the axis between its intercepts: \{x\mid -2<x<4,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
(b) -x^2+2x+8\le0
\{x\mid x\le-2\ \text{or}\ x\ge4,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
(c) -x^2+2x+8>5  (Draw the line y=5.)
The line y=5 meets the graph at x=-1 and x=3 (check: -1-2+8=5, -9+6+8=5). The graph is above the line between them: \{x\mid -1<x<3,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}

Solving with a Graphing Calculator

When the roots are not whole numbers, a graphing calculator reads them quickly.

1Move every term to one side: ax^2+bx+c\ \square\ 0.
2Graph Y_1=ax^2+bx+c.
3Use 2ndCALC zero to find each x-intercept.
4Read where the graph is above (>0) or below (<0) the x-axis.
Or, without rearranging: graph Y_1= left side and Y_2= right side, use intersect, and read where Y_1 is above or below Y_2.

The quadratic formula gives the same roots exactly: use it when the question asks for exact values.

Example 3

Calculator first, then exact

Solve each inequality with a graphing calculator, to the nearest tenth. Then give the exact solution.

(a) x^2-4x-3>0
Y_1=X^2-4X-3; zeros x\approx-0.6 and x\approx4.6.
The parabola opens up: above the axis outside the zeros.
\{x\mid x<-0.6\ \text{or}\ x>4.6,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\} (approx.)
Exact: x=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{16+12}}{2}=2\pm\sqrt7, so
\{x\mid x<2-\sqrt7\ \text{or}\ x>2+\sqrt7,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
(b) 2x^2\le5x+4
2x^2-5x-4\le0; Y_1=2X^2-5X-4.
Zeros x\approx-0.6 and x\approx3.1; on or below the axis between them:
\{x\mid -0.6\le x\le3.1,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\} (approx.)
Exact: x=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{25+32}}{4}=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{57}}{4}, so
\left\{x\;\middle|\;\frac{5-\sqrt{57}}{4}\le x\le\frac{5+\sqrt{57}}{4},\ x\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
On your own calculator. Put the expression in Y=, press ZOOM6 (ZStandard), then 2ndTRACE 2:zero: a left bound, a right bound and a guess on either side of each x-intercept. For (b) without rearranging: Y_1=2X^2, Y_2=5X+4 and 5:intersect.
3

Roots and Test Points

A graph is not needed. The graph of y=ax^2+bx+c can only cross the x-axis at a root, so the expression ax^2+bx+c can only change sign at a root. The roots split the number line into intervals, and on each interval the expression keeps one sign. One test point in each interval tells you that sign.

Roots and test points
1Write the inequality with 0 on one side.
2Solve the related equation ax^2+bx+c=0 (factor, or use the quadratic formula). These roots are the boundary points.
3Mark the roots on a number line; they make two or three intervals.
4Substitute one test point from each interval into the inequality.
5The solution is every interval whose test point makes it true; include the roots for \le or \ge, exclude them for < or >.

Example 5

Rearrange, factor, test

Solve using roots and test points. Show each solution on a number line.

(a) 2x^2+5x\le3
2x^2+5x-3\le0
(2x-1)(x+3)=0: roots -3 and \frac12.
Test x=-4: 32-20-3=9; 9\le0? no.
Test x=0: -3\le0? yes.
Test x=1: 2+5-3=4\le0? no.
\left\{x\;\middle|\;-3\le x\le\frac12,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
(b) 15+2x-x^2<0
15+2x-x^2=0\Rightarrow x^2-2x-15=0
(x-5)(x+3)=0: roots -3 and 5.
Test x=-4: 15-8-16=-9<0? yes.
Test x=0: 15<0? no.
Test x=6: 15+12-36=-9<0? yes.
\{x\mid x<-3\ \text{or}\ x>5,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}

In (b) you could first multiply by -1 to get x^2-2x-15>0 (the sign reverses); the test points still check the original inequality, which is the safest habit.

Example 6

Expand or rearrange first

Expand or rearrange first, then solve.

(a) x(x+4)\ge5
x^2+4x-5\ge0
(x+5)(x-1)=0: roots -5 and 1.
Test -6: -6(-2)=12\ge5 yes;  0: 0\ge5 no;  2: 2(6)=12\ge5 yes.
\{x\mid x\le-5\ \text{or}\ x\ge1,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
(b) (x-2)^2<3x-2
x^2-4x+4-3x+2<0\Rightarrow x^2-7x+6<0
(x-1)(x-6)=0: roots 1 and 6.
Test 0: 4<-2 no;  2: 0<4 yes;  7: 25<19 no.
\{x\mid 1<x<6,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}

When There Are One or No Roots

If the related equation has a double root, or no real roots (discriminant b^2-4ac<0), the graph touches the x-axis once or never. Then the expression has the same sign everywhere (except 0 at a double root), and one test point decides everything. The solution can be all real numbers, one number, all real numbers but one, or no real numbers at all.

Example 7

All, one, all but one, or none

Solve each inequality.

(a) x^2-8x+16>0
(x-4)^2>0. A square is positive unless it is 0, which happens only at x=4.
\{x\mid x\ne4,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
(b) x^2-8x+16\le0
(x-4)^2\le0. A square is never negative, so it must equal 0: x=4 only, \{x\mid x=4\}.
(c) x^2+2x+5<0
b^2-4ac=4-20=-16<0: no roots, so one sign everywhere. Test x=0: 5<0 false.
No solution.
(d) x^2+2x+5>0
Same expression; test x=0: 5>0 true, so it is true for every x.
\{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\} (all real numbers)
5

Applications

Quadratic inequalities answer “for which values” questions: which values of a parameter give two, one or no roots (the discriminant from Unit 5), and which measurements keep a quantity in a range. In a context, also apply the restrictions of the situation (a length is positive, a count is a whole number).

Example 8

For which values?

(a) For what values of k does x^2+kx+9=0 have two distinct real roots? For what values does it have no real roots?
Two distinct real roots: b^2-4ac>0\Rightarrow k^2-36>0.
Roots of k^2-36=0: k=\pm6. Test k=0: -36>0 false; test k=\pm7: 13>0 true.
Two distinct real roots: \{k\mid k<-6\ \text{or}\ k>6,\ k\in\mathbb{R}\}.
No real roots: k^2-36<0: \{k\mid -6<k<6,\ k\in\mathbb{R}\}.  (At k=\pm6 there is one double root.)
(b) The length of a rectangle is 4 cm more than its width. For what widths is the area at most 21\text{ cm}^2?
Width w, length w+4:  w(w+4)\le21\Rightarrow w^2+4w-21\le0.
(w+7)(w-3)=0: roots -7 and 3. Test w=0: -21\le0 true, so -7\le w\le3.
A width is positive: 0<w\le3. The width is more than 0 cm and at most 3 cm.
✓

Key ideas

From the graph. >0: where the parabola is above the x-axis; <0: below. For \ge or \le, include the x-intercepts. Against a line: above or below the line.
Writing it. Between: \{x\mid -3<x<4,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}. Outside: x\le-3 or x\ge4. Open dot: <,>; closed dot: \le,\ge.
Roots and test points. 0 on one side, find the roots, test one point in each interval, keep the true intervals. Calculator: zero or intersect; exact roots from the quadratic formula.
One or no roots. One sign everywhere: all real numbers, one value, all but one, or no solution. In problems, also apply the context (a width is positive).
