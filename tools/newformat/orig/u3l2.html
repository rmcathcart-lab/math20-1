Trigonometric Ratios from a Point on the Terminal Arm

Any point P(x,\,y) on the terminal arm of an angle tells us everything about the angle: how far P is from the origin, the exact values of \sin\theta, \cos\theta and \tan\theta, and the angle \theta itself.

I can…
  • find the distance r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2} from the origin to a point on the terminal arm, as an exact radical
  • find the exact values of \sin\theta=\dfrac{y}{r}, \cos\theta=\dfrac{x}{r} and \tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{x} for a point in any quadrant, with the right signs
  • state the ratios of the quadrantal angles 0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ, 270^\circ, 360^\circ, and say when \tan\theta is undefined
  • write a ratio of \theta as the same ratio of its reference angle, with the sign the quadrant gives it
  • find \theta from a point: \theta_R=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{|y|}{|x|}\right), then place \theta in its quadrant
T2 Solve problems, using the three primary trigonometric ratios for angles from 0° to 360° in standard position.
Today: r, \sin\theta, \cos\theta and \tan\theta from a point on the terminal arm, and the angle \theta from a point.
Warm-up

The Tower Crane

A site map, seen from above: the crane’s tower is at the origin, east is +x, north is +y (metres). The trolley rolls along the jib and the jib swings around the tower.

1
The trolley is at (6, 8): 6 m east and 8 m north of the tower. How far is it from the tower?
10 m. East and north make a right angle: \sqrt{6^2+8^2}=\sqrt{100}=10.
2
The jib swings until the trolley is at (−8, 6). Is it closer to the tower now?
No, still 10 m. \sqrt{(-8)^2+6^2}=10. West is negative, but a distance never is: squaring takes the sign away.
!
The big one. Back at (6, 8), the trolley rolls out along the jib until it is 25 m from the tower. How far east of the tower is it now? Guess first.
A 21 m
B 15 m
C 20 m
D 25 m
B, 15 m. On this jib, east is always \tfrac{6}{10}=\tfrac{3}{5} of the distance out: \tfrac{3}{5}\times25=15 (and north is \tfrac{4}{5}\times25=20: that’s C).
A adds the 15 m it rolled to the 6 m east. But the trolley rolls along the slanted jib, not east. The fraction stays the same along the whole jib: today that fraction is \cos\theta.

Challenge your neighbour: where is the trolley when it is 35 m out along the same jib?

1

The Distance r from the Origin

Let P(x,\,y) be any point on the terminal arm of \theta, other than the origin. Dropping a perpendicular from P to the x-axis makes a right triangle with legs of length \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{|x|} and \textcolor{#c93a33}{|y|}. Its hypotenuse is the distance \textcolor{#a33357}{r} from the origin to P.

x^2+y^2=r^2,\qquad\text{so}\qquad r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}
(r is a distance: always r>0)

The triangle is the reference triangle: its angle at the origin is the reference angle \textcolor{#c27a0e}{\theta_R}. Leave r as an exact radical in simplest form when it is not a whole number.

Example 1

Find the distance r

Sketch \theta and determine the exact distance r from the origin to the point on its terminal arm.

(a) P(-20,\,21)
r=\sqrt{(-20)^2+21^2}
=\sqrt{400+441}=\sqrt{841}=\boldsymbol{29}
(b) Q(4,\,-6)
r=\sqrt{4^2+(-6)^2}=\sqrt{52}
=\sqrt{4\cdot13}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{13}}
(c) R(-3,\,-3)
r=\sqrt{(-3)^2+(-3)^2}=\sqrt{18}
=\boldsymbol{3\sqrt{2}}
2

The Ratios in Terms of x, y and r

In Math 10C, \sin\theta=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \cos\theta=\dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} and \tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} (SOH CAH TOA) for an acute angle in a right triangle. For an angle in standard position, the ratios are defined with the coordinates of P(x,\,y):

\sin\theta=\dfrac{\textcolor{#c93a33}{y}}{\textcolor{#a33357}{r}}\cos\theta=\dfrac{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{x}}{\textcolor{#a33357}{r}}\tan\theta=\dfrac{\textcolor{#c93a33}{y}}{\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{x}}\ \ (x\ne0)

In quadrant 1 these are exactly SOH CAH TOA. In other quadrants x or y is negative, so a ratio can be negative. Since r>0, the sign of \sin\theta is the sign of y, and the sign of \cos\theta is the sign of x. Write exact answers with a rational denominator (Unit 2).

Example 2

A point in quadrant 1

The point (7,\,24) lies on the terminal arm of \theta. Determine r and the exact values of \sin\theta, \cos\theta and \tan\theta.

r=\sqrt{7^2+24^2}=\sqrt{49+576}=\sqrt{625}=\boldsymbol{25}
\sin\theta=\dfrac{y}{r}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{24}{25}}\qquad\cos\theta=\dfrac{x}{r}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{7}{25}}\qquad\tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{x}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{24}{7}}
Example 3

Points in the other quadrants

Each point lies on the terminal arm of \theta. Sketch \theta, then determine the exact values of \sin\theta, \cos\theta and \tan\theta.

(a) (-9,\,40)
r=\sqrt{81+1\,600}=\sqrt{1\,681}=41
\sin\theta=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{40}{41}}
\cos\theta=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{9}{41}}
\tan\theta=\dfrac{40}{-9}=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{40}{9}}
(b) (-5,\,-12)
r=\sqrt{25+144}=\sqrt{169}=13
\sin\theta=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{12}{13}}
\cos\theta=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{5}{13}}
\tan\theta=\dfrac{-12}{-5}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{12}{5}}
(c) (6,\,-3)
r=\sqrt{36+9}=\sqrt{45}=3\sqrt{5}
\sin\theta=\dfrac{-3}{3\sqrt{5}}=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{5}}
\cos\theta=\dfrac{6}{3\sqrt{5}}=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}}
\tan\theta=\dfrac{-3}{6}=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{1}{2}}
(d) In each part, which ratios are negative? What decides the sign?
(a) cos and tan; (b) sin and cos; (c) sin and tan. Since r>0, the sign of each ratio comes only from the signs of x and y: \sin\theta follows y, \cos\theta follows x, and \tan\theta is positive when x and y have the same sign.
3

Quadrantal Angles

For a quadrantal angle, the terminal arm lies on an axis, so one coordinate of P is 0. Using the point with r=1 on each axis makes the values easy to read. When x=0, \tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{0} is undefined (we cannot divide by zero).

(b) The point (0,\,-7) is on the terminal arm of \theta. State \theta and the values of \sin\theta, \cos\theta and \tan\theta.
The point is on the negative y-axis, so \boldsymbol{\theta=270^\circ} and r=7.
\sin\theta=\dfrac{-7}{7}=\boldsymbol{-1},   \cos\theta=\dfrac{0}{7}=\boldsymbol{0},   \tan\theta=\dfrac{-7}{0} is undefined. (The same as with (0,\,-1): any point on the arm gives the same ratios.)

4

Same Reference Angle, Same Ratios up to Sign

Reflecting P in the axes keeps the reference triangle the same size, so the ratios keep the same size; only the signs of x and y change. (And sliding P along its arm changes the size of the triangle but not its shape, so the ratios don’t change at all.)

The reference angle gives the size, the quadrant gives the sign

The value of \sin\theta, \cos\theta or \tan\theta is the value of the same ratio of \textcolor{#c27a0e}{\theta_R}, with a sign decided by the quadrant: positive or negative as y (for sine), x (for cosine) or \dfrac{y}{x} (for tangent) is in that quadrant.

5

Finding the Angle \theta from a Point

The inverse tangent key gives the reference angle when it is given the positive ratio of the legs. Set the calculator to Degree mode first (TI-84: MODE, choose DEGREE).

1 Plot P(x,\,y) and decide its quadrant. (If P is on an axis, \theta is 0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ or 270^\circ: no calculator needed.)
2 Find the reference angle: \textcolor{#c27a0e}{\theta_R}=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{|y|}{|x|}\right)   (2ndTAN⁻¹).
3 Place \theta in its quadrant:   Q1 \theta=\theta_R;   Q2 \theta=180^\circ-\theta_R;   Q3 \theta=180^\circ+\theta_R;   Q4 \theta=360^\circ-\theta_R.
!
Keep the signs out of \tan^{-1}. Entering \tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right) with the signs left in gives an angle from -90^\circ to 90^\circ, which is wrong outside quadrant 1. Using |x| and |y| and a sketch avoids this.

✓

Key ideas

The distance. r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}, always r>0. It is the hypotenuse of the reference triangle; leave it as an exact radical in simplest form.
The ratios. \sin\theta=\tfrac{y}{r}, \cos\theta=\tfrac{x}{r}, \tan\theta=\tfrac{y}{x}. Sine follows the sign of y, cosine the sign of x; tangent is positive when x and y have the same sign. Any point on the arm gives the same ratios.
Quadrantal angles. Use r=1: the point is (\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta). \tan 90^\circ and \tan 270^\circ are undefined (x=0). Same \theta_R: same ratios up to sign.
Finding θ. Sketch, \theta_R=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{|y|}{|x|}\right) in Degree mode, then Q1 \theta_R, Q2 180^\circ-\theta_R, Q3 180^\circ+\theta_R, Q4 360^\circ-\theta_R.
